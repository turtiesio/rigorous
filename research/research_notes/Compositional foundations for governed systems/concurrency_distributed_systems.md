# Concurrency theory and distributed systems as foundations for composing and controlling ~10^10 interacting actors

Provenance note for the report writer. Scale figures marked "(checked Nov 2026)" were retrieved by web search in this session, mostly as search-result excerpts rather than full-page reads. Classical theorems and their citations are stated from the researcher's knowledge of the primary literature; the DOI/URL given is the canonical location of the primary paper but was not re-fetched in this session, so exact wording and page-level details should be treated as paraphrase, not quotation. Where a figure could not be confirmed against a primary source it is listed under Gaps, not Findings.

## 1. Process calculi: which is most minimal, and what does compositionality buy?

### Takeaway
All mainstream process calculi share one tiny kernel: parallel composition, name scoping (restriction), and a single interaction rule; they differ in what may be communicated (nothing, names, processes, quoted processes) and whether locations/boundaries are primitive. The payoff of the algebraic approach is a behavioural equivalence that is a congruence, so a part can be replaced by an equivalent part inside any context without re-verifying the whole. That is the formal content of "a system of systems is again a system"; but no single calculus is canonical, and separation results show the choice of primitives (synchrony, mixed choice, locality) is not expressively neutral.

### Cited Findings
- CCS (Milner 1980): primitives are inaction, action prefix, sum, parallel composition, restriction, relabelling, recursion; communication is binary synchronisation on complementary labels producing a silent action tau. Strong bisimilarity is a congruence for all operators; weak bisimilarity is not preserved by sum, which motivates observation congruence. — [Milner, A Calculus of Communicating Systems, LNCS 92](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-10235-3)
- CSP (Hoare 1978; book 1985): processes synchronise on shared events (multiway synchronisation), with external and internal choice, hiding, and a denotational semantics (traces, stable failures, failures-divergences). Correctness is refinement: Spec is refined by Impl when Impl's behaviours are a subset of Spec's. All CSP operators are monotone with respect to refinement, which gives compositional stepwise development. — [Hoare, Communicating Sequential Processes, CACM 1978](https://doi.org/10.1145/359576.359585); [Hoare, CSP book (online edition)](http://www.usingcsp.com/cspbook.pdf)
- FDR is the refinement checker for CSP; FDR3 (2014) is a parallel implementation that checks traces, failures and failures-divergences refinement on multi-core machines and clusters. — [Gibson-Robinson, Armstrong, Boulgakov, Roscoe, FDR3, TACAS 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-54862-8_13)
- Pi-calculus (Milner, Parrow, Walker 1992): adds one idea to CCS, that channel names are themselves the values communicated, so the interconnection topology changes during computation (mobility as link passing). Scope extrusion lets a private name be exported, which models creation and controlled sharing of fresh references. — [Milner, Parrow, Walker, A Calculus of Mobile Processes I, Inf. & Comp. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0890-5401(92)90008-4)
- The pi-calculus encodes the lambda-calculus (lazy and call-by-value), so it is Turing-complete from name-passing alone. — [Milner, Functions as Processes, MSCS 1992](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0960129500001407)
- Join calculus (Fournet & Gonthier 1996): replaces channels-with-choice by join patterns, in which a reaction fires when messages are present on several channels at once, and the receiver of every channel is defined in exactly one place (locality). It was designed so that the calculus can be implemented in a distributed setting without distributed consensus on rendezvous; it is inter-encodable with the asynchronous pi-calculus. — [Fournet & Gonthier, The Reflexive CHAM and the Join-Calculus, POPL 1996](https://doi.org/10.1145/237721.237805)
- Ambient calculus (Cardelli & Gordon 1998/2000): the primitive is the ambient, a named, bounded place that contains processes and sub-ambients and that moves as a unit. Capabilities are `in n`, `out n`, `open n`; boundaries nest to arbitrary depth, and crossing a boundary requires holding the capability derived from the ambient's name. Pure mobility (no communication) is already Turing-complete. — [Cardelli & Gordon, Mobile Ambients, TCS 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(99)00231-5)
- A labelled bisimulation for mobile ambients that coincides with reduction barbed congruence was only obtained in 2005, showing that behavioural theory for nested mobile boundaries is substantially harder than for flat name passing. — [Merro & Zappa Nardelli, Behavioral Theory for Mobile Ambients, JACM 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1101821.1101825)
- Bigraphs (Milner 2001-2009): a bigraph is two independent structures on the same node set, a place graph (a forest: nesting/locality) and a link graph (a hypergraph: connectivity), with a signature of controls giving node arities. Dynamics are user-supplied reaction rules (bigraphical reactive systems). Bigraphs form a symmetric monoidal (pre)category whose arrows compose by plugging roots into sites and inner names into outer names, so composition is the basic operation rather than an afterthought. CCS, pi-calculus, ambients and Petri nets are recovered as instances. — [Milner, The Space and Motion of Communicating Agents, CUP 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511626661); [Jensen & Milner, Bigraphs and Mobile Processes (revised), UCAM-CL-TR-580](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/techreports/UCAM-CL-TR-580.pdf)
- Congruence for free: Leifer and Milner showed that if a reactive system has sufficient relative pushouts (RPOs), a labelled transition system can be derived from the reaction rules alone (labels are minimal contexts enabling a reaction), and bisimilarity on it is a congruence. Bigraphs were engineered to have RPOs. — [Leifer & Milner, Deriving Bisimulation Congruences for Reactive Systems, CONCUR 2000](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-44618-4_19)
- Tooling for bigraphs exists but is research-grade: BigraphER supports bigraphs with sharing, rule priorities and stochastic rules, with simulation and export to model checkers. — [Sevegnani & Calder, BigraphER, CAV 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-41540-6_27)
- Fusion calculus (Parrow & Victor 1998): simplifies pi by making input and output symmetric; an interaction fuses (identifies) names rather than substituting, with a single binding operator. It has fewer primitives than pi and contains it as a sub-calculus. — [Parrow & Victor, The Fusion Calculus, LICS 1998](https://doi.org/10.1109/LICS.1998.705654)
- Psi-calculi (Bengtson, Johansson, Parrow, Victor 2009/2011): a parametric framework in which terms, conditions and assertions are arbitrary nominal data types with an entailment relation; pi, applied pi, spi, fusion and concurrent-constraint variants are instances, and the meta-theory (bisimilarity is a congruence under stated conditions) is machine-checked in Isabelle/Nominal. — [Bengtson et al., Psi-calculi, LMCS 7(1:11) 2011](https://doi.org/10.2168/LMCS-7(1:11)2011)
- Rho-calculus (Meredith & Radestock 2005): removes the separate infinite set of names; names are quoted processes (`@P`) and can be dereferenced (`*x`), so the calculus is reflective and higher-order with no primitive `new`. Name equality becomes structural equivalence of quoted code. — [Meredith & Radestock, A Reflective Higher-order Calculus, ENTCS 141(5) 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcs.2005.05.016)
- Palamidessi's separation (1997/2003): there is no uniform (parallel-preserving, renaming-respecting), semantically reasonable encoding of the pi-calculus with mixed (input and output guarded) choice into the asynchronous pi-calculus or pi with separate choice. The proof uses leader election in symmetric networks, which is solvable with mixed choice but not without. This is a formal statement that synchronous multi-party choice is a real coordination primitive, not syntactic sugar. — [Palamidessi, Comparing the Expressive Power of the Synchronous and Asynchronous pi-calculi, MSCS 2003](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0960129503004043)
- Gorla (2008/2010) proposed a standard for what counts as a valid encoding between calculi: compositionality, name invariance, operational correspondence, divergence reflection and success sensitiveness. He showed that several known separation results can be re-derived under these criteria. — [Gorla, Towards a Unified Approach to Encodability and Separation Results for Process Calculi, Inf. & Comp. 208(9) 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ic.2010.05.002) (publication details checked Nov 2026)

### Inferences
- "Most minimal" depends on the measure. By operator count the asynchronous pi-calculus (output, input, parallel, restriction, replication) or the fusion calculus is smallest; the rho-calculus removes the name sort at the cost of making reflection primitive; bigraphs are the smallest framework that makes both nesting and linking first-class and independent, which is the structure the larger investigation is asking for (hierarchy of containment plus a cross-cutting graph of relationships).
- Congruence buys three things relevant to 10^10 actors: (a) substitutivity, so that a verified subsystem can be swapped without re-verification of the whole; (b) modular proof, in which properties are established at one level and reused at every nesting level; (c) abstraction, so that an entire organisation can be replaced by its observable interface. It buys nothing about resource cost, liveness under failure, or authority, which the other sections cover.
- Palamidessi's theorem and the join calculus's locality design both point the same way as the distributed-computing results in section 4: primitives that need symmetric simultaneous agreement between distributed parties are exactly the ones that cannot be implemented without coordination. A kernel for very large systems should be asynchronous with receiver-local choice.
- Process calculi model interaction, not obligation or authority. They have no native notion of who may do what, though the pi-calculus restriction operator gives the mathematical core of capability security (unforgeable names, connectivity by introduction; see section 5).

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of any process calculus used directly as the runtime foundation of a production system at billion-actor scale. Their influence is indirect (Erlang, Go channels, session types, BPMN semantics, model checkers).
- The practical status of rho-calculus-based platforms (RChain/Rholang and successors) through 2026 was not verified in this session.
- Bigraph tooling scalability limits (state-space sizes handled by BigraphER) were not retrieved.

## 2. Actor model, Erlang/OTP supervision, Akka, Orleans: axioms, recursive fault containment, reported scale

### Takeaway
The actor model is the calculus-family member with the strongest production record: three axioms (send, create, become), asynchronous messaging and no shared state. Erlang/OTP adds the one structure that makes it recursively manageable, the supervision tree, in which every worker has a supervisor responsible for its failure and failures escalate upward only when local restart fails. Deployments reach 10^6 concurrent actors per node and 10^8 to 10^9 users per service, with small operating teams.

### Cited Findings
- Actor model origin: Hewitt, Bishop and Steiger (1973) proposed actors as a universal primitive of computation. — [Hewitt, Bishop, Steiger, A Universal Modular ACTOR Formalism for Artificial Intelligence, IJCAI 1973](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/73/Papers/027B.pdf)
- Axioms: in response to a message an actor may (1) send a finite number of messages to actors whose addresses it has, (2) create a finite number of new actors, (3) designate the behaviour to be used for the next message it receives. Delivery is asynchronous and unordered, and every sent message is eventually delivered (fairness). — [Agha, Actors: A Model of Concurrent Computation in Distributed Systems, MIT AI-TR-844, 1985](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/6952)
- Laws (Hewitt & Baker 1977): events are partially ordered (no global clock), with only finitely many events between any two; an actor can only learn an address by being created with it, creating the addressee, or receiving it in a message (locality laws). — [Hewitt & Baker, Laws for Communicating Parallel Processes, 1977](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/41962)
- Clinger gave a denotational semantics based on event diagrams and showed that the guaranteed-delivery axiom yields unbounded nondeterminism, which distinguishes actors from CSP-style models of the time. — [Clinger, Foundations of Actor Semantics, MIT AI-TR-633, 1981](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/6935)
- Erlang's design position (Armstrong 2003): fault tolerance requires isolation, so processes share nothing, communicate only by asynchronous message passing, and failure is detected by another process (links/monitors), which then handles it. "Let it crash" means a process should not attempt defensive recovery from unexpected errors; it fails and a supervisor restores a known-good state. The thesis reports the Ericsson AXD301 switch (over a million lines of Erlang) with a claimed reliability of nine nines. — [Armstrong, Making Reliable Distributed Systems in the Presence of Software Errors, PhD thesis, 2003](https://erlang.org/download/armstrong_thesis_2003.pdf)
- OTP supervisors are processes whose only job is to start, monitor and restart children, which may be workers or other supervisors; restart strategies are `one_for_one`, `one_for_all` and `rest_for_one`, and a maximum restart intensity causes the supervisor itself to terminate and escalate to its own supervisor. — [Erlang/OTP Design Principles: Supervisor Behaviour](https://www.erlang.org/doc/system/sup_princ.html)
- WhatsApp reported more than 2 million simultaneous TCP connections on a single Erlang/FreeBSD server in January 2012. — [WhatsApp blog, 1 million is so 2011](https://blog.whatsapp.com/1-million-is-so-2011)
- A 2014 account of Rick Reed's talk reports roughly 465 million monthly users, about 11,000 cores, and more than 70 million Erlang messages per second, with production servers by then run at about 1 million connections each because each was doing more work. — [High Scalability, How WhatsApp Grew to Nearly 500 Million Users, 11,000 cores, and 70 Million Messages a Second, 2014](https://highscalability.com/how-whatsapp-grew-to-nearly-500-million-users-11000-cores-an/) (checked Nov 2026, search excerpt)
- Wired reported in September 2015 that WhatsApp served 900 million users with about 50 engineers. Secondary sources conflict on team size at earlier dates (32 engineers at about 450 million users in 2014 is also widely quoted). — [Wired, Why WhatsApp Only Needs 50 Engineers for Its 900M Users, 2015](https://www.wired.com/2015/09/whatsapp-serves-900-million-users-50-engineers/)
- Discord reported in 2017 scaling its Elixir (BEAM) real-time system to 5 million concurrent users. — [Discord, How Discord Scaled Elixir to 5,000,000 Concurrent Users, 2017](https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-scaled-elixir-to-5-000-000-concurrent-users)
- Orleans introduced virtual actors ("grains"): an actor always exists logically, is addressed by identity, is activated on demand on some server, deactivated when idle, and re-activated elsewhere after failure, so the application never manages actor lifecycle or placement. — [Bernstein, Bykov, Geller, Kliot, Thelin, Orleans: Distributed Virtual Actors for Programmability and Scalability, MSR-TR-2014-41](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/orleans-distributed-virtual-actors-for-programmability-and-scalability/)
- Orleans has run in production on Azure since 2011, initially for the Halo franchise's cloud services, and later for other Microsoft services. — [Microsoft Learn, Orleans overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/orleans/overview) (checked Nov 2026, search excerpt)
- Akka brought Erlang-style actors and supervision hierarchies to the JVM; in Akka every actor has a parent that is its supervisor, so the actor system is one tree. — [Akka documentation](https://doc.akka.io/)

### Inferences
- Actors versus processes versus objects: an actor is an object whose method calls are asynchronous messages and which has its own thread of control and failure boundary; a process-calculus process is anonymous and communicates over named channels, whereas an actor has an identity (address) and a mailbox. Actors are closest to the join calculus and the asynchronous pi-calculus, which is why they are implementable without rendezvous coordination.
- The Hewitt-Baker locality laws are the same rule as object-capability connectivity (section 5): reference only by creation, endowment or introduction. The actor model is therefore already an authority model if addresses are unforgeable.
- The supervision tree is the only widely deployed mechanism in this strand that is literally recursive governance: each level holds responsibility for the level below, handles what it can, and escalates what it cannot. It is a tree of failure responsibility that is orthogonal to the graph of message flow, which is the same place/link separation as bigraphs.
- The virtual actor idea (logical existence decoupled from physical activation) is what makes 10^10 addressable entities affordable: only the active fraction consumes resources.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found in this session for current (2025-2026) WhatsApp server-side architecture figures; the 2012-2015 numbers are the last primary-quality data points, and user counts above 2 billion come from secondary reporting.
- Vendor claims for Akka (tens of millions of messages per second per node, millions of actors per GB of heap) were not verified against a primary source in this session.
- No published figure was found for the largest number of live actors in a single Orleans or Akka cluster.

## 3. Petri nets, event structures, traces, true concurrency; workflow nets, BPMN, choreographies

### Takeaway
Petri nets and their relatives give a resource-and-causality view that process calculi lack: concurrency is independence, not interleaving, and state is distributed by construction. They are the semantic backbone of business-process notations, so they are the existing bridge between formal concurrency and organisational processes. Their analysis is decidable but can be astronomically expensive, and compositionality is weaker than in process algebra.

### Cited Findings
- A Petri net is a bipartite graph of places and transitions with a marking (tokens on places); a transition is enabled when its input places hold enough tokens, and firing consumes and produces tokens locally. Standard analysis questions are reachability, boundedness and liveness, with structural techniques (invariants, siphons/traps) for subclasses. — [Murata, Petri Nets: Properties, Analysis and Applications, Proc. IEEE 1989](https://doi.org/10.1109/5.24143)
- Reachability for general Petri nets (vector addition systems) is decidable but Ackermann-complete: the lower bound was established in 2021, matching the earlier upper bound. — [Czerwinski & Orlikowski, Reachability in Vector Addition Systems is Ackermann-complete, FOCS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13866); [Leroux, The Reachability Problem for Petri Nets is Not Primitive Recursive, FOCS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12695)
- Event structures (Nielsen, Plotkin, Winskel 1981): a set of events with a causality partial order and a conflict relation; configurations are conflict-free, causally closed sets. They connect Petri-net unfoldings to Scott domains. — [Nielsen, Plotkin, Winskel, Petri Nets, Event Structures and Domains, Part I, TCS 1981](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(81)90112-2)
- Mazurkiewicz traces (1977): equivalence classes of action sequences under commutation of independent actions; a trace is a partial order of events. — [Mazurkiewicz, Concurrent Program Schemes and their Interpretations, DAIMI PB-78, 1977](https://doi.org/10.7146/dpb.v6i78.7691)
- Interleaving equivalences are not preserved when an atomic action is refined into a multi-step process, whereas suitable partial-order ("true concurrency") equivalences, such as history-preserving bisimulation, are. — [van Glabbeek & Goltz, Refinement of Actions and Equivalence Notions for Concurrent Systems, Acta Informatica 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002360000041)
- Workflow nets (van der Aalst): a Petri net with one source place and one sink place where every node is on a path between them; the central correctness property is soundness (proper completion is always possible, no leftover tokens, no dead transitions), which reduces to liveness and boundedness of the short-circuited net. — [van der Aalst, The Application of Petri Nets to Workflow Management, J. Circuits, Systems and Computers 1998](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218126698000043)
- The workflow patterns initiative catalogued 20 original control-flow patterns (sequence, parallel split, synchronisation, exclusive choice, multi-merge, deferred choice, multiple instances, cancellation and others) and used them to compare workflow languages. — [van der Aalst, ter Hofstede, Kiepuszewski, Barros, Workflow Patterns, Distributed and Parallel Databases 2003](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022883727209)
- BPMN 2.0 is the OMG standard notation for business processes, with process, collaboration and choreography diagrams. — [OMG, BPMN 2.0 specification](https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/2.0/)
- Multiparty session types: a global type (choreography) is projected onto each participant's local type; well-typed participants are guaranteed communication safety, protocol fidelity and progress within a session. — [Honda, Yoshida, Carbone, Multiparty Asynchronous Session Types, POPL 2008](https://doi.org/10.1145/1328438.1328472)
- Choreographic programming: write the global interaction once and compile to endpoint code that is deadlock-free by construction. — [Carbone & Montesi, Deadlock-freedom-by-design: Multiparty Asynchronous Global Programming, POPL 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2429069.2429101)

### Inferences
- True-concurrency semantics matters for recursion: only equivalences that survive action refinement let one treat "an action" at one level as "a whole system" at the level below. This is a precise technical condition for "a system of systems is again a system" that interleaving semantics fails.
- Choreography-with-projection is the constructive counterpart to verification: a global contract generates local behaviours that are correct by construction. It works for bounded, pre-agreed protocols (a contract, a transaction, a procedure), not for an open population.
- Ackermann-completeness of reachability means whole-system verification is not an option at any interesting scale; guarantees must come from structure (subclasses such as free-choice nets, sound workflow nets, typed sessions) and from composition.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence was found on the largest Petri-net or BPMN models verified in practice.
- Open (compositional) Petri nets via decorated/structured cospans belong to the category-theory strand and were not researched here.

## 4. Distributed computing theory: how much global coordination can 10^10 actors afford, and which invariants survive without it?

### Takeaway
The impossibility results agree: global agreement, global simultaneity and global common knowledge are either impossible or cost time and messages that grow with participants and faults, so they must be confined to small groups. The positive results (CALM, CRDTs, invariant confluence) characterise exactly what can be done with no coordination at all: monotone computations and invariants that are closed under merge. At 10^10 actors the design rule is that coordination is a scarce, local resource, spent only on non-monotone decisions.

### Cited Findings
- FLP impossibility (Fischer, Lynch, Paterson 1985). Assumptions: asynchronous message passing (no bound on delays or relative speeds), reliable eventual delivery, deterministic processes, at most one crash failure. Result: no protocol solves consensus (agreement, validity, termination); every such protocol has an admissible execution that never decides. — [Fischer, Lynch, Paterson, Impossibility of Distributed Consensus with One Faulty Process, JACM 1985](https://doi.org/10.1145/3149.214121)
- Ways round FLP: partial synchrony (bounds exist but are unknown or hold only eventually) permits consensus with n >= 2f+1 for crash faults and n >= 3f+1 for Byzantine faults; safety always holds, liveness after stabilisation. — [Dwork, Lynch, Stockmeyer, Consensus in the Presence of Partial Synchrony, JACM 1988](https://doi.org/10.1145/42282.42283)
- Unreliable failure detectors: consensus is solvable with the eventually-weak detector class and a majority of correct processes. — [Chandra & Toueg, Unreliable Failure Detectors for Reliable Distributed Systems, JACM 1996](https://doi.org/10.1145/226643.226647)
- Byzantine bound: with unauthenticated ("oral") messages, agreement among n processes with f traitors is possible if and only if n >= 3f+1; with unforgeable signatures in a synchronous system, broadcast is solvable for any number of faults. — [Pease, Shostak, Lamport, Reaching Agreement in the Presence of Faults, JACM 1980](https://doi.org/10.1145/322186.322188); [Lamport, Shostak, Pease, The Byzantine Generals Problem, TOPLAS 1982](https://doi.org/10.1145/357172.357176)
- Message lower bound: deterministic Byzantine agreement needs Omega(f^2) messages (and Omega(nf) signatures in the authenticated case), so with f proportional to n the cost per decision is quadratic. — [Dolev & Reischuk, Bounds on Information Exchange for Byzantine Agreement, JACM 1985](https://doi.org/10.1145/2455.214112)
- Byzantine quorum systems: for arbitrary data, masking quorums require any two quorums to intersect in at least 2f+1 servers, which needs n >= 4f+1; dissemination quorums for self-verifying (signed) data need intersection of f+1 and n >= 3f+1. — [Malkhi & Reiter, Byzantine Quorum Systems, Distributed Computing 1998](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004460050050)
- Paxos and Raft implement crash-fault-tolerant replicated logs with majority quorums (2f+1 replicas tolerate f crashes); both funnel decisions through a leader. — [Lamport, The Part-Time Parliament, TOCS 1998](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/lamport-paxos.pdf); [Ongaro & Ousterhout, In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm, USENIX ATC 2014](https://raft.github.io/raft.pdf)
- PBFT made Byzantine replication practical with 3f+1 replicas and quadratic message complexity in the normal case; HotStuff reduced communication to linear per view with a rotating leader and threshold signatures. — [Castro & Liskov, Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance, OSDI 1999](https://pmg.csail.mit.edu/papers/osdi99.pdf); [Yin, Malkhi, Reiter, Gueta, Abraham, HotStuff, arXiv 1803.05069](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05069)
- CAP (Gilbert & Lynch 2002, formalising Brewer's conjecture). In an asynchronous network, no implementation of a read/write object can guarantee both atomic consistency (linearizability) and availability (every request to a non-failed node gets a response) in all executions in which messages may be lost (partitions). — [Gilbert & Lynch, Brewer's Conjecture and the Feasibility of Consistent, Available, Partition-Tolerant Web Services, SIGACT News 2002](https://doi.org/10.1145/564585.564601)
- Brewer's 2012 revision: "two of three" is misleading; partitions are rare, the choice between C and A is made per operation and per data item during a partition, and the design task is to detect partitions, limit operations, and reconcile and compensate afterwards. — [Brewer, CAP Twelve Years Later, IEEE Computer 2012](https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.2012.37)
- PACELC: if Partitioned, trade Availability against Consistency; Else trade Latency against Consistency. The consistency-latency trade-off exists all the time, not just during failures. — [Abadi, Consistency Tradeoffs in Modern Distributed Database System Design, IEEE Computer 2012](https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.2012.33)
- CALM theorem: a program has a consistent, coordination-free distributed implementation if and only if it is monotonic (its output only grows as its input grows). The informal statement is by Hellerstein (2010) and Hellerstein & Alvaro; the paper positions it as answering "what is the family of problems that can be consistently computed in a distributed fashion without coordination". — [Hellerstein & Alvaro, Keeping CALM: When Distributed Consistency is Easy, arXiv 1901.01930 / CACM 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01930) (abstract checked Nov 2026)
- The formal proof is in the relational-transducer model: the queries computable by coordination-free transducer networks are exactly the monotone queries. — [Ameloot, Neven, Van den Bussche, Relational Transducers for Declarative Networking, JACM 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2450142.2450151)
- CRDTs: if replica states form a join-semilattice, updates are inflationary and merge is the least upper bound (state-based), or concurrent operations commute and are delivered causally (operation-based), then replicas that have received the same updates are in the same state with no coordination. This is strong eventual consistency. — [Shapiro, Preguica, Baquero, Zawirski, Conflict-free Replicated Data Types, SSS 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-24550-3_29); [INRIA RR-7506, A Comprehensive Study of Convergent and Commutative Replicated Data Types](https://inria.hal.science/inria-00555588)
- Invariant confluence (Bailis et al. 2014/2015): a set of transactions can be executed with coordination-freedom, availability and convergence while preserving an application invariant I if and only if the transactions are I-confluent, meaning that merging any two I-valid divergent states reachable from a common ancestor yields an I-valid state. The paper states this as a necessary and sufficient condition. — [Bailis, Fekete, Franklin, Ghodsi, Hellerstein, Stoica, Coordination Avoidance in Database Systems, VLDB 2015 / arXiv 1402.2237](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2237) (checked Nov 2026)
- Empirical consequence in the same paper: 10 of the 12 TPC-C invariants are I-confluent, and a coordination-avoiding prototype reached over 12.7 million New-Order transactions per second on 200 servers, reported as a 25-fold improvement over prior results. — [Bailis et al., arXiv 1402.2237](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2237) (checked Nov 2026, search excerpt; an early arXiv version reported a smaller 100-server figure)
- Local-first software: CRDT-based applications keep the primary copy on the user's device, work offline and sync opportunistically; the paper sets out seven ideals and evaluates existing systems against them. — [Kleppmann, Wiggins, van Hardenberg, McGranaghan, Local-first Software, Onward! 2019](https://www.inkandswitch.com/local-first/)
- Asynchronous computability theorem (Herlihy & Shavit 1993/1999). In the wait-free shared-memory (read/write) model, a decision task is solvable if and only if there is a chromatic subdivision of the input complex and a colour-preserving simplicial map from it to the output complex that respects the task specification. Consequence: k-set agreement among n+1 processes is not wait-free solvable for k <= n. Solvability is a topological property (connectivity, holes) of the task, not of the algorithm. — [Herlihy & Shavit, The Topological Structure of Asynchronous Computability, JACM 1999](https://doi.org/10.1145/331524.331529)
- Consensus hierarchy: shared objects are ranked by consensus number, the largest number of processes for which they solve wait-free consensus; read/write registers have number 1, queues and test-and-set 2, compare-and-swap infinity; consensus objects are universal (any object can be built wait-free from them). — [Herlihy, Wait-free Synchronization, TOPLAS 1991](https://doi.org/10.1145/114005.102808)
- Common knowledge (Halpern & Moses 1984/1990): simultaneous coordinated action requires common knowledge; common knowledge cannot be attained in any system where communication is not guaranteed, nor in systems with guaranteed delivery but uncertain delivery time. Achievable weakenings are epsilon-common knowledge, eventual common knowledge and timestamped common knowledge, which correspond to "within epsilon", "eventually" and "at local clock time T" coordination. — [Halpern & Moses, Knowledge and Common Knowledge in a Distributed Environment, JACM 1990](https://doi.org/10.1145/79147.79161)
- Lamport (1978): in a distributed system events are only partially ordered by the happened-before relation; logical clocks give a consistent total order extending it, and a replicated state machine can be built on that order. — [Lamport, Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System, CACM 1978](https://doi.org/10.1145/359545.359563)
- Vector clocks characterise causality exactly, but any such exact characterisation needs vectors of size n in the worst case. — [Charron-Bost, Concerning the Size of Logical Clocks in Distributed Systems, IPL 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(91)90055-M)

### Inferences
- Coordination budget at 10^10. All-to-all BFT agreement among n = 10^10 actors would need on the order of n^2 = 10^20 messages per decision (Dolev-Reischuk with f proportional to n); even linear protocols need 10^10 messages and a leader that every actor can reach. Production consensus groups are 3 to 9 replicas for crash faults and tens to low thousands for BFT. Global consensus over the whole population is therefore not a usable primitive; consensus has to be a local service whose outputs are then disseminated monotonically.
- What can be kept without coordination, by CALM and I-confluence: anything expressible as growth in a lattice (sets that only grow, counters that only increase, maxima, logs keyed by unique IDs, grants of permission under attenuation-only delegation, evidence accumulation, reputation as a monotone aggregate), referential integrity on insert, and uniqueness when identifiers are locally generated.
- What cannot: uniqueness of a chosen value, mutual exclusion, non-negative balances under concurrent withdrawal, revocation and deletion that must be respected immediately, "exactly one holder of role R", sequential numbering, and any threshold test on a negation ("nobody objected"). These need either a single owner (sharding the invariant to one authority), an escrow/quota split (turning a global bound into local budgets, each locally enforceable), or a consensus group.
- The dominant engineering move is therefore to convert non-monotone global invariants into locally owned ones: partition the resource, give each partition one authority, and let cross-partition effects be monotone messages. This is the same shape as hierarchical delegation in DNS and IP addressing (section 6).
- Halpern-Moses implies that "everyone knows the rule changed at time T" is unattainable at scale except in the timestamped sense, which requires synchronised clocks with known error (the Spanner approach). Governance changes in a 10^10-actor system propagate eventually, never simultaneously; rules must be versioned and actors must tolerate neighbours on a different version.
- Charron-Bost's bound implies exact global causality tracking is unaffordable at 10^10 (vectors of 10^10 entries); causality must be tracked per scope (per object, per group, per cell), again forcing hierarchy.

### Gaps
- No theorem was found that directly bounds the fraction of a workload that must be coordinated as a function of n; CALM and I-confluence are per-program characterisations, not scaling laws.
- CALM's formal statement holds in specific models (relational transducers; refinements depend on what nodes know about data partitioning). The general informal statement should not be quoted as a fully model-independent theorem.

## 5. Capability-based security and object capabilities as a compositional theory of authority and delegation

### Takeaway
Object capabilities reduce authority to one rule, "a reference is the permission, and references are obtained only by creation, endowment or introduction", which is the same graph-rewriting rule as pi-calculus name passing and actor locality. Authority then composes by ordinary programming abstraction: attenuation, revocation, auditing and confinement are small patterns, not kernel features. It is the only authority model in this strand that is both decentralised (no global identity or policy store) and formally verified in deployed kernels, but end-user and inter-organisational deployments remain small and the open standards are still drafts in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Dennis & Van Horn (1966) introduced capabilities: a computation's authority is exactly its capability list (C-list) of unforgeable (object, rights) pairs, with protected procedures for controlled entry. — [Dennis & Van Horn, Programming Semantics for Multiprogrammed Computations, CACM 1966](https://doi.org/10.1145/365230.365252)
- Least privilege and seven other design principles (economy of mechanism, fail-safe defaults, complete mediation, open design, separation of privilege, least common mechanism, psychological acceptability). — [Saltzer & Schroeder, The Protection of Information in Computer Systems, Proc. IEEE 1975](https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1975.9939)
- Confused deputy (Hardy 1988): a program holding authority from two sources is tricked into using one principal's authority on a designation supplied by another; the root cause is separating designation (a name) from authority (ambient, identity-based permission). Capabilities combine the two and remove the class of bug. — [Hardy, The Confused Deputy, Operating Systems Review 1988](https://doi.org/10.1145/54289.871709)
- Safety is undecidable in the general access-matrix model: whether a given right can ever leak to a given subject cannot be decided for arbitrary protection systems. — [Harrison, Ruzzo, Ullman, Protection in Operating Systems, CACM 1976](https://doi.org/10.1145/360303.360333)
- In the take-grant capability model, by contrast, the safety question is decidable in linear time in the size of the protection graph. — [Lipton & Snyder, A Linear Time Algorithm for Deciding Subject Security, JACM 1977](https://doi.org/10.1145/322017.322025)
- "Capability Myths Demolished" refutes three claims (capabilities cannot confine, cannot revoke, are equivalent to ACLs) and distinguishes ACLs-as-columns, capabilities-as-rows, capabilities-as-keys and object capabilities. — [Miller, Yee, Shapiro, Capability Myths Demolished, SRL2003-02](https://srl.cs.jhu.edu/pubs/SRL2003-02.pdf)
- Miller's thesis (2006) argues that access control and concurrency control are one problem, robust composition under mutual suspicion; it sets out the object-capability model (authority only via references; references only by initial conditions, parenthood, endowment, introduction), the principle of least authority (POLA) applied at every scale from objects to organisations, and the E language's communicating event loops with promise pipelining. — [Miller, Robust Composition: Towards a Unified Approach to Access Control and Concurrency Control, PhD thesis, Johns Hopkins 2006](http://www.erights.org/talks/thesis/markm-thesis.pdf)
- Patterns expressed in the thesis and on erights.org: attenuation by forwarder/facet, revocation by caretaker (a revocable forwarder), the membrane (a caretaker that transitively wraps every reference passing through it, so a whole subgraph can be revoked at once), sealer/unsealer pairs (rights amplification), and the mint (money as an ocap abstraction). — [erights.org](http://www.erights.org/)
- Formal support: in a capability-safe language subset, the reachability-based authority analysis is sound (capability safety implies authority safety), proved for a JavaScript subset. — [Maffeis, Mitchell, Taly, Object Capabilities and Isolation of Untrusted JavaScript Applications, IEEE S&P 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.2010.16)
- Reference-graph reasoning alone is too coarse for stateful abstractions; logical relations with effect parametricity give compositional proofs of ocap patterns. — [Devriese, Birkedal, Piessens, Reasoning about Object Capabilities with Logical Relations and Effect Parametricity, EuroS&P 2016](https://doi.org/10.1109/EuroSP.2016.22)
- seL4 is a capability-based microkernel with a machine-checked proof that its C implementation refines its abstract specification, later extended to integrity and information-flow (confidentiality) proofs and binary correctness on some platforms. — [Klein et al., seL4: Formal Verification of an OS Kernel, SOSP 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1629575.1629596); [seL4 whitepaper](https://sel4.systems/About/seL4-whitepaper.pdf)
- CHERI extends processor instruction sets with hardware capabilities (bounded, permission-carrying, tagged pointers that cannot be forged and can only be narrowed). — [University of Cambridge, CHERI project](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/security/ctsrd/cheri/)
- CHERI status 2026: the CHERIoT project announced first CHERIoT silicon in March 2026. CHERI-seL4 was released in 2025 as an experimental prototype that is not formally verified. — [cheriot.org](https://cheriot.org/); [Cambridge CHERI-seL4 page](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/security/ctsrd/cheri/cheri-sel4.html) (checked Nov 2026, search excerpts)
- Cap'n Proto RPC implements the E-derived CapTP model, with object references as capabilities passed in messages and promise pipelining ("time-travel RPC"). — [Cap'n Proto RPC protocol](https://capnproto.org/rpc.html)
- Macaroons: bearer credentials built from chained HMACs, to which any holder can append caveats that only restrict authority (attenuation and contextual confinement without contacting the issuer), with third-party caveats for delegated checks. — [Birgisson, Politz, Erlingsson, Taly, Vrable, Lentczner, Macaroons, NDSS 2014](https://research.google/pubs/macaroons-cookies-with-contextual-caveats-for-decentralized-authorization-in-the-cloud/)
- UCAN: signed, chained delegation tokens rooted in public-key identifiers (DIDs), verifiable offline; as of late 2026 the specification repository lists v1.0.0-rc.1 as current, with separate Delegation, Invocation, Promise and Revocation sub-specifications; no final 1.0 was found. — [ucan-wg/spec](https://github.com/ucan-wg/spec) (checked Nov 2026)
- OCapN (Spritely Institute and others) is a pre-standardisation effort for an interoperable CapTP, netlayers and capability URIs; Spritely Goblins is a distributed, transactional ocap actor system. Draft specifications are not yet frozen; Spritely's own documentation warns that the protocol is not stable, and the group states an intention to take the documents to a standards body later. — [ocapn/ocapn](https://github.com/ocapn/ocapn); [Spritely, Introducing OCapN](https://spritely.institute/news/introducing-ocapn-interoperable-capabilities-over-the-network.html) (checked Nov 2026)

### Inferences
- Contrast with ACL/identity-based control: ACLs need a global namespace of principals, a policy store consulted on each access, and administrators with authority over the store; delegation needs either sharing identity or an administrator. Capabilities need none of these, so authority checks are local and O(1) and delegation is a message send. At 10^10 actors with no common administrator that is a structural advantage, not a stylistic one.
- Attenuation-only delegation is monotone in the CALM sense (a delegation chain only ever narrows, and verifying a chain needs no coordination), which is why macaroons and UCAN can be verified offline. Revocation is the non-monotone operation: it needs either a reachable caretaker (online check), short expiry with renewal, or a propagated revocation set that is only eventually consistent. This is the capability-world instance of the section 4 boundary.
- The membrane is the capability analogue of an ambient boundary or a supervision scope: one object mediates all traffic across a boundary and can sever the whole subgraph. Nested membranes give nested, revocable domains of authority, a candidate mechanism for "a system of systems is again a system" on the authority axis.
- HRU versus take-grant shows that decidability of "can this authority ever reach that actor" depends on restricting the rule set; ocap systems inherit the tractable case only for the reference graph, and behaviour of the objects on the path has to be reasoned about separately (Devriese et al.).
- Capabilities say who can do what; they do not say who is accountable. Pure bearer authority has no identity for audit. Deployed designs (UCAN, signed delegation chains) re-introduce keys for accountability while keeping authority in the chain.

### Gaps
- No deployment figures were found for ocap systems at user scale beyond kernels and infrastructure (seL4 device counts, Cloudflare Workers' use of Cap'n Proto) in this session.
- No formal treatment of capability revocation latency or cost at very large scale was found.
- Whether CHERI silicon is shipping in volume products in 2026 was not established; sources conflict on dates of first commercial silicon.

## 6. Architecture principles from systems that run at 10^9 to 10^10 endpoints

### Takeaway
Every system that actually reaches billions of endpoints is a federation of autonomous domains joined by a deliberately thin shared protocol, with hierarchical naming and addressing, hard state owned locally, and only soft, eventually consistent state shared globally. None of them runs a global consensus or a global controller; control is exercised by delegation of namespaces and by local reconciliation loops, and failures are contained by cells.

### Cited Findings
- Scale reference points (dated): about 6 billion Internet users (74% of world population) and 9.2 billion mobile-cellular subscriptions in 2025. — [ITU, Facts and Figures 2025 press release, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.itu.int/en/mediacentre/Pages/PR-2025-11-17-Facts-and-Figures.aspx) (checked Nov 2026)
- The global IPv4 BGP table held 1,044,785 entries announced by 78,339 autonomous systems on 18 April 2026. — [APNIC weekly routing table report to NANOG, 18 Apr 2026](https://seclists.org/nanog/2026/Apr/44); see also [Huston, BGP in 2025, APNIC blog](https://blog.apnic.net/2026/01/08/bgp-in-2025/) (checked Nov 2026)
- Visa processed 257.5 billion transactions on its networks in fiscal 2025 (233.8 billion in fiscal 2024). — [Visa Fiscal 2025 Annual Report](https://s29.q4cdn.com/385744025/files/doc_downloads/2025/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf) (checked Nov 2026, search excerpt)
- India's UPI processed a record 24.51 billion transactions in August 2026 and 24.07 billion in September 2026, with daily volume above 800 million. — [StartupTalky reporting NPCI data, Sept 2026](https://startuptalky.com/india-upi-transactions-september-2026/) (checked Nov 2026; secondary report of NPCI data)
- End-to-end argument: a function can be completely and correctly implemented only with the knowledge of the application at the endpoints, so placing it in the network is at best a performance optimisation. — [Saltzer, Reed, Clark, End-to-End Arguments in System Design, TOCS 1984](https://doi.org/10.1145/357401.357402)
- Internet design priorities in order: survivability under loss of networks or gateways, multiple service types, variety of networks, distributed management, cost effectiveness, easy host attachment, accountability. Survivability led to "fate-sharing": conversation state lives in the endpoints, and gateways are stateless. — [Clark, The Design Philosophy of the DARPA Internet Protocols, SIGCOMM 1988](https://doi.org/10.1145/52324.52336)
- Hierarchical routing: clustering nodes into an m-level hierarchy reduces routing table size from N to roughly on the order of ln N entries at optimal clustering, with bounded path-length increase. — [Kleinrock & Kamoun, Hierarchical Routing for Large Networks, Computer Networks 1977](https://doi.org/10.1016/0376-5075(77)90022-2)
- CIDR: address allocation follows provider topology so that routes aggregate; this is what kept the global table near 10^6 entries for more than 10^9 hosts. — [RFC 4632, Classless Inter-domain Routing](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4632)
- BGP: each autonomous system is a separately administered routing domain that applies its own local policy and exchanges path-vector reachability with neighbours. — [RFC 4271, A Border Gateway Protocol 4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4271)
- BGP with arbitrary policies need not converge: deciding whether a stable routing exists (the stable paths problem) is NP-complete, and instances exist with no stable solution. — [Griffin, Shepherd, Wilfong, The Stable Paths Problem and Interdomain Routing, IEEE/ACM ToN 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/90.993304)
- Gao-Rexford: if every AS follows local rules derived from customer-provider and peer relationships (prefer customer routes; do not export peer or provider routes to other peers or providers) and the provider hierarchy is acyclic, BGP is guaranteed to converge without any global coordination. — [Gao & Rexford, Stable Internet Routing Without Global Coordination, IEEE/ACM ToN 2001](https://doi.org/10.1109/90.974523)
- DNS: a tree-structured namespace divided into zones; authority for a subtree is delegated to its administrator, and resolvers cache answers for a time-to-live, so the data are loosely coherent by design. — [RFC 1034, Domain Names: Concepts and Facilities](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1034)
- Hourglass: a minimal spanning layer in the middle (IP) maximises both the applications that can be supported above and the technologies that can support it below; a formal treatment argues that the weaker (logically simpler) the waist specification, the more implementations it admits. — [Beck, On the Hourglass Model, CACM 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3274770)
- An evolutionary model shows layered protocol stacks under competition converge to an hourglass with few, long-lived protocols at the waist. — [Akhshabi & Dovrolis, The Evolution of Layered Protocol Stacks Leads to an Hourglass-Shaped Architecture, SIGCOMM 2011](https://doi.org/10.1145/2018436.2018460)
- REST: constraints are client-server, statelessness, cacheability, uniform interface, layered system and optional code on demand; the stated aim is scalability of component interactions, independent evolution and intermediaries. — [Fielding, Architectural Styles and the Design of Network-based Software Architectures, PhD thesis, 2000](https://ics.uci.edu/~fielding/pubs/dissertation/top.htm)
- Dynamo: an always-writable key-value store built from consistent hashing, sloppy quorums, vector clocks, hinted handoff, Merkle-tree anti-entropy and gossip-based membership; it chose availability over consistency for the shopping-cart class of workload. — [DeCandia et al., Dynamo: Amazon's Highly Available Key-value Store, SOSP 2007](https://www.allthingsdistributed.com/files/amazon-dynamo-sosp2007.pdf)
- Spanner: globally distributed, externally consistent transactions using Paxos groups per shard plus TrueTime (clock uncertainty made explicit and waited out). Strong consistency at scale is bought by sharding consensus and bounding clock error. — [Corbett et al., Spanner: Google's Globally-Distributed Database, OSDI 2012](https://research.google/pubs/spanner-googles-globally-distributed-database-2/)
- Kubernetes lineage: Borg, Omega and Kubernetes converged on declarative desired state held in a central store, with many independent controllers each running a reconciliation loop (observe, diff, act) that is level-triggered and idempotent. — [Burns, Grant, Oppenheimer, Brewer, Wilkes, Borg, Omega, and Kubernetes, ACM Queue 2016](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=2898444)
- Kubernetes's documented single-cluster design envelope is 5,000 nodes, 150,000 pods and 300,000 containers. — [Kubernetes documentation, Considerations for large clusters](https://kubernetes.io/docs/setup/best-practices/cluster-large/)
- GKE supports 65,000 nodes per cluster, and Google reported running a 130,000-node cluster in experimental mode in late 2025. — [Google Cloud blog, How we built a 130,000-node GKE cluster](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/how-we-built-a-130000-node-gke-cluster/) (checked Nov 2026)
- Cell-based architecture: a workload is split into independent, complete replicas (cells) behind a thin routing layer; each cell has a capped maximum size and no shared state with others, so a failure, bad deployment or poison request affects at most one cell's share of customers. — [AWS Well-Architected, Reducing the Scope of Impact with Cell-Based Architecture](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/reducing-scope-of-impact-with-cell-based-architecture/reducing-scope-of-impact-with-cell-based-architecture.html)
- Shuffle sharding assigns each customer a random subset of workers so that the number of customers sharing exactly the same subset is combinatorially small. — [AWS Builders' Library, Workload Isolation Using Shuffle-Sharding](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/workload-isolation-using-shuffle-sharding/)
- Static stability: the data plane must keep working with the last known configuration when the control plane is unavailable. — [AWS Builders' Library, Static Stability Using Availability Zones](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/static-stability-using-availability-zones/)
- Counter-example of global control-plane coupling: on 4 October 2021 a backbone configuration command disconnected Facebook's data centres; DNS servers then withdrew their BGP advertisements, taking all services offline for about six hours, and the loss of internal tools slowed recovery. — [Meta Engineering, More details about the October 4 outage](https://engineering.fb.com/2021/10/05/networking-traffic/outage-details/)
- Promise theory (Burgess; Bergstra & Burgess): agents are autonomous; an agent can only make promises about its own behaviour; a promise is not an obligation; cooperation requires a promise to give matched by a promise to accept (use); impositions and obligations are modelled separately and are argued to be unreliable as a foundation. CFEngine applies this as convergent, idempotent, locally evaluated configuration. — [Bergstra & Burgess, Promise Theory: Principles and Applications (Book of Promises)](https://markburgess.org/BookOfPromises.pdf); [Bergstra & Burgess, Promises, Impositions, and other Directionals, arXiv 1401.3381](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3381) (checked Nov 2026)
- Intent-based networking: an intent is a declarative statement of operational goals and outcomes without specifying how to achieve them; the system translates, activates and continuously assures it in a closed loop. — [RFC 9315, Intent-Based Networking: Concepts and Definitions, 2022](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9315)

### Inferences
- Recurrent structural properties across Internet, DNS, Web, cellular, payments and cloud control planes:
  1. Federation of autonomous domains. Tens of thousands of ASes, hundreds of operators and thousands of issuing banks each own their internal state and policy.
  2. Hierarchical, delegated naming and addressing that aggregates. This is what compresses 10^9 to 10^10 endpoints into about 10^6 routing entries and about 10^5 domains of control.
  3. A narrow waist: one small, weak, stable interface that everything shares (IP datagram, DNS query, HTTP verb on a URL, ISO-style payment message).
  4. Soft state plus caching plus eventual consistency for everything global; hard state only at the owner.
  5. Idempotent, retryable operations and level-triggered reconciliation rather than exactly-once commands.
  6. Local policy with provable global properties under structural restrictions (Gao-Rexford) and without them possible divergence (Griffin et al.). This is the clearest worked example of "local rules, global guarantee" in a deployed planetary system.
  7. Control plane separated from data plane, with static stability; the 2021 Facebook outage shows what a globally coupled control plane costs.
  8. Blast-radius caps: cells, zones, shards with maximum size.
  9. Backpressure, load shedding and admission control at every boundary.
- The largest centrally reconciled control domain publicly documented is of order 10^5 nodes (GKE at 65,000 supported, 130,000 experimental). Reaching 10^10 needs about five more orders of magnitude, which the evidence says come only from federation and hierarchy, not from a bigger controller.
- Promise theory is the only item in this strand that starts from the autonomy of agents as an axiom and derives cooperation from voluntary, bilateral promises. It matches how BGP peering, DNS delegation and payment-network membership actually work, and it is the natural model for actors (humans, organisations, AI agents) that cannot be commanded. It is less rigorous than process calculi (no canonical semantics or congruence results were found).
- Payment networks show that strong invariants (no double spend of a balance) are kept by assigning each account to exactly one authority (the issuer) and by separating real-time authorisation from later clearing and settlement by netting. This is the I-confluence recipe: single-owner for the non-monotone part, eventual reconciliation for the rest. (Structural description; no primary architectural source retrieved this session.)

### Gaps
- Cloudflare's current request and DNS-query rates and the global count of connected IoT devices were searched but only unverified secondary figures were found; they are omitted.
- Cellular core architecture (3GPP home/visited network federation, roaming) was not researched from primary specifications in this session.
- The VisaNet capacity figure (65,000 versus 83,000 transaction messages per second) differs between Visa pages; neither was confirmed from a primary document read in full.

## 7. Scaling laws and their implications for fractal hierarchies

### Takeaway
The scaling laws all penalise pairwise interaction and shared serialised resources, and reward logarithmic structures. For 10^10 actors, hierarchy depth is only 5 to 12 levels for spans of 150 down to 7, and epidemic dissemination takes a few dozen rounds, so depth and diffusion time are not the binding constraints. The binding constraints are the coherency (crosstalk) term, the fragility of hubs, and the latency and distortion of control across levels.

### Cited Findings
- Amdahl's law: with serial fraction s, speedup on N processors is at most 1 / (s + (1 - s)/N), bounded by 1/s. — [Amdahl, Validity of the Single Processor Approach to Achieving Large Scale Computing Capabilities, AFIPS 1967](https://doi.org/10.1145/1465482.1465560)
- Universal Scalability Law: relative capacity C(N) = N / (1 + alpha(N - 1) + beta N(N - 1)), where alpha is contention (queueing for shared resources) and beta is coherency delay (pairwise exchange to keep state consistent). With beta > 0 throughput peaks at N* = sqrt((1 - alpha)/beta) and then falls (retrograde scaling). — [Gunther, A General Theory of Computational Scalability Based on Rational Functions, arXiv 0808.1431](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1431)
- Brooks: n people have n(n - 1)/2 potential communication channels; adding people to a late project makes it later. — [Brooks, The Mythical Man-Month, 1975 (anniversary edition)](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/mythical-man-month-the-essays-on-software-engineering-anniversary-edition/P200000009278)
- Metcalfe's n^2 value claim is contested; Briscoe, Odlyzko and Tilly argue network value grows as n log n. — [Briscoe, Odlyzko, Tilly, Metcalfe's Law is Wrong, IEEE Spectrum 2006](https://spectrum.ieee.org/metcalfes-law-is-wrong)
- Small worlds: rewiring a small fraction of edges in a regular lattice gives short average path lengths (logarithmic in n) while keeping high clustering. — [Watts & Strogatz, Collective Dynamics of Small-World Networks, Nature 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/30918)
- Decentralised search is efficient only for a specific structure: on a d-dimensional lattice with long-range links chosen with probability proportional to distance^(-r), greedy routing with local information finds short paths in polylogarithmic time if and only if r = d. — [Kleinberg, The Small-World Phenomenon: An Algorithmic Perspective, STOC 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/335305.335325)
- The Facebook graph of 721 million users (2011) had average distance 4.74. — [Backstrom, Boldi, Rosa, Ugander, Vigna, Four Degrees of Separation, arXiv 1111.4570](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.4570)
- Scale-free networks arise from growth with preferential attachment and have power-law degree distributions. They are robust to random node failure but fragile to targeted removal of hubs. — [Barabasi & Albert, Emergence of Scaling in Random Networks, Science 1999](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.286.5439.509); [Albert, Jeong, Barabasi, Error and Attack Tolerance of Complex Networks, Nature 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35019019)
- Epidemic (gossip) protocols: anti-entropy and rumour mongering spread an update to all n sites in O(log n) rounds with simple, failure-tolerant local rules. — [Demers et al., Epidemic Algorithms for Replicated Database Maintenance, PODC 1987](https://doi.org/10.1145/41840.41841)
- Push rumour spreading on the complete graph informs everyone in log2 n + ln n + O(1) rounds with high probability. — [Pittel, On Spreading a Rumor, SIAM J. Appl. Math. 1987](https://doi.org/10.1137/0147013)
- Push-pull rumour spreading needs O(log n) rounds and only O(n log log n) message transmissions, and this is near-optimal for address-oblivious algorithms. — [Karp, Schindelhauer, Shenker, Vocking, Randomized Rumor Spreading, FOCS 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.2000.892324)
- SWIM: membership protocol with constant per-member message load and expected failure-detection time independent of group size, with infection-style dissemination. — [Das, Gupta, Motivala, SWIM, DSN 2002](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/projects/Quicksilver/public_pdfs/SWIM.pdf)
- Structured overlays: Chord resolves a key in O(log N) hops with O(log N) routing state per node. — [Stoica, Morris, Karger, Kaashoek, Balakrishnan, Chord, SIGCOMM 2001](https://doi.org/10.1145/383059.383071)
- Dunbar's regression of neocortex ratio against group size in primates predicts a human group size of about 150. — [Dunbar, Neocortical Size as a Constraint on Group Size in Primates, J. Human Evolution 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0047-2484(92)90081-J)

  | 100 | 5 | 1.0 x 10^8 | 1.0% |
  | 150 | 5 | 6.7 x 10^7 | 0.7% |

- Diffusion arithmetic (researcher's computation from Pittel's formula): for n = 10^10, log2 n is about 33.2 and ln n about 23.0, so push gossip informs everyone in roughly 56 rounds; push-pull in a small multiple of 33. Hierarchical routing state per node is of order log N, a few tens of entries.
- Pairwise arithmetic: 10^10 actors have about 5 x 10^19 potential pairs. Any design whose cost has a beta N^2 term with non-negligible beta is retrograde long before this; the USL peak N* = sqrt((1 - alpha)/beta) means that to scale one coherent group to 10^10 would need beta below about 10^-20. Hence coherent groups must be small, and scale has to come from many groups whose mutual coupling is weak and mediated by aggregation.
- A fractal hierarchy with small-world cross-links combines the logarithmic depth of trees with the short lateral paths and robustness of small-world graphs; Kleinberg's result says the cross-links must follow the right distance distribution for local agents to route without global knowledge, so "add some shortcuts" is not enough. Scale-free hubs give efficiency and a targeted-failure liability, which is the argument for capping cell and hub size (section 6).
- With 5 to 12 levels and a per-level control delay tau, top-to-bottom reaction time is depth times tau and each level can distort or filter. This is the structural reason deployed systems push decisions to the lowest level that owns the state and pass only aggregates and intents upward and downward. (The control-theoretic treatment belongs to the cybernetics strand.)

### Gaps
- No primary source was found that measures USL alpha and beta coefficients for organisations or for whole distributed platforms at planetary scale.
- Span-of-control evidence (Graicunas, Urwick, modern organisational data) is left to the organisation-theory strand; the range 7 to 150 used above is the range given in the brief.

## 8. Self-similar and recursive system designs; blockchains and DAOs as programmable institutions

### Takeaway
Several designs make recursion the primary idea: RINA (one layer type repeated over different scopes), the Fractal component model (components containing components with a reflective membrane), holonic manufacturing and systems-of-systems engineering. Their shared lesson is that the repeating unit must carry both a functional interface and its own management/control interface. Blockchains show the opposite extreme, a single global consensus used as an institution, and their measured throughput confirms the section 4 limits: scale has come from layering and sharding, not from the global ledger.

### Cited Findings
- RINA: networking is inter-process communication and only IPC. There is a single type of layer, the Distributed IPC Facility (DIF), which repeats over different scopes; every DIF has the same mechanisms (data transfer, data-transfer control, layer management, i.e. enrolment, routing, resource allocation and security) with different policies. Applications ask for a destination by name, addresses are internal to a DIF, and joining a DIF requires explicit enrolment. — [Day, Matta, Mattar, Networking is IPC: A Guiding Principle to a Better Internet, ReArch/CoNEXT 2008](https://doi.org/10.1145/1544012.1544079)
- Fractal component model: components are recursively composable (composite components contain sub-components, with sharing allowed), and each component has a membrane exposing control interfaces for introspection and reconfiguration (attribute, binding, content and lifecycle controllers). — [Bruneton, Coupaye, Leclercq, Quema, Stefani, The Fractal Component Model and its Support in Java, Software: Practice and Experience 2006](https://doi.org/10.1002/spe.767)
- Holonic manufacturing: PROSA defines three basic holon types (product, resource, order) plus optional staff holons that advise; holons are autonomous and cooperative, and aggregate into larger holons. — [Van Brussel, Wyns, Valckenaers, Bongaerts, Peeters, Reference Architecture for Holonic Manufacturing Systems: PROSA, Computers in Industry 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-3615(98)00102-X)
- Systems of systems: the defining traits are operational and managerial independence of the constituents, evolutionary development, emergent behaviour and geographic distribution; the architecting principles are stable intermediate forms, policy triage, leverage at the interfaces and ensuring cooperation, because constituents cannot be commanded. — [Maier, Architecting Principles for Systems-of-Systems, Systems Engineering 1998](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1520-6858(1998)1:4<267::AID-SYS3>3.0.CO;2-D)
- Bitcoin introduced open-membership consensus by proof of work on a hash chain. — [Nakamoto, Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System, 2008](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf)
- A 2016 measurement study found that Bitcoin's parameters limited it to roughly 3 to 7 transactions per second and that re-parametrisation of block size and interval could give only limited gains, so that scaling needs protocol redesign (sharding, off-chain, layering). — [Croman et al., On Scaling Decentralized Blockchains, FC Workshops 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-53357-4_8)
- Ethereum in late 2025: the block gas limit rose from 45 million to 60 million on 25 November 2025, ahead of the Fusaka upgrade. — [The Block, Ethereum raises block gas limit to 60M, Nov 2025](https://www.theblock.co/post/380687/ethereum-block-gas-limit-fusaka) (checked Nov 2026)
- Layer-2 throughput: secondary sources report combined Ethereum layer-2 throughput exceeding about 5,600 transactions per second in early 2026, with higher short-lived peaks dominated by single application-specific rollups. These are not from a primary measurement source and methodologies differ. — [BlockEden, Fusaka blob expansion and L2 fees, Jan 2026](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/01/24/ethereum-fusaka-blob-expansion-l2-fees-peerdas/) (checked Nov 2026; secondary, treat as approximate)

### Inferences
- RINA is the cleanest existing statement of "one recursive unit with a fixed mechanism set and variable policy, repeated at every scope, with explicit enrolment". Replace "IPC" by "interaction" and the DIF is a candidate template for a recursive governed domain: common mechanisms, scope-specific policy, names outside and addresses inside, membership by enrolment.
- Fractal's membrane, RINA's layer management, Erlang's supervisor and the ocap membrane are the same design element seen from four communities: every composite carries a control interface that mediates what crosses its boundary and manages what is inside. This convergence is evidence that the recursive unit should be (content, boundary controller, external interface).
- Maier's criteria apply directly to the target population: organisations, governments and AI agents are operationally and managerially independent, so the governing model must be cooperation-based (promises, contracts, capabilities), not command-based.
- Blockchains as programmable institutions: a smart-contract platform gives a single totally ordered, globally verifiable rule-execution log. Comparing orders of magnitude, the base layers deliver 10^1 to 10^2 transactions per second and the layer-2 ecosystem 10^3 to 10^4, against about 8 x 10^3 per second average for one card network (257.5 billion per year) and about 9 x 10^3 per second average for UPI (24 billion per month). Programmable global consensus is therefore viable as a root of trust and settlement layer for a hierarchy, not as the interaction substrate for 10^10 actors. The rollup pattern (local execution, periodic commitment to a shared root) is itself a recursive hierarchy.

### Gaps
- No reliable primary data were found in this session on DAO governance scale (number of DAOs, voter participation rates, concentration of voting power) or on current Ethereum validator counts; l2beat-style primary throughput measurements were not retrieved.
- Digital twins were not researched; no formal compositional theory of digital twins was identified in this strand.
- RINA deployment remains experimental as far as the researcher knows; no production-scale deployment evidence was found or searched in depth.
- Multi-agent-systems organisational models (for example electronic institutions, normative MAS) are left to the AI-agent governance strand.

## 9. Reflection in systems: meta-object protocols, reflective towers, MAPE-K, control plane versus data plane

### Takeaway
Computational reflection has a precise meaning: a system holds a causally connected representation of itself that it can inspect and modify. The engineering forms that scale all bound it: the meta-level is a separate, smaller system (a control plane) with a narrow, declared protocol to the base level, and the potentially infinite tower is cut off after one or two levels.

### Cited Findings
- 3-Lisp (Smith 1982/1984): procedural reflection through an infinite tower of interpreters, each interpreting the one below; a reflective procedure runs at the level of the interpreter with access to the expression, environment and continuation of the level below. — [Smith, Reflection and Semantics in Lisp, POPL 1984](https://doi.org/10.1145/800017.800513)
- The tower can be explained and implemented finitely: levels are created lazily, only when reflective code actually runs. — [Wand & Friedman, The Mystery of the Tower Revealed, LFP 1986](https://doi.org/10.1145/319838.319871)
- Maes defined computational reflection for object-oriented languages: a reflective system has a causally connected self-representation, and every object has a meta-object. — [Maes, Concepts and Experiments in Computational Reflection, OOPSLA 1987](https://doi.org/10.1145/38765.38821)
- Meta-object protocols: the language implementation is itself an object-oriented program whose documented protocol lets users adjust the language's semantics incrementally, within bounds set by the protocol designer. — [Kiczales, des Rivieres, Bobrow, The Art of the Metaobject Protocol, MIT Press 1991](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262610742/the-art-of-the-metaobject-protocol/)
- Autonomic computing: systems manage themselves according to administrator goals through self-configuration, self-optimisation, self-healing and self-protection; an autonomic element is a managed resource plus an autonomic manager running a Monitor-Analyse-Plan-Execute loop over shared Knowledge (MAPE-K), and autonomic elements interact with one another. — [Kephart & Chess, The Vision of Autonomic Computing, IEEE Computer 2003](https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.2003.1160055)
- A three-layer reference architecture for self-managed systems: component control (feedback loops), change management (executing pre-computed plans in response to state changes) and goal management (time-consuming planning from high-level goals). — [Kramer & Magee, Self-Managed Systems: An Architectural Challenge, FOSE 2007](https://doi.org/10.1109/FOSE.2007.19)
- Patterns for decentralising MAPE-K loops across many managed subsystems: coordinated control, information sharing, master/slave, regional planning and hierarchical control, each trading scalability against global optimality. — [Weyns et al., On Patterns for Decentralized Control in Self-Adaptive Systems, LNCS 7475, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-35813-5_4)
- Control plane/data plane separation: the 4D proposal split network control into decision, dissemination, discovery and data planes, with network-wide objectives set in the decision plane; OpenFlow gave a standard interface for an external controller to program switch flow tables. — [Greenberg et al., A Clean Slate 4D Approach to Network Control and Management, CCR 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1096536.1096541); [McKeown et al., OpenFlow: Enabling Innovation in Campus Networks, CCR 2008](https://doi.org/10.1145/1355734.1355746)
- The rho-calculus makes reflection a primitive of a concurrency calculus through quote and dereference of processes as names. — [Meredith & Radestock, A Reflective Higher-order Calculus, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcs.2005.05.016)
- Kubernetes is reflective in the MOP sense: its API objects describe the system's own desired state and controllers reconcile towards them; custom resources and controllers extend the system using the same mechanism it uses for itself. — [Burns et al., Borg, Omega, and Kubernetes, ACM Queue 2016](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=2898444)

### Inferences
- Three workable forms of self-reflection, in increasing scalability and decreasing generality: (1) full reflective tower (3-Lisp, rho-calculus), which is maximally expressive, with no deployed large-scale use found; (2) meta-object protocol, i.e. reflection through a designed, bounded interface, which is the form used by Fractal membranes, Erlang supervisors and Kubernetes controllers; (3) control plane over data plane, where the meta-level is a separate system, which is the planetary-scale form.
- A recursive architecture gets reflection cheaply if each unit's controller is itself a unit of the same kind: the meta-level of level k is an ordinary object at level k+1. Supervision trees, hierarchical MAPE-K and RINA's management DIFs are all of this kind. This gives a finite, lazily extended tower in the Wand-Friedman sense rather than an infinite one.
- The operational hazards of reflection at scale are documented in section 6: a control plane that depends on the data plane it controls (Facebook 2021), and data planes that are not statically stable. Reflection must be stratified with respect to dependency: the meta-level may observe and change the base, and the base must be able to keep running without it.

### Gaps
- No formal result was found connecting reflective towers to distributed-systems limits (for example what a system can consistently know about its own global state); the nearest results are Halpern-Moses on common knowledge and consistent-snapshot theory, and the logical limits belong to the self-reference strand.
- No evidence was collected on how self-adaptive (MAPE-K) research prototypes perform beyond small scale.

## 10. Comparison of candidate minimal kernels

### Takeaway
No single existing formalism covers interaction, nesting, authority, failure and consistency at once, but the candidates are strikingly compatible. They converge on a small set of primitives: autonomous units with private state, asynchronous messages, unforgeable references passed by introduction, nested boundaries with a controller, and monotone (lattice) shared state, with consensus confined to small local groups. The strongest composite kernel supported by both theory and operating evidence is "capability-secure actors inside nested supervised boundaries, sharing only monotone state, coordinating locally".

### Inferences
- Convergent primitives. Reading across the table, five ingredients recur and are mutually consistent:
  1. Unit: an autonomous entity with private state and behaviour (actor, process, agent, holon, component).
  2. Interaction: asynchronous message passing over references; no shared mutable state; no global clock (partial order of events).
  3. Authority: references are unforgeable and transferable only by introduction; delegation is attenuating; so the reference graph is the authority graph (pi restriction = actor locality = ocap rule).
  4. Boundary: units nest inside boundaries that have a controller responsible for what is inside and for what crosses (ambient, bigraph place, supervisor, membrane, DIF, cell). Placing and linking are independent structures, which is Milner's bigraph insight and is seen in practice as supervision tree versus message graph, and DNS/address hierarchy versus traffic graph.
  5. Shared state: only monotone, mergeable (lattice) state is shared without coordination; every non-monotone invariant has a single owning boundary or a small local consensus group.
- Suggested composite kernel (a synthesis, not an existing formalism): a bigraph-like structure whose nodes are capability-secure actors, whose place graph is a supervision/membrane hierarchy, whose link graph is the capability graph, whose shared data are lattices, and whose reaction rules are local. Each composite presents itself outward as a single actor (recursion), and its controller is an ordinary actor one level up (bounded reflection). Coordination primitives (consensus, locks, uniqueness) exist only inside a boundary of bounded size.
- What each existing candidate lacks relative to that composite: process calculi lack authority and failure; actors lack a formal congruence theory in common use and a data-consistency theory; CRDT/CALM lacks structure and authority; ocaps lack liveness, failure handling and accountability; promise theory lacks formal semantics; RINA and reconciliation lack formal foundations; ledgers lack scale.
- What theory says such a kernel cannot do, whatever its design: reach global agreement in bounded time under asynchrony and failure (FLP); stay both linearizable and available under partition (CAP); achieve simultaneous global knowledge of anything (Halpern-Moses); enforce non-monotone global invariants without coordination (CALM, I-confluence); verify whole-system reachability properties at scale (Ackermann-complete Petri-net reachability, undecidable HRU safety); or keep a coherent group growing without retrograde scaling if pairwise coherency cost is non-zero (USL). Governance at 10^10 therefore has to be local enforcement plus monotone global propagation plus structural guarantees of the Gao-Rexford type (local rules whose global consequence is proved once).
- What operating evidence says does scale: federation of autonomous domains, delegated hierarchical namespaces, a thin weak shared protocol, idempotent declarative reconciliation, static stability, capped cells, gossip dissemination, and soft state. None of the 10^9-endpoint systems reviewed uses a global controller or global consensus.

### Gaps
- No published formalism was found that integrates all five ingredients with a proved congruence result. The closest are bigraphs (structure and congruence, no authority or data consistency), psi-calculi (parametric data and logic, no nesting) and ocap actor systems such as E and Goblins (authority and distribution, limited formal metatheory).
- No evidence exists for any formally founded kernel operating at 10^10 actors. The largest formally verified artefacts are single kernels (seL4); the largest operating systems (Internet, payments) are not formally founded. Bridging that gap is open.
- Accountability and obligation (who answers for an action; what an actor owes) are not captured by any kernel in this strand except informally by promise theory; they are presumably covered by the logic/organisation/governance strands.
- Quantitative costs of nested membranes or supervision at depth 5 to 12 (latency, message overhead per level) have not been measured in the literature found.
