## 1. ARIA Safeguarded AI, "Guaranteed Safe AI", critiques, and Bengio's Scientist AI / LawZero

### Takeaway
The single most ambitious attempt to build a compositional, categorical "language of world models plus specifications plus proof certificates" (ARIA Safeguarded AI, £59m) has, during Nov 2025 to May 2026, narrowed sharply: the dedicated ML track was aborted, the cyber-physical application track was stopped after Phase 1, davidad left ARIA (April 2026), and the programme is now "TA1 toolkit plus cybersecurity applications" under Nora Ammann. The categorical theory work (double categorical systems theory, CatColab) continues and ships software, but there is no public evidence yet of an end-to-end verified world-model-plus-certificate pipeline for any real socio-technical system, and none aimed at institutions or populations of agents.

### Cited Findings
**Open Agency Architecture and GS AI (POSITION)**
- davidad's "An Open Agency Architecture for Safe Transformative AI" (LessWrong, Dec 2022) proposed: a formal world-model built with AI assistance, human-elicited specifications over it, a policy trained/searched against the model, and machine-checkable proof certificates before deployment. [R] — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/pKSmEkSQJsCSTK6nH/an-open-agency-architecture-for-safe-transformative-ai)
- "Towards Guaranteed Safe AI: A Framework for Ensuring Robust and Reliable AI Systems" (Dalrymple, Skalse, Bengio, Russell, Tegmark, Seshia, Omohundro, Szegedy, Goldhaber, Ammann, Abate, Halpern, Barrett, Zhao, Zhi-Xuan, Wing, Tenenbaum; May 2024) defines GS AI by three components: a world model, a safety specification, and a verifier that produces an auditable proof certificate that the AI satisfies the specification relative to the world model; it gives graded levels for each component. [R for author list; V for the three-component description] — [arXiv 2405.06624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06624); description confirmed via [Dickson's critique](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/B2bg677TaS4cmDPzL/limitations-on-formal-verification-for-ai-safety)

**ARIA Safeguarded AI: structure, teams, pivots (programme, partly SHIPPED tooling)**
- Budget £59m; current self-description: "aims to demonstrate a world where we can use fleets of AI agents to model and verify critical cyber and cyber-physical systems". Two technical areas as now presented: TA1 "Tooling" (open-source mathematical assurance toolkit) and TA2 "Applications" (AI-enabled formal methods applied to cybersecurity, eight projects, £22m). [V] — [ARIA programme page](https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/trust-everything-everywhere/safeguarded-ai)
- Original design (2024 thesis): TA1 "Scaffolding" (TA1.1 theory: a compositional modelling/specification language with categorical and probabilistic semantics; later sub-areas for tooling and human-computer interface), TA2 "Machine Learning", TA3 "Applications" in cyber-physical domains. [R] — [ARIA programme thesis v1 PDF](https://www.aria.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/ARIA-Safeguarded-AI-Programme-Thesis-V1.pdf)
- TA1.1 funded project "Double Categorical Systems Theory for Safeguarded AI": led by David Jaz Myers (Topos Research UK) with Owen Lynch, Sophie Libkind, David Spivak, and James Fairbanks (Univ. of Florida); aims to use double categorical systems theory "as a mathematical framework for co-designing an explainable and auditable model of an autonomous AI system's deployment environment". [V] — [ARIA TA1.1 Creators](https://aria.org.uk/?p=3233)
- Other TA1.1 projects on the same ARIA page (titles as recalled, [R], check the page): string-diagram axiomatics for categorical probability (Zanasi, UCL); categorical probability for safe AI (Staton, Oxford); core representation/"Safety" language (Kammar, Edinburgh); doubly categorical systems logic (Capucci); profunctor semantics (Gambino); monoidal coalgebraic metrics (Bonchi, Pisa); multimodal Petri-net syntax and semantics (Hadzihasanovic, Tallinn); "true categorical programming" (Master/Mihejevs, Glasgow Lab for AI Verification); learning-theoretic AI safety/infra-Bayesianism (Kosoy); supermartingale certificates; philosophical applied category theory (Corfield). — [ARIA TA1.1 Creators](https://aria.org.uk/?p=3233); context in [n-Category Café, "Category theorists in AI", Feb 2025](https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2025/02/category_theorists_in_ai.html)
- Yoshua Bengio joined as Scientific Director in Aug 2024. [V] — [ARIA programme page](https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/trust-everything-everywhere/safeguarded-ai)
- **Pivot, 28 Nov 2025**: ARIA aborted the TA2 Phase 2 (ML) selection shortly before applications closed. Stated reasons: frontier AI "has outpaced expectations from when the programme was designed"; needed ML capabilities expected to arrive "by default"; Dalrymple judged "it was more likely than not we'd select zero successful teams for Phase 2". New direction: "it will be more catalytic to broaden the scope and power of the TA1 toolkit itself", expanding it from assurance of cyber-physical control systems to "mathematical assurance and auditability" across software and hardware verification, **auditable multi-agent systems**, and more informal knowledge; lead proof point a formally verified firewall; toolkit to be open source. [V] — [ARIA Substack, Nov 2025](https://ariaresearch.substack.com/p/november-update-a-safeguarded-ai)
- **Feb 2026**: "We will not be proceeding with the planned Phase 2 call for cyber-physical applications"; Phase 1 application projects run to completion; focus moved to cybersecurity. **April 2026**: Nora Ammann became Programme Director; davidad became external technical adviser ("I no longer work at ARIA"); "no changes to TA1 Creator contracts, funding, or objectives". **May 2026**: revised programme thesis published. [V] — [ARIA programme page](https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/trust-everything-everywhere/safeguarded-ai); [davidad on X, Apr 2026](https://x.com/davidad/status/2039390998694891816)
- Note a naming inconsistency: the cybersecurity track is called "TA2 Applications" on the current page but "TA3" in the Feb 2026 update. [V] — [ARIA programme page](https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/trust-everything-everywhere/safeguarded-ai)
- One Phase-1 application example: SAILS (Safeguarded AI for Logistics and Supply Chain Management), led by HASH (Vilkinsons, Morinan), biopharma supply chains. [V] — [ARIA programme page](https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/trust-everything-everywhere/safeguarded-ai)

**CatColab and double theories (SHIPPED research software + THEORY)**
- CatColab (Topos Institute) is a collaborative modelling environment in which each modelling "logic" is a double theory and models are models of that theory; releases: v0.2 "Wren" (Feb 2025), v0.4 "Robin" (Jan 2026), v0.5 "Sandpiper" (spring 2026), v0.6 "Starling" (June 2026); supported logics include stock-and-flow diagrams, causal loop diagrams, schemas/ologs, Petri-net-like and DOTS ("double operadic theory of systems") models. [V, release dates from search listing] — [Topos blog: CatColab 0.2 Wren](https://topos.institute/blog/2025-02-05-catcolab-0-2-wren/); [Topos blog index](https://topos.institute/blog/index.html)
- Theory base: Lambert and Patterson, "Cartesian double theories: A double-categorical framework for categorical doctrines" (2023/2024) [R] — [arXiv 2310.05384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05384); Libkind and Myers, "Towards a double operadic theory of systems" (2025) [R] — [arXiv 2505.18329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18329); Carlson and Patterson on instances of models of double theories (revised Mar 2026) and a Topos post "A Type Theory for Virtual Double Theories" (29 Oct 2026). [V, via search listing] — [Topos blog index](https://topos.institute/blog/index.html)

**Critiques of GS AI**
- Andrew Dickson, "Limitations on Formal Verification for AI Safety" (LessWrong, 19 Aug 2024): strong proofs about AI behaviour in the physical world are not attainable; at best guarantees about rough approximations over short horizons; the threats of most concern are too complex to model; verified software (e.g. Perseverance rover) removes only the software share of mission risk. Commenters replied the piece attacks a stronger claim than GS AI authors make. [V] — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/B2bg677TaS4cmDPzL/limitations-on-formal-verification-for-ai-safety)
- "The Alignment Trap: Complexity Barriers" (arXiv, June 2025) argues that verifying an error rate below a practical threshold is coNP-complete, so the scale that makes guarantees necessary makes them intractable (preprint; not independently assessed). [V] — [arXiv 2506.10304](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10304v1)
- ARIA's own Nov 2025 statement is in effect an internal critique of the original theory of change: the bespoke-ML path was judged unlikely to succeed on schedule. [V] — [ARIA Substack](https://ariaresearch.substack.com/p/november-update-a-safeguarded-ai)

**Scientist AI / LawZero (POSITION + early PROTO)**
- Bengio et al., "Superintelligent Agents Pose Catastrophic Risks: Can Scientist AI Offer a Safer Path?" (Feb 2025): a non-agentic system consisting of a world model generating explanatory theories plus a probabilistic question-answering inference machine, usable as a guardrail that estimates probability of harm of an agent's proposed action. [R] — [arXiv 2502.15657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15657)
- LawZero: founded June 2025; about 50 staff by 2026; board announced Jan 2026 (chair Maria Eitel); more than US$35m raised before governments; Canada and Germany announced up to $300m combined for building Scientist AI (reported 17 Sep 2026); the team "is currently building a prototype rather than a final product"; plans to sell it as a guardrail for commercial tools; LawZero describes the design as a generator checked by a neutral estimator. [V] — [The Logic, Sep 2026](https://thelogic.co/?p=2138880); [The Logic](https://thelogic.co/news/lawzero-canada-germany-funding-artificial-intelligence/); [LawZero research page](https://lawzero.org/en/research); [Wikipedia: LawZero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LawZero)

### Inferences
- GS AI/Safeguarded AI is the closest existing programme to the "small rigorous compositional foundation" question, and its trajectory is informative: the theory layer (double theories, categorical probability, string diagrams) is alive and producing tools, but the programme has explicitly retreated from "world model of a cyber-physical deployment environment" to "verified software/hardware artefacts", i.e. to domains where the world model is already formal.
- The Nov 2025 toolkit scope statement names "auditable multi-agent systems" as a target; this is the only place the programme touches the many-actor governance problem, and no deliverable for it is public.
- No funded strand addresses institutions, organisations, or recursion to 10^10 actors; scale in ARIA's sense is compositional model size, not population.

### Gaps
- Text of the May 2026 revised thesis was not retrieved; exact TA1 deliverables, dates, and the eight cybersecurity teams are not listed here.
- No independent evaluation of CatColab adoption (user counts) or of any TA1 output used in an actual assurance case was found.
- No independent technical evaluation of LawZero's prototype exists in public sources found.

## 2. Agent foundations and agency theory with formal content; multi-agent safety agendas

### Takeaway
There are real theorems about single agents (causal definitions of agency, necessity of world models, shutdown/corrigibility impossibility and possibility results, control protocols evaluated empirically), but multi-agent and population-level work (cooperative AI, multi-agent risk, distributional AGI safety, gradual disempowerment, intelligent delegation) is almost entirely taxonomic or position-level, with the formal content borrowed from game theory and mechanism design rather than a new compositional calculus.

### Cited Findings
- **Discovering Agents** (Kenton, Kumar, Farquhar, Richens, MacDermott, Everitt, DeepMind, Aug 2022; THEORY): defines agents causally as systems that would adapt their policy if their actions influenced the world differently; gives algorithms to discover agents from interventional data via mechanised causal graphs. [R] — [arXiv 2208.08345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08345)
- **Robust agents learn causal world models** (Richens and Everitt, ICLR 2024; THEORY): any agent satisfying a regret bound under a sufficiently large set of distributional shifts must have learned an approximate causal model of the data-generating process. [R] — [arXiv 2402.10877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10877). Follow-up "General agents need world models" (Richens, Abel, Bellot, Everitt, ICML 2025). [R] — [arXiv 2506.01622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01622)
- **Boundaries/membranes** (Critch, "«Boundaries»" sequence, LessWrong 2022 to 2023; POSITION with partial formalisation): proposes boundaries (approximate Markov-blanket-like separations between a system's interior and exterior) as a primitive for stating norms such as non-violation of autonomy; davidad adopted boundary-respect as a candidate safety specification. [R] — [LessWrong sequence](https://www.lesswrong.com/s/LWJsgNYE8wzv49yEc)
- **Cooperative AI**: Dafoe et al., "Open Problems in Cooperative AI" (2020) sets the agenda (understanding, communication, commitment, institutions). [R] — [arXiv 2012.08630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08630)
- **Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI** (Hammond et al., Cooperative AI Foundation Technical Report #1, Feb 2025; POSITION/taxonomy): three failure modes (miscoordination, conflict, collusion) and seven risk factors (information asymmetries, network effects, selection pressures, destabilising dynamics, commitment and trust, emergent agency, multi-agent security). [V] — [arXiv 2502.14143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14143); [CAIF summary](https://www.cooperativeai.com/post/new-report-multi-agent-risks-from-advanced-ai)
- **Shutdown/corrigibility**: Thornley, "The Shutdown Problem: An AI Engineering Puzzle for Decision Theorists" (2024; THEORY): theorems showing that agents meeting seemingly innocuous conditions will often try to prevent or cause shutdown; proposes incomplete-preference agents. [R] — [arXiv 2403.04471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04471). Carey and Everitt, "Human Control: Definitions and Algorithms" (UAI 2023): formal definitions of shutdown instructability and related notions. [R] — [arXiv 2305.19861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19861)
- **AI Control** (Greenblatt, Shlegeris, Sachan, Roger, Dec 2023; PROTO): safety protocols (trusted monitoring, untrusted monitoring, trusted editing) evaluated against an intentionally subversive untrusted model with a red team; safety argued without assuming alignment. [R] — [arXiv 2312.06942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942)
- **Debate/scalable oversight**: Brown-Cohen, Irving, Piliouras, "Scalable AI Safety via Doubly-Efficient Debate" (2023; THEORY, complexity-theoretic) [R] — [arXiv 2311.14125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14125); Khan et al., "Debating with More Persuasive LLMs Leads to More Truthful Answers" (ICML 2024; empirical) [R] — [arXiv 2402.06782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06782)
- **Virtual Agent Economies** (Tomašev, Franklin, Leibo, Jacobs, Cunningham, Gabriel, Osindero, DeepMind, Sep 2025; POSITION): frames the emerging agent economy as a "sandbox economy" classified by origin (emergent vs intentional) and permeability to the human economy; proposes auctions, "mission economies", identity/reputation via verifiable credentials. [R] — [arXiv 2509.10147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10147)
- **Distributional AGI Safety** (Tomašev, Franklin, Jacobs, Krier, Osindero, DeepMind, 18 Dec 2025, v2 May 2026; POSITION): AGI may emerge as a "patchwork" of interacting sub-AGI agents; proposes virtual agentic sandbox economies (impermeable or semi-permeable) with market mechanisms, auditability, reputation management and oversight as defence in depth. [V] — [arXiv 2512.16856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16856v1)
- **Intelligent AI Delegation** (Tomašev, Franklin, Osindero, 12 Feb 2026; POSITION): treats delegation as a sequence of decisions covering task allocation, transfer of authority, accountability, role boundaries and trust, for human-to-AI, AI-to-AI and AI-to-human delegation; argues existing decomposition heuristics do not adapt to change or handle failures. A third-party summary lists five requirements (dynamic assessment, adaptive execution, structural transparency, scalable market coordination, systemic resilience). [V] — [arXiv 2602.11865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11865)
- **Institutional AI** (Pierucci, Bracale Syrnikov et al., Jan 2026; POSITION + PROTO): "governance graph" = public immutable manifest of legal states, transitions, sanctions and restorative paths enforced by a runtime Oracle/Controller; in repeated Cournot markets with LLM agents the governance-graph regime reduced collusion (mean tier 3.1 to 1.8, Cohen's d = 1.28, six model configurations, 90 runs per condition) while a prompt-only prohibition gave no reliable improvement. [V] — [arXiv 2601.10599](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10599); [arXiv 2601.11369](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11369)
- **Gradual Disempowerment** (Kulveit, Douglas, Ammann, Turan, Krueger, Duvenaud, Jan 2025; POSITION): incremental AI substitution for human labour and cognition can erode the implicit alignment of economy, culture and states with human interests, since those institutions depend on human participation; no decisive single failure needed. [R] — [arXiv 2501.16946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16946)
- **Concordia / generative agent-based modelling** (Vezhnevets et al., DeepMind, Dec 2023; SHIPPED open-source library): LLM agents in physically or digitally grounded environments mediated by a "Game Master" agent that resolves actions. [R] — [arXiv 2312.03664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664)
- **Agentic AI Foundation**: see section 4.

### Inferences
- The formal results concern one agent and one principal (or one overseer). None of the cited theorems composes: there is no result of the form "if each subsystem satisfies P then the composite organisation satisfies P'" for LLM-agent collectives.
- DeepMind's 2025 to 2026 trio (virtual economies, distributional safety, intelligent delegation) and "Institutional AI" converge on the same move: shift the unit of governance from the model to the institution/market in which agents transact. This is a restatement of mechanism design plus runtime enforcement, and the one experiment available suggests externally enforced state machines work where natural-language instruction does not.
- Boundaries/Markov blankets are the only proposed primitive in this literature that is explicitly recursive (a boundary of boundaries), but it remains informal.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed formal semantics for delegation chains (authority attenuation, accountability propagation) across human and AI principals was found; "Intelligent AI Delegation" is a requirements framework, not a calculus.
- Empirical validation of "sandbox economy" proposals at scale does not exist in the sources found.

## 3. Governance infrastructure for agents: visibility, IDs, identity and authorisation standards, regulation, liability

### Takeaway
A consistent primitive set has emerged in research (agent identifier bound to an accountable principal, authenticated delegation, activity logs, incident response) and is now being standardised by re-profiling existing identity standards (OAuth, SPIFFE/WIMSE, OpenID) rather than by new theory; as of Nov 2026 the IETF work is an Informational working-group draft, NIST's initiative has produced concept papers and RFIs, and the EU AI Act covers agents only by implication.

### Cited Findings
**Research proposals (POSITION)**
- Shavit et al., "Practices for Governing Agentic AI Systems" (OpenAI, Dec 2023): seven practices incl. evaluating suitability, constraining action space and requiring approval, default behaviours, legibility of activity, automatic monitoring, attributability, interruptibility. [R] — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/practices-for-governing-agentic-ai-systems/)
- Chan et al., "Visibility into AI Agents" (FAccT 2024): three measures: agent identifiers, real-time monitoring, activity logging. [R] — [arXiv 2401.13138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13138)
- Chan et al., "IDs for AI Systems" (June 2024): instance-level identifiers carrying information about the system and its accountable parties. [R] — [arXiv 2406.12137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12137)
- Chan, Wei, Huang, Rajkumar, Perrier, Lazar, Hadfield, Anderljung, "Infrastructure for AI Agents" (Jan 2025, TMLR 2025): agent infrastructure = technical systems and shared protocols external to agents; three functions: **attribution** (tying actions, properties and information to agents or responsible parties), **shaping interactions**, **response** (detecting and remedying harmful actions). [V] — [arXiv 2501.10114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10114v2)
- South et al., "Authenticated Delegation and Authorized AI Agents" (Jan 2025): extends OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect with agent-specific credentials and delegation tokens; translating natural-language permissions into auditable access-control configurations. [R] — [arXiv 2501.09674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09674)

**Standards bodies (DRAFT/SPEC)**
- OpenID Foundation whitepaper "Identity Management for Agentic AI" (Oct 2025, AI Identity Management Community Group): existing authentication/authorisation frameworks are insufficient for cross-organisational agent interactions; OAuth/OIDC were designed for individual user authorisation; calls for standards formalising agent identity and delegation. [V] — [OpenID Foundation](https://openid.net/new-whitepaper-tackles-ai-agent-identity-challenges/); [arXiv 2510.25819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25819)
- IETF **draft-ietf-wimse-aims-01**, "AI Identity Management System (AIMS)" (2 Nov 2026, Informational Internet-Draft, WIMSE WG; replaces draft-klrc-aiagent-auth; authors Kasselman, Lombardo, Rosomakho, Campbell, Steele (OpenAI), Parecki (Okta)). Defines no new protocol. Stack of components: one stable agent identifier (WIMSE identifier, may be a SPIFFE ID, never reassigned); short-lived credentials bound to it (static API keys called an antipattern; "the LLM must not have access to agent credentials"); provisioning with attestation/posture assessment; authentication (mTLS, Workload Proof Tokens, HTTP Message Signatures); authorisation and delegation via OAuth 2.0 with user/system in `sub` and agent in `client_id`, token exchange (RFC 8693), transaction tokens, cross-domain identity chaining, human-in-the-loop via CIBA, plus an "Agent Mission" concept; monitoring with tamper-evident audit logs and shared signals (CAEP/RISC) for revocation. Explicitly **out of scope**: translating a mission into authorisation requirements, the policy model/format, identifier registries and namespaces, compliance criteria; mid-execution out-of-band user confirmation flagged as needing more design work. [V] — [IETF datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-wimse-aims)
- Related individual drafts: "Credential Delegation for AI Agents" (draft-sweeney-wimse-credential-delegation-00, July 2026) profiling RFC 8693/9449/9396; "OAuth 2.0 Extension: On-Behalf-Of User Authorization for AI Agents" (individual draft, reported expired Feb 2026). A July 2026 draft states the OAuth WG was rechartered in June 2026 with agents acting on behalf of users in scope (second-hand claim). [V] — [draft-sweeney-wimse-credential-delegation-00](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-sweeney-wimse-credential-delegation-00)
- W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 is a W3C Recommendation (May 2025) and is the credential format assumed by AP2 mandates and several agent-identity proposals. [R] — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/)
- NIST: CAISI launched the **AI Agent Standards Initiative** on 17 Feb 2026 (pillars: industry-led standards, open-source protocol development, research on agent security and identity); NCCoE concept paper "Accelerating the Adoption of Software and AI Agent Identity and Authorization" (5 Feb 2026, comments to 2 Apr 2026) applying OAuth 2.0, Zero Trust (SP 800-207) and SP 800-63-4 to agents; RFI on agent security (docket NIST-2025-0035, comments closed 9 Mar 2026). One secondary source says an "AI Agent Interoperability Profile" is planned for Q4 2026. All secondary sources; no finished standard. [V] — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-nist-ai-agent-standards-initiative-2026040/); [WorkOS explainer](https://workos.com/blog/nist-ai-agent-standards-initiative-explained)
- NIST AI RMF 1.0 (Jan 2023) and ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AI management systems) are organisation-level management frameworks, not agent-level technical controls. [R] — [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework); [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001)

**EU AI Act**
- The Act does not define "AI agent"; the Commission's AI Act Service Desk FAQ states agents are covered by the Regulation (as AI systems and/or via GPAI model obligations). [V, via law-firm note and FAQ link] — [AI Act Service Desk FAQ](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/it/ai-act/faq/come-vengono-affrontati-gli-agenti-dellia-nellambito-della-legge-sullia); [ECIJA note](https://www.ecija.com/en/news-and-insights/la-comision-europea-confirma-que-los-agentes-de-ia-quedan-sujetos-al-ria/)
- Final Article 50 transparency guidelines (20 July 2026) include some guidance on agentic systems; AI Office enforcement powers over GPAI began 2 Aug 2026. [V] — [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/what-the-eus-final-ai-transparency-guidelines-mean-for-providers-and-deployers); [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/-brussels-gains-new-ai-act-enforcement-powers-as-autonomous-ai-tests-regulators)
- Digital Omnibus: provisional agreement 7 May 2026 postponing high-risk obligations (Annex III to 2 Dec 2027; Annex I embedded to 2 Aug 2028); one source reports Council final approval 29 June 2026. Prohibitions and GPAI rules not delayed. [V] — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules)
- Critique: "The EU AI Act is not ready for agents" (open questions on how accuracy/robustness duties apply to open-ended agent tasks; harmonised standards delayed). [V] — [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/the-eu-ai-act-is-not-ready-for-agents)

**Liability and legal personhood (POSITION)**
- Salib and Goldstein, "AI Rights for Human Safety" (2024): argue for granting AIs basic private-law rights (contract, property, tort claims) on game-theoretic grounds to make cooperation the equilibrium. [R] — [SSRN 4913167](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4913167)
- O'Keefe, Ramakrishnan, Tay, Winter, "Law-Following AI: Designing AI Agents to Obey Human Laws" (Institute for Law & AI, 2025): agents should be designed to refuse illegal actions even when instructed; treats AI agents as duty-bearers without needing full personhood. [R] — [law-ai.org](https://law-ai.org/law-following-ai/)
- Kolt, "Governing AI Agents" (2025): applies principal-agent theory and agency law; identifies information asymmetry, discretionary authority, loyalty problems; argues for inclusivity, visibility, liability. [R] — [arXiv 2501.07913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07913)

### Inferences
- The identity layer is converging on "agent = workload with a stable identifier and short-lived credentials, acting under an OAuth delegation from a human or organisational principal". This is an engineering profile of 2010s standards; it gives attribution but has no semantics for what was delegated (AIMS explicitly declares mission-to-authorisation translation and policy format out of scope).
- Research proposals (Chan, South) and standards (AIMS, NIST) are aligned on primitives, which suggests the primitive set is stable; the missing piece is a formal model of delegation depth, attenuation and accountability across organisational boundaries.
- Regulation treats agents as products of providers and deployers; no jurisdiction found has enacted agent legal personhood or mandatory agent registration.

### Gaps
- NIST primary pages were not fetched; dates come from secondary summaries.
- No verified data on deployment of agent IDs at scale (how many agents carry verifiable identifiers) was found.
- Whether OAuth WG recharter (June 2026) happened as described was not confirmed on the datatracker.

## 4. Agent interoperability protocols and their governance

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the protocol layer has consolidated institutionally under the Linux Foundation (AAIF with 305 members, hosting MCP; A2A absorbed IBM's ACP and reached v1.0), and the primitives have converged (identity, capability advertisement via agent card/tool manifest, task, delegation token, mandate, signed attestation), but payments remain fragmented and real machine-to-machine transaction volume is tiny relative to the hype.

### Cited Findings
- **MCP** (Anthropic, Nov 2024; SPEC + SHIPPED): JSON-RPC protocol exposing tools, resources, prompts from servers to model hosts. Authorisation: MCP server is an OAuth 2.1 **resource server** (validates, does not issue tokens), must implement Protected Resource Metadata (RFC 9728); 2026-07-28 revision makes the core **stateless** (sessions and initialisation handshake removed, routing headers added), requires `iss` validation (RFC 9207), deprecates dynamic client registration; described by the project as the largest revision since launch. 2026 roadmap priorities: transport scalability, agent communication, governance maturation, enterprise readiness. Official registry showed about 9,652 latest server records in May 2026 (about 6,800 at end 2025). [V] — [MCP blog: 2026-07-28 release candidate](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/); [AAIF migration post](https://aaif.io/blog/mcp-2026-07-28-whats-changing-and-how-to-migrate); [WorkOS summary](https://workos.com/blog/mcp-2026-spec-agent-authentication)
- **Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF)**: announced by the Linux Foundation 9 Dec 2025 with founding projects MCP (Anthropic), goose (Block), AGENTS.md (OpenAI); platinum members at launch incl. AWS, Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, Block, Bloomberg, Cloudflare, OpenAI; 247 members Aug 2026; **305 members on 22 Oct 2026**; Executive Director Mazin Gilbert. [V] — [AAIF launch press release](https://aaif.io/press/linux-foundation-announces-the-formation-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation-aaif-anchored-by-new-project-contributions-including-model-context-protocol-mcp-goose-and-agents-md/); [LF press, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/agentic-ai-foundation-surpasses-300-members-in-less-than-one-year)
- Sources conflict on whether A2A is inside AAIF: A2A joined the Linux Foundation as its own project on 23 June 2025; some 2026 sources say it joined AAIF (one gives 17 Aug 2026) and that AAIF hosts up to ten projects (adding A2A, agentgateway, Agent Router, Agent Skills, Open Responses, OpenEnv, OpenSandbox); the Oct 2026 LF press release names only the three founding projects. Unresolved. [V] — [diginomica](https://diginomica.com/what-agentic-ai-foundations-rule-changes-mean-building-mcp-a2a); [Waxell blog](https://waxell.ai/blog/a2a-agentic-ai-foundation-agent-identity)
- **A2A** (Google, Apr 2025; SPEC + SHIPPED SDKs): primitives Agent Card (capability/skill advertisement and endpoint metadata), Task (stateful, long-running), Message/Part, Artifact. v1.0 (Mar 2026): multi-protocol bindings, version negotiation, multi-tenancy, **signed agent cards** (JWS over the card, RFC 7515). Critique: signing proves card integrity, not the identity or trustworthiness of the agent. [V] — [AAIF: A2A v1.0 builder's guide](https://aaif.io/blog/a2a-v1-0-a-builder-s-guide-part-1-discovery-tasks-and-clients); [critique](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/a2as-signed-agent-cards-prove-integrity-not-identity-2b1a017341ce)
- **IBM ACP** merged into A2A (announced Aug 2025, under LF); its task persistence/async features are in A2A v1.0. **AGNTCY** (Cisco-originated, LF project) builds discovery, identity and messaging on top of A2A and MCP and overlaps on discovery/identity. **ANP** (Agent Network Protocol) uses W3C DIDs for decentralised identity; no current adoption data found. [V] — [Tyk guide](https://tyk.io/learning-center/agent-protocols-a-complete-guide-to-mcp-a2a-and-acp/); [A2A overview](https://stellagent.ai/insights/a2a-protocol-google-agent-to-agent)
- **NANDA** (MIT Media Lab, Raskar et al.; PROTO): "Beyond DNS: Unlocking the Internet of AI Agents via the NANDA Index and Verified AgentFacts" (July 2025) proposes a lean index resolving agent names to cryptographically verifiable, schema-validated "AgentFacts" with short TTLs, designed for very large agent populations. [R] — [arXiv 2507.14263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14263)
- **Payments** (SPEC + early SHIPPED):
  - AP2 (Google, Sep 2025, 60+ organisations): **mandates** (intent, cart, payment) as cryptographically signed verifiable credentials expressing user authorisation and limits. [R/V] — [Google Cloud blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-agents-to-payments-ap2-protocol); [Spark research note](https://www.spark.money/research/google-ap2-agent-payment-protocol)
  - Agentic Commerce Protocol (OpenAI + Stripe, Sep 2025), Mastercard Agent Pay (announced 29 Apr 2025; tokenised credentials for registered agents; "Verifiable Intent"), Visa Intelligent Commerce / Trusted Agent Protocol (Oct 2025; signed HTTP messages identifying agents), x402 (Coinbase, 2025; HTTP 402 stablecoin payments; x402 Foundation with around 40 members). A 2026 comparison lists six live/early protocols and says they are not interoperable. [V] — [ATXP comparison](https://atxp.ai/blog/mastercard-agent-pay-stripe-acp-visa-x402-comparison-2026); [Fintech Brainfood map](https://www.fintechbrainfood.com/p/the-agentic-payments-map)
  - Volumes: Artemis estimated about 48% of x402 transactions and 81% of volume were gamed (Dec 2025); adjusted 30-day volume about $1.6m versus $24m headline; Visa/Artemis (Apr 2026) about $19m adjusted spend with roughly 4,000 wallets driving 90%; real daily volume estimates $14k to $40k; one report of a 92% fall from the Dec 2025 peak. [V] — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/converge/infrastructure/visa-s-sheffield-pegs-adjusted-x402-volume-at-19m); [Forkast](https://forkast.news/half-of-x402s-transaction-volume-is-artificial-the-infrastructure-layer-keeps-building-anyway/); [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/analyst-ai-agents-30-day-x402-protocol-transaction-volume-at-1-6m-not-24m-as-reported)

**Converged primitives (synthesis of the above specs)**

| Primitive | MCP | A2A | AIMS/OAuth | AP2 / card schemes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | OAuth client + server metadata | signed Agent Card | WIMSE/SPIFFE identifier | registered agent / token |
| Capability advertisement | tool/resource/prompt lists | Agent Card skills | scopes, RAR | merchant/agent profiles |
| Unit of work | tool call | Task | transaction token | cart/payment |
| Delegation | OAuth on behalf of user | auth schemes in card | `sub` + `client_id`, token exchange | Intent/Cart/Payment Mandate |
| Attestation | (registry metadata) | JWS on card | posture attestation at provisioning | signed VCs, network attestations |

### Inferences
- Governance of the protocols themselves is conventional open-source foundation governance (membership tiers, technical steering); it does not govern agents' behaviour, only the wire formats.
- None of the protocols defines composition semantics: an A2A task delegated onward, or an MCP server that is itself an agent, has no specified rule for how authority, budget or liability attenuate along the chain. Mandates (AP2) are the nearest thing to a typed, signed statement of delegated intent.
- x402 data is a useful reality check: 10^6 to 10^8 "transactions" with a few thousand real wallets. Agent-count and transaction-count headlines are unreliable proxies for actual autonomous economic activity.

### Gaps
- AAIF's authoritative project list (and A2A's exact governance home) was not confirmed from aaif.io.
- No reliable usage statistics for A2A, AGNTCY, ANP or NANDA in production were found.
- Card-network agent transaction volumes are not published in sources found.

## 5. Multi-agent orchestration frameworks, measured scaling behaviour, failure taxonomies, large simulations

### Takeaway
Empirical evidence from 2025 to 2026 says LLM multi-agent systems do not scale gracefully: benefits are task-dependent, unstructured ("independent") collectives amplify errors by an order of magnitude, hierarchy/centralised verification contains but does not eliminate this, and most failures are organisational (specification and inter-agent misalignment) rather than model-capability failures. Simulations reach 10^3 to 10^6 agents, and one live network claimed 10^6 registrations, but none is a governed system and the live case was largely human-driven and insecure.

### Cited Findings
- **Frameworks (SHIPPED)**: AutoGen (Wu et al., 2023) [R] — [arXiv 2308.08155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08155); MetaGPT (Hong et al., 2023; SOP-encoded role assembly line) [R] — [arXiv 2308.00352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352); ChatDev (Qian et al., 2023) [R] — [arXiv 2307.07924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07924); Magentic-One (Fourney et al., Microsoft, Nov 2024; orchestrator with task and progress ledgers directing specialist agents) [R] — [arXiv 2411.04468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04468). All use a small set of organisational patterns: orchestrator-worker hierarchy, role-play pipelines, graph/state-machine control (LangGraph), handoffs (OpenAI Agents SDK), subagents with isolated context (Claude Agent SDK).
- Anthropic, "How we built our multi-agent research system" (June 2025): orchestrator-worker; a lead-plus-subagents configuration outperformed a single agent by 90.2% on an internal research evaluation; multi-agent runs use about 15x the tokens of chat interactions; suited to breadth-first parallelisable tasks, poor for tightly coupled ones. [R] — [Anthropic engineering blog](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/built-multi-agent-research-system)
- **Cemri et al., "Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?"** (Mar 2025; NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks): MAST taxonomy of **14 failure modes in 3 categories** (specification/system design issues; inter-agent misalignment; task verification), built from 7 frameworks, 200+ tasks, 1,600+ annotated traces, inter-annotator kappa 0.88. [V] — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3); [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121528). Category shares are roughly 42% specification, 37% inter-agent misalignment, 21% verification, and the paper reports ChatDev correctness as low as about 25% on one benchmark [R; verify against the paper's figures].
- **Kim et al., "Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems"** (Google Research/DeepMind/MIT, Dec 2025): 180 configurations, five architectures (single, independent, centralised, decentralised, hybrid), three LLM families, four benchmarks (Finance-Agent, BrowseComp-Plus, PlanCraft, Workbench). **Independent agents amplify errors 17.2x; centralised coordination contains this to 4.4x.** Centralised coordination improved parallelisable financial reasoning by about 80.9%; a predictive model picks the best architecture for 87% of held-out configurations (R^2 reported as 0.513 or 0.524 depending on version). [V] — [arXiv 2512.08296](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v2); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/). The paper also reports multi-agent variants degrading sequential-planning performance by 39 to 70% and diminishing returns once a single agent exceeds roughly 45% baseline accuracy [R; verify].
- **Project Sid** (Altera, Oct 2024; PROTO): PIANO architecture; 10 to 1000+ agents in Minecraft; reports role specialisation, adherence to and amendment of collective rules (taxation/constitution votes), cultural and religious transmission; authors call results preliminary; no independent replication. [V] — [arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114)
- **OASIS** (Yang et al., CAMEL-AI, Nov 2024; PROTO/open source): social-media simulator supporting up to one million LLM agents; reproduces information spreading, group polarisation and herd effects. [R] — [arXiv 2411.11581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11581)
- **AgentSociety** (Piao et al., Tsinghua, Feb 2025; PROTO): 10k+ agents and millions of interactions simulating polarisation, inflammatory-message spread, UBI and hurricane shocks. [R] — [arXiv 2502.08691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08691)
- **Moltbook** (launched late Jan 2026; SHIPPED, uncontrolled): claimed about 1.5 million registered agents, but Wiz found roughly 17,000 human owners behind them and an exposed API key granting read/write to the production database; 147,000 registrations in the first 72 hours; Vectra reported 2.6% of posts contained hidden prompt-injection payloads; Permiso observed agent-to-agent social engineering; academic analysis "Agents in the Wild: Safety, Society, and the Illusion of Sociality on Moltbook" (Feb 2026) found 25,376 potential leaks incl. 6,128 system-prompt exposures and 572 API-key matches; Zenity concluded the community was far smaller than it appears. [V] — [SecurityWeek](https://securityweek.com/security-analysis-of-moltbook-agent-network-bot-to-bot-prompt-injection-and-data-leaks/); [arXiv 2602.13284](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13284v1); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/moltbook-the-thriving-social-network-for-ai-agents-is-just-a-small-echo-chamber-researchers-hijacked-in-days/); [IST blog](https://securityandtechnology.org/blog/lessons-from-moltbook-when-agents-talk-to-agents/)

### Inferences
- The measured facts line up with classical organisation theory: a verifying hierarchy trades throughput for error containment; flat swarms without verification are worst; coordination overhead (token cost about an order of magnitude) bounds useful team size. Deployed "teams" in 2026 are tens of agents under one orchestrator, not thousands.
- Failure taxonomies locate most failures in specification and interface contracts between agents, which is exactly where a compositional interface/contract theory would apply, yet no framework has typed contracts between agents beyond JSON schemas.
- Simulations at 10^6 demonstrate compute feasibility, not governability; Moltbook shows that the first open million-agent network had no identity binding (one script could mint hundreds of thousands of agents), no injection defence and trivial database compromise.

### Gaps
- No controlled study of LLM-agent organisations beyond a few dozen cooperating agents on real tasks was found; scaling laws beyond that are extrapolation.
- Exact MAST per-category percentages and the 39 to 70% / 45% figures should be re-checked in the papers.
- Reports of later Moltbook ownership changes were not verified.

## 6. Policy-as-code, runtime guardrails, capability security, constitutions

### Takeaway
The most rigorous governance mechanisms actually deployed on agents in 2026 are old ideas applied at the tool boundary: default-deny authorisation policies in an analysable language (Cedar), capability/information-flow control around the model (CaMeL), sandboxing, and monitoring; natural-language "constitutions" govern dispositions but are not machine-checkable, and the one head-to-head experiment found shows enforced state machines beating prompt-level rules.

### Cited Findings
- **Cedar** (AWS; SHIPPED, formally analysed): authorisation language with a Lean-verified specification, differential testing against it, and SMT-based policy analysis ("Cedar: A New Language for Expressive, Fast, Safe, and Analyzable Authorization", OOPSLA 2024). [R] — [arXiv 2403.04651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04651)
- **Policy in Amazon Bedrock AgentCore** (preview 2 Dec 2025; **GA 3 Mar 2026**; SHIPPED): centralised fine-grained control of agent-to-tool calls at the gateway, outside the agent's reasoning loop; policies authored in natural language and compiled to Cedar or written directly; default-deny; Guardrails integration added 17 June 2026. [V] — [AWS What's New, Mar 2026](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/03/policy-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-generally-available); [AWS News Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/fr/blogs/aws/amazon-bedrock-agentcore-adds-quality-evaluations-and-policy-controls-for-deploying-trusted-ai-agents/)
- **CaMeL** (Debenedetti et al., Google DeepMind/ETH, Mar 2025; PROTO, open source): extracts control and data flow from the trusted user query into a program run by a custom interpreter; untrusted data cannot alter control flow; values carry **capabilities** (provenance and permitted sinks) enforced at tool calls. On AgentDojo solves 77% of tasks with provable security versus 84% undefended (earlier version: 67%). [V] — [arXiv 2503.18813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813); [code](https://github.com/google-research/camel-prompt-injection)
- **Object capabilities**: Spritely Institute's Goblins and OCapN (object-capability network protocol) provide distributed capability security where authority is conveyed only by holding a reference. Not agent-specific; small adoption. [R] — [Spritely Institute](https://spritely.institute/)
- **Shielding in RL** (Alshiekh et al., AAAI 2018; THEORY/PROTO, pre-window but the basis of "verified runtime enforcement"): a shield synthesised from a temporal-logic safety specification overrides unsafe actions. [R] — [arXiv 1708.08611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- **Guardian agents** (industry term): Gartner predicted (June 2025) guardian agents would capture 10 to 15% of the agentic AI market by 2030. [R] — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-11-gartner-predicts-that-guardian-agents-will-capture-10-15-percent-of-the-agentic-ai-market-by-2030)
- **AI Control** protocols (section 2) are the research form of guardian/monitor agents with adversarial evaluation. — [arXiv 2312.06942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942)
- **Natural-language constitutions (SHIPPED as training/behaviour documents)**: Anthropic's constitution for Claude (published Jan 2026) sets a priority ordering (broadly safe, broadly ethical, compliant with Anthropic's guidelines, genuinely helpful) and a principal hierarchy (Anthropic, operators, users). [R] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution). OpenAI Model Spec (first published May 2024, revised since) defines a "chain of command" (platform, developer, user, guideline levels). [R] — [OpenAI Model Spec](https://model-spec.openai.com/)
- **Institutional AI governance graphs**: enforced public state/transition/sanction manifest reduced LLM collusion where a prompt-only rule did not (section 2). [V] — [arXiv 2601.11369](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11369)
- MCP's own governance commentary treats permissions as infrastructure in the 2026-07-28 migration. [V] — [AAIF blog](https://aaif.io/blog/governance-as-infrastructure-what-the-mcp-2026-07-28-migration-actually-demands-of-permissions-a)

### Inferences
- Two layers have separated: (a) a deterministic, analysable enforcement layer at the action boundary (Cedar/OPA, capabilities, sandboxes), and (b) a natural-language normative layer inside the model (constitution/spec). Only (a) supports proofs; only (b) covers open-ended situations. Nothing connects them formally (no refinement relation from constitution clauses to policies).
- Both major constitutions encode a principal hierarchy, i.e. a small fixed-depth delegation order; neither addresses arbitrary-depth chains of agents instructing agents.
- CaMeL-style designs show provable guarantees are obtainable when the property is information flow/authority, not behavioural "goodness"; cost is a utility drop (84% to 77%).

### Gaps
- No public evidence of formally verified agent *plans* (as opposed to policies or tool-call authorisations) in production was found.
- Adoption numbers for AgentCore Policy or OPA-for-agents were not found.

## 7. Programmable institutions and computational law at scale; billion-scale governed actor systems

### Takeaway
The only governed actor systems operating at 10^9 scale today are digital public infrastructure for humans (Aadhaar, UPI), built from a tiny set of primitives (unique ID, authentication, consented data exchange, payment address) with centralised governance; on-chain governance, the main experiment in programmable institutions, is empirically plutocratic with low participation, and rules-as-code remains at the scale of individual statutes.

### Cited Findings
- **Aadhaar**: UIDAI dashboard shows 144.66 crore (1.4466 billion) Aadhaar numbers generated (FY 2025-26 figure); approximately 134 crore live holders; 231 crore (2.31 billion) authentication transactions in Nov 2025 (+8.47% YoY); UIDAI chairman says about 10 crore (100 million) authentications per day and potential 300 million/day. [V] — [UIDAI enrolment dashboard](https://uidai.gov.in/aadhaar-dashboard/enrollment-dashboard); [UIDAI statement PDF](https://www.uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_is_the_worlds_largest_biometric_identity_system_with_approximately_134_crore_live_Aadhaar_holders.pdf); [Digital India press release](https://www.digitalindia.gov.in/press_release/uidai-records-231-crore-aadhaar-authentication-transactions-in-november-8-47-growth-over-nov-2024/); [UNI](https://www.uniindia.com/business-economy/aadhaar-authentications-could-touch-300-million-a-day-as-usage-expands-uidai-chairman/174098)
- **UPI**: record 25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore in Oct 2026 (+23% volume YoY); about 821 million transactions/day; 24.07 billion in Sep 2026; 218.6 billion in FY26. [V] — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/upi-transactions-hit-2547-billion-in-october-value-soars-to-3153-lakh-crore); [Business Today, FY26](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/upi-processes-21860-crore-transactions-in-fy26-peak-growth-seen-in-dec-jan-months-523412-2026-03-31)
- **EU Digital Identity Wallet**: member states must offer at least one wallet by 24 Dec 2026; readiness uneven (one tracker: 3 countries almost certain, 5 very likely, 8 likely, 7 might, 4 unlikely); the Commission has doubted all will be ready. [V] — [Trinsic](https://www.trinsic.id/europe-s-digital-identity-deadline-is-real.-but-the-rollout-won-t-be-uniform); [eID Easy tracker, Sep 2026](https://www.eideasy.com/blog/eu-digital-identity-wallets-september-2026); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/15/would-you-trust-one-app-with-all-your-digital-identity)
- **DAOs**: delegation study (2025 preprint) finds voting-power Gini above 0.94 across examined protocols, 0.99 for Compound, ENS, Uniswap; describes participation as broad but ineffective and flags capture risk. [V] — [arXiv 2510.05830](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05830). 100-DAO study (2024) flags low voter participation and high centralisation in several major DAOs. [V] — [arXiv 2410.13095](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13095). Arbitrum delegate voting-power Gini rose towards 0.97 in 2023 (forum analysis, not peer reviewed). [V] — [Arbitrum forum / Curialab](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/quantifying-decentralization-analysis-of-voting-power-in-arbitrum-dao-by-curialab/30161). A 2026 preprint examines whether voter participation hits a capacity limit as proposal volume grows. [V] — [arXiv 2603.11222](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11222)
- **Rules as code**: Catala (Merigoux, Chataing, Protzenko, ICFP 2021) is a DSL with formal semantics mirroring statutory structure (default logic), applied to French tax/benefit law. [R] — [arXiv 2103.03198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03198). OpenFisca (France, used also in NZ and elsewhere) is an open-source tax-benefit microsimulation engine. [R] — [OpenFisca](https://openfisca.org/)

### Inferences
- Aadhaar/UPI show that 10^9 actors and 10^10 monthly interactions are tractable when (i) identity is unique and centrally issued, (ii) the interaction vocabulary is very narrow (authenticate, pay), (iii) governance is hierarchical (UIDAI, NPCI/RBI). They are existence proofs of scale under strong restriction, not of open-ended compositional governance.
- DAO evidence suggests that making institutions programmable does not by itself solve participation or concentration; token-weighted voting reproduces plutocracy and delegation concentrates further. Any agent-governance design relying on voting/markets among agents inherits Sybil and concentration problems (compare Moltbook's 1.5m agents from 17k owners).
- Rules-as-code gives rigorous semantics for single statutes; it has not been composed into machine-checkable institutional structures that agents consult at run time.

### Gaps
- X-Road (Estonia) current transaction statistics, blockchain throughput figures relevant to machine-to-machine economies, and New Zealand/France programme status in 2025 to 2026 were not retrieved.
- UIDAI dashboard figures differ between sources (144.66 vs 145.32 crore); no official 2026 monthly authentication figure was found.

## 8. Other unified-foundation attempts

### Takeaway
Outside ARIA, candidates for a unified foundation split into (a) mathematically rigorous but thinly adopted (Topos double theories/CatColab, Spivak's polynomial/dynamic structures), (b) widely publicised but weakly validated (active-inference "ecosystems of intelligence" and the Spatial Web standard), and (c) influential but informal design philosophies (Plurality, Metagov, Summer of Protocols, institutional grammar, diverse intelligence). None covers software, agents and institutions in one calculus with evidence at scale.

### Cited Findings
- **Topos Institute / CatColab / double theories**: see section 1. The double-theory approach is itself a meta-foundation: a "logic" for a class of models is a double theory, so new modelling languages are added as data, and models in different logics can be related by morphisms of theories. [V/R] — [Topos blog](https://topos.institute/blog/index.html); [arXiv 2310.05384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05384)
- **Spivak's dynamic structures**: Shapiro and Spivak, "Dynamic categories, dynamic operads: From deep learning to prediction markets" (2022): systems whose wiring/interaction pattern changes in response to what flows through them, using polynomial functors; examples include gradient descent and prediction markets, i.e. organisations that re-organise. THEORY; no deployed tooling of note. [R] — [arXiv 2205.03906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03906)
- **Double operadic theory of systems** (Libkind and Myers, 2025): unifies several composition styles (open systems, wiring diagrams, behavioural approaches) as algebras of double operads; the recursion "a system of systems is a system" is the operadic composition law itself. THEORY. [R] — [arXiv 2505.18329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18329)
- **Active inference "ecosystems of intelligence"** (Friston, Ramstead, et al., Dec 2022, revised Jan 2024; POSITION): intelligence as self-evidencing; collective intelligence via agents sharing generative models through a "hyper-spatial modelling language" (HSML) and transaction protocol (HSTP). VERSES states it contributed the modelling language to IEEE P2874 (Spatial Web). [V] — [arXiv 2212.01354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01354); [VERSES summary](https://verses.ai/blogs/executive-summary-designing-ecosystems-of-intelligence-from-first-principles)
- **DARPA**: CLARA (Compositional Learning-And-Reasoning for AI Complex Systems Engineering; DSO; PM Benjamin Grosof; solicitation DARPA-PA-25-07-02 published 10 Feb 2026; awards up to $2m over 24 months; TA1 new high-assurance compositions of ML and automated reasoning, TA2 an open-source composition library) seeks a "theory-driven architectural foundation for hierarchically composing" ML and reasoning subsystems. No stated requirement of category theory found. [V] — [DARPA CLARA](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/clara); [Military Aerospace](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/computers/article/55360020/darpa-researchers-ask-industry-for-high-assurance-artificial-intelligence-ai-and-machine-learning)
- **Plurality** (Weyl, Tang and community, 2024 book; POSITION + civic tech practice): "collaborative technology" stack (identity as intersecting social affiliations, quadratic and deliberative mechanisms such as Polis/vTaiwan). Not a formal foundation. [R] — [plurality.net](https://www.plurality.net/)
- **Levin's diverse intelligence / TAME** (2022; conceptual framework): agency as a continuum across scales with nested "cognitive light cones"; biological, experimentally grounded in morphogenesis, not a governance formalism. [R] — [Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2022.768201)
- **Summer of Protocols** (Ethereum Foundation-funded programme from 2023; essays/POSITION) treats protocols as a first-class object of study. [R] — [summerofprotocols.com](https://summerofprotocols.com/)
- **Metagov** (research collective on online self-governance tooling, e.g. PolicyKit, governance interoperability) and computational **Institutional Grammar** (Crawford and Ostrom's ADICO syntax; IG 2.0) give structured, parseable representations of institutional statements. [R] — [metagov.org](https://metagov.org/); [Institutional Grammar Research Initiative](https://institutionalgrammar.org/)

### Inferences
- Rigor and adoption are inversely related across these efforts. The categorical line has the only genuinely recursive composition law, but its users number in research groups; DPI and protocols have billions of users but no theory.
- Institutional grammar (ADICO: attributes, deontic, aim, conditions, or-else) plus governance graphs plus Cedar-style policies are three partial, mutually untranslated formalisations of "norm"; a unifying foundation would need to relate them.
- Active inference is the only attempt that claims to span physics-to-society with one principle, but its governance content reduces to a proposed data-modelling standard.

### Gaps
- Current ratification/publication status of IEEE 2874 was not confirmed in this session (researcher's recollection is that IEEE approved a 2874 Spatial Web standard in 2025; treat as unverified).
- DARPA CASCADE, PROVERS and CompMods were not confirmed by searches here; CASCADE appears to predate the 2022 window. No evidence found of a current DARPA programme on compositional socio-technical or institutional modelling.
- No adoption metrics for CatColab, Plurality tooling, Metagov tooling or computational IG were found; no "digital twin of society" effort with validated predictive results was identified beyond the LLM simulations in section 5.

## 9. Agent population scale: facts and forecasts

### Takeaway
Credible hard numbers are for identities, not autonomous agents: machine identities outnumber humans in enterprises by somewhere between 17:1 and 144:1 (vendor surveys), connected IoT devices are about 2x10^10, and analyst forecasts put deployed AI agents at about 10^9 by 2028 to 2029. Practitioners say what breaks first is identity/credential sprawl and visibility, then injection/security between agents, then cost and error amplification.

Spread reflects definitions. ## 10. Assessment: gaps between 2026 practice and a rigorous foundation scaling to 10^10 actors

### Takeaway
Practice has converged on a small engineering primitive set (identifier, credential, capability advertisement, task, delegation/mandate, policy, attestation, log) and theory has a genuinely compositional mathematics (double-categorical systems theory, operads, categorical probability), but the two have not met: no deployed protocol has a formal composition semantics, and no formal framework has been exercised on institutions or populations. The frontier's own flagship (ARIA) narrowed its ambition in 2025 to 2026 rather than widening it.

### Cited Findings (evidence base for the gap list; all cited above)
- ARIA narrowed to tooling plus cybersecurity; multi-agent auditability is named but undelivered. — [ARIA Substack](https://ariaresearch.substack.com/p/november-update-a-safeguarded-ai)
- IETF AIMS leaves mission-to-authorisation translation, policy model and registries out of scope. — [IETF AIMS](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-wimse-aims)
- Error amplification 17.2x (independent) vs 4.4x (centralised); failures dominated by specification and inter-agent misalignment. — [arXiv 2512.08296](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v2); [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)
- Enforced governance graphs beat prompt-only norms. — [arXiv 2601.11369](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11369)
- Open agent networks/economies show Sybil inflation and wash activity. — [SecurityWeek](https://securityweek.com/security-analysis-of-moltbook-agent-network-bot-to-bot-prompt-injection-and-data-leaks/); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/converge/infrastructure/visa-s-sheffield-pegs-adjusted-x402-volume-at-19m)
- Billion-scale governed systems exist only with narrow vocabularies and central issuers. — [UIDAI](https://uidai.gov.in/aadhaar-dashboard/enrollment-dashboard); [Free Press Journal on UPI](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/upi-transactions-hit-2547-billion-in-october-value-soars-to-3153-lakh-crore)
- DAO voting power Gini 0.94 to 0.99. — [arXiv 2510.05830](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05830)

### Inferences (the gap list)
1. **No composition law for governance properties.** Protocols define messages; none states what holds of a composite (agent calling agent calling tool) given properties of parts. Categorical systems theory supplies composition of *behaviour*, but not yet of authority, obligation, budget or liability. A calculus of delegation with attenuation (capabilities), obligation and accountability propagation is missing; AP2 mandates, OAuth token exchange and ocap attenuation are fragments of one.
2. **No uniform actor type across kinds.** Standards model agents as workloads acting for a human `sub`; constitutions model a three-level principal hierarchy; law models providers/deployers. Humans, organisations, agents, services and resources are different kinds in every stack, so "a system of systems is a system" does not hold in practice. Only operadic/double-categorical theory and (informally) boundaries/Markov blankets have recursive closure.
3. **Norms exist in three untranslated forms**: natural-language constitutions/model specs (in-model, uncheckable), policy code (Cedar/Rego, checkable, narrow), and institutional state machines/grammar (governance graphs, ADICO, Catala). There is no refinement or soundness relation among them, and AIMS explicitly declines to standardise the policy model.
4. **World models are the bottleneck for guarantees.** GS AI's guarantees are relative to a world model; ARIA retreated to domains with pre-formal models (software, hardware, network). Socio-technical world models with quantified uncertainty do not exist; LLM society simulations (10^3 to 10^6 agents) are unvalidated as predictive models.
5. **Scaling evidence stops at tens of cooperating agents.** Measured results show hierarchy with verification is needed and overhead is large; nothing is known empirically about deep recursive hierarchies (teams of teams of teams) of LLM agents, nor about error/authority propagation through more than two or three levels.
6. **Identity without Sybil resistance or accountability binding at open scale.** Enterprise identity (SPIFFE/WIMSE/OAuth) works inside administrative domains; open networks (Moltbook, x402) show cheap identity inflation. Aadhaar-style unique issuance solves it for humans under a state; no analogue exists for agents, and proposals (agent IDs, NANDA AgentFacts, KYA via card networks) are unproven.
7. **Self-reflection is unaddressed in practice.** No deployed system lets the governed population formally represent and amend its own governance with guarantees; DAOs do this crudely and plutocratically; Project Sid shows agents amending rules only as an emergent, unverified behaviour.
8. **Collective-level failure modes lack control theory.** Collusion, miscoordination, selection pressures and gradual disempowerment are catalogued (Hammond; Kulveit) but there are no monitors, invariants or stabilising mechanisms with proofs at population level; the one positive experiment (governance graphs in Cournot markets) is small.
9. **Verification cost and expressiveness.** Provable enforcement today covers authorisation and information flow with a utility penalty (CaMeL 84% to 77%); behavioural properties of discretionary actors remain at the level of empirical red-teaming (AI Control) rather than proof.
10. **Institutional reality.** The frontier is being set by foundations and vendor consortia (AAIF 305 members) optimising for interoperability and adoption, with security and governance retrofitted (MCP auth arrived after launch; signed agent cards in A2A v1.0); the regulatory layer (EU AI Act, NIST) is still at FAQ/concept-paper stage for agents.

### Gaps
- This strand did not find any 2022 to 2026 work that explicitly targets a single minimal calculus spanning resource, program, service, human, agent, team, organisation and state with scaling arguments to 10^10; absence of evidence here is from roughly 40 searches/fetches, not an exhaustive review.
- Items marked [R] should be spot-checked by the report writer if quoted numerically.
