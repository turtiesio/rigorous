## 1. Fixed points and recursion: what makes "fractal / self-similar / self-referential" rigorous

### Takeaway
There is one diagonal/fixed-point core (Lawvere 1969) behind Cantor, Russell, Gödel, Tarski and Turing, and it cuts both ways: wherever a system can represent all of its own maps into a type with a fixed-point-free operation (negation), paradox or incompleteness follows; wherever self-representation is demanded, every operation must have a fixed point (so totality, negation, or classical truth has to give). The cleanest rigorous meaning of "the whole has the same type as its parts" is not one thing but three: (i) closure under composition (operads / monoidal categories: a composite of systems is a system), (ii) a structural isomorphism X ≅ F(X) (Lambek's lemma; initial algebra for finite well-founded nesting, final coalgebra for unbounded/behavioural nesting, Leinster for geometric fractals), and (iii) self-containment D ≅ [D → D] or a universe containing codes for itself, which is only consistent with a restriction (continuity, guardedness, or stratification).

### Cited Findings

**Lawvere and the diagonal core**
- Lawvere's fixed-point theorem (1969, "Diagonal arguments and cartesian closed categories"): in a cartesian closed category, if there is a point-surjective morphism φ: A → Y^A, then every endomorphism f: Y → Y has a fixed point (a point y: 1 → Y with f∘y = y). Contrapositive: if Y has a fixed-point-free endomap (e.g. negation on truth values), no A can enumerate all maps A → Y. Cantor's theorem is the case Y = 2. — [Lawvere, TAC Reprint 15](http://www.tac.mta.ca/tac/reprints/articles/15/tr15abs.html); [nLab: Lawvere's fixed point theorem](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Lawvere%27s+fixed+point+theorem)
- Yanofsky (2003, Bull. Symbolic Logic 9(3):362–386) restates Lawvere in sets and functions and shows that "many self-referential paradoxes, incompleteness theorems and fixed point theorems fall out of the same simple scheme" (Cantor, Russell, Grelling, liar, Richard, Turing's halting problem, Gödel's first theorem, Tarski, Löb's paradox, Kleene's recursion theorem, Rice's theorem, and others). His reading: there are "really no paradoxes", only limitations; violating a limitation gives an inconsistent system. **[checked]** — [Yanofsky, arXiv:math/0305282](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305282)
- Caveat on overstatement: Lawvere/Yanofsky is a common scheme, not a single proof that yields each theorem for free. Gödel's theorems additionally need arithmetisation of syntax and the derivability conditions; Turing needs a universal machine. — [SEP: Gödel's incompleteness theorems](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/); [SEP: Self-reference](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-reference/)

**Kleene, quines, Y**
- Kleene's second recursion theorem (1938): for every total computable f there is an index e with φ_e = φ_{f(e)}; equivalently for every partial computable g(x, y) there is e with φ_e(y) = g(e, y). Consequence: programs can obtain and use their own source (quines; self-reproducing and self-inspecting programs are always constructible in any acceptable programming system). The first recursion theorem gives computable least fixed points of recursive operators. — [Wikipedia: Kleene's recursion theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleene%27s_recursion_theorem)
- Untyped λ-calculus has fixed-point combinators (Y = λf.(λx.f(xx))(λx.f(xx))), so every term has a fixed point; by Lawvere's contrapositive this is incompatible with a fixed-point-free "negation" on the same terms, which is exactly Curry's paradox when untyped λ-calculus is used as a logic. Simply typed and other strongly normalising calculi have no Y: totality and unrestricted self-application exclude one another. — [Wikipedia: Fixed-point combinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-point_combinator); [SEP: Curry's paradox](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/curry-paradox/)

**Domain theory and recursive domain equations**
- Scott (1969–72) built D∞ with D ≅ [D → D], where [D → D] is the space of Scott-continuous functions; a set-theoretic solution with all functions is impossible by Cantor, so reflexivity is paid for by restricting to continuous (computably approximable) maps and admitting a bottom/divergence element. — [Wikipedia: Domain theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_theory); [Wikipedia: Scott domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_domain)
- Smyth and Plotkin (1982) give the general category-theoretic solution of recursive domain equations X ≅ F(X), including mixed-variance F, via embedding–projection pairs and the limit–colimit coincidence. — [Smyth & Plotkin, SIAM J. Comput. 11(4)](https://doi.org/10.1137/0211062)

**Initial algebras, final coalgebras, Lambek**
- Lambek's lemma (1968): the structure map of an initial F-algebra F(μF) → μF is an isomorphism; dually the final coalgebra νF → F(νF) is an isomorphism. So both are canonical solutions of X ≅ F(X): the initial algebra carries induction/recursion (finite, well-founded structures), the final coalgebra carries coinduction/corecursion (possibly infinite behaviours, equality = bisimilarity). — [nLab: initial algebra of an endofunctor](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/initial+algebra+of+an+endofunctor)
- Rutten (2000), "Universal coalgebra: a theory of systems": state-based systems (automata, transition systems, streams, objects) are coalgebras X → F(X); the final coalgebra is the universe of behaviours; bisimulation is the canonical equivalence. — [Rutten, TCS 249](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00056-6)
- Limit: final coalgebras do not exist for every functor. A final coalgebra for the full powerset functor on Set would give X ≅ P(X), contradicting Cantor (via Lambek); bounded/accessible functors (e.g. finite powerset) are fine. — [nLab: terminal coalgebra for an endofunctor](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/terminal+coalgebra+for+an+endofunctor)
- Leinster, "A general theory of self-similarity" (2004 preprints; Advances in Mathematics 2011): a self-similar space is defined by a system of simultaneous equations in which each space is a gluing of copies of the others; the "universal solution" is a terminal coalgebra; he gives an explicit necessary and sufficient condition for existence and an explicit construction. Freyd's characterisation of [0,1] as the universal space made of two copies of itself glued end to end is the motivating example; the theory is described as a categorification of simultaneous linear equations. **[checked]** — [Leinster, arXiv:1010.4474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4474); [part I, arXiv:math/0411344](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411344); [part II, arXiv:math/0411345](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411345)

**Non-well-founded sets**
- Aczel (1988): replace Foundation by the Anti-Foundation Axiom (every graph has a unique decoration). Then sets like Ω = {Ω} exist, set equality is bisimilarity, and the universe is a final coalgebra of the (class) powerset functor. AFA is consistent relative to ZFC without Foundation. Circular membership is therefore harmless; what stays impossible is unrestricted comprehension (Russell). — [SEP: Non-wellfounded set theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nonwellfounded-set-theory/)

**Guarded recursion**
- Nakano (2000) introduced a "later" modality ▸ with next: A → ▸A and a fixed-point operator fix: (▸A → A) → A; guarded recursive definitions have unique fixed points and guarded recursive types solve even negative-occurrence equations as long as the recursion is under ▸. Birkedal, Møgelberg, Schwinghammer and Støvring (2012) model this in the topos of trees ("synthetic guarded domain theory"). The typing of fix is formally Löb's rule, so the same shape that blocks self-trust in provability logic licenses productive self-reference when one "time step" is consumed per unfolding. — [Birkedal et al., arXiv:1208.3596](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.3596); [Jung et al., "Iris from the ground up", JFP 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956796818000151)

**Universes, Girard, induction-recursion**
- Girard's paradox (1972): type theory with Type : Type (and Girard's System U) is inconsistent, i.e. every type is inhabited; Hurkens (1995) gave a short derivation. The remedy is a predicative hierarchy of universes U_0 : U_1 : U_2 : … — [Wikipedia: System U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_U); [nLab: Burali-Forti's paradox](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Burali-Forti%27s+paradox)
- Induction-recursion (Dybjer 2000; Dybjer–Setzer): simultaneously define a type U of codes inductively and a decoding function T: U → Type recursively. This is the rigorous way a system can contain an internal universe of descriptions of (smaller) systems of its own kind, closed under chosen constructors, without Type : Type. — [Wikipedia: Induction-recursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induction-recursion)

**Operads and "algebras of algebras"**
- An operad axiomatises many-input one-output operations closed under substitution (tree grafting); an algebra of an operad interprets every wiring pattern, so any composite of things of the kind is again a thing of the kind. Spivak's operad of wiring diagrams formalises "plug-and-play" nesting of boxes inside boxes. — [Spivak, arXiv:1305.0297](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0297); [Leinster, Higher Operads, Higher Categories, arXiv:math/0305049](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305049)
- Baez–Dolan microcosm principle: certain algebraic structures can be defined in any category equipped with a categorified version of the same structure (monoids live in monoidal categories, which are themselves pseudomonoids in Cat). This is the precise sense in which the theory of a structure has the shape of the structure one level up. — [nLab: microcosm principle](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/microcosm+principle)

### Inferences
- "Fractal" should be disambiguated in any candidate foundation into three separately checkable claims:
  1. Closure: Sys is closed under composition (operad algebra / monoidal category). This is cheap, consistent, and is what "a system of systems is a system" needs. It says nothing about properties being preserved.
  2. Self-similarity: Sys ≅ F(Sys) for a specified functor F (e.g. "a system is a governance layer plus a finite family of systems"). Initial algebra if nesting depth is finite and the bottom is atomic (supports induction, hence proofs by structural recursion and termination of audits); final coalgebra if systems are open-ended processes identified by behaviour (supports only coinductive, bisimulation-style reasoning; safety yes, termination no). For a governed hierarchy with finite depth the initial algebra is the cleaner choice; for ongoing interaction the final coalgebra is. Lambek's lemma is the exact statement "the whole has the same type as its parts".
  3. Reflexivity: Sys contains (codes for) Sys → Sys or for Sys itself. Lawvere says this forces every endo-operation on the "verdict" type to have a fixed point. The known consistent options are: partiality (Scott, bottom), time-indexing (guarded recursion, "you may consult yourself only as of the previous step"), stratification (universe levels, induction-recursion), or non-classical truth. Each maps to a governance design: undefined verdicts, delayed self-application, meta-level hierarchy, or graded/probabilistic verdicts.
- Kleene's theorem is the positive half: self-description and self-reproduction are always available in a universal system. The negative half (Rice, Gödel, Löb) is about self-*verification*, not self-*reference*.
- Self-similarity gives uniformity of description, not uniformity of behaviour: Leinster's universal solutions include very wild compact spaces, and the same F at every level does not imply the same properties at every level (see section 4).

### Gaps
- I did not find (and do not know of) a published theorem that singles out one of these as "the" formalisation of recursive systems-of-systems governance; the choice among initial algebra, final coalgebra and operad algebra is a modelling decision. The category-theory strand should confirm how operad/polynomial-functor frameworks treat reflexivity specifically.
- Leinster part II's exact recognition theorem (which spaces arise as universal solutions) was not re-read; I recall it as covering a very broad class of compact metrisable spaces but did not verify the statement.

## 2. Reflection: what a system can soundly know, verify and trust about itself and its successors

### Takeaway
A consistent, effectively axiomatised system strong enough for arithmetic cannot prove its own soundness or consistency (Gödel II, Löb) or define its own truth (Tarski); it can verify a strictly weaker system, or assume more via reflection principles, generating an open-ended ordinal-indexed hierarchy that cannot be effectively completed (Turing 1939, Feferman 1962). Twenty years of agent-foundations work (tiling agents, logical induction, reflective oracles, Payor's lemma) has produced real partial results — probabilistic self-trust in the limit, fixed-point cooperation, consistent reflective oracles — but no accepted, practical construction of an agent or institution that proves the safety of unboundedly many self-modifications or successors at undiminished strength; practice (e.g. Darwin Gödel Machine, 2025) has replaced proof with empirical validation.

### Cited Findings

**Classical limits**
- Gödel I (with Rosser): every consistent, recursively axiomatisable theory interpreting Robinson arithmetic is incomplete. Gödel II: such a theory satisfying the Hilbert–Bernays–Löb derivability conditions (e.g. extending PA) does not prove its own standard consistency statement. — [SEP: Gödel's incompleteness theorems](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/)
- Tarski's undefinability theorem: the set of true sentences of arithmetic is not arithmetically definable; no sufficiently expressive classical language contains a truth predicate for itself satisfying all T-biconditionals. Truth for level n is definable at level n+1. — [SEP: Tarski's truth definitions](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/tarski-truth/)
- Löb's theorem (1955): if T ⊢ Prov_T(⌜P⌝) → P then T ⊢ P. So T can assert "my proofs of P are correct" only for P it already proves; a blanket self-soundness schema makes T inconsistent. Provability logic GL axiomatises exactly this. — [SEP: Provability logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-provability/)
- Willard (2001): there are consistent "self-verifying" arithmetics that prove (a form of) their own consistency, at the cost of being too weak to prove that multiplication (in some systems, even addition-based growth) is total. Gödel II is evaded only below a sharp strength threshold. — [Wikipedia: Self-verifying theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-verifying_theories)

**Reflection principles, progressions, ordinals**
- Turing's "Systems of logic based on ordinals" (1939) iterates T ↦ T + Con(T) along ordinal notations; every true Π1 sentence is provable at some notation for a small ordinal, but which notation to use is itself non-effective. Feferman (1962) showed progressions based on uniform reflection prove every true arithmetical sentence along suitable paths through Kleene's O; the incompleteness is thereby moved into the (non-computable) choice of path, not removed. — [Wikipedia: Systems of Logic Based on Ordinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_of_Logic_Based_on_Ordinals); [Feferman, JSL 27(3), 1962](https://doi.org/10.2307/2964544)
- Proof-theoretic ordinals measure how much well-founded recursion a theory can certify: PA has ordinal ε0 (Gentzen 1936); stronger theories have larger ordinals; a theory cannot prove well-foundedness of its own ordinal. Iterated reflection and ordinal analysis are closely tied (Schmerl; Beklemishev). — [SEP: Proof theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/proof-theory/); [Beklemishev, APAL 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apal.2003.11.030)

**Reflective towers in programming languages**
- Smith's 3-Lisp (POPL 1984) defines a language by an infinite tower of meta-circular interpreters with reify/reflect operations; Wand and Friedman ("The mystery of the tower revealed", 1986/88) gave a non-reflective semantics showing the infinite tower is finitely implementable (levels are created lazily). Amin and Rompf (POPL 2018) show towers of interpreters can be collapsed by staging to remove interpretive overhead. — [Smith, POPL 1984](https://doi.org/10.1145/800017.800513); [Amin & Rompf, POPL 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3158140)
- Cost of full reflection: Wand (1998), "The theory of fexprs is trivial" — in a language where programs can inspect arbitrary operand syntax, contextual equivalence collapses to syntactic identity, so no non-trivial program transformation or modular reasoning is sound. — [Wikipedia: Fexpr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fexpr)

**MIRI / agent foundations**
- Löbian obstacle (Yudkowsky & Herreshoff 2013): an agent that acts only on proofs in theory T cannot, by Löb, approve a successor that also reasons in T on the grounds "whatever the successor proves safe is safe"; naive trust chains need strictly decreasing strength (T ⊇ T' + Con(T') …). Proposed workarounds (parametric polymorphism, descending-soundness chains) each have defects. — [Tiling Agents for Self-Modifying AI, and the Löbian Obstacle](https://intelligence.org/files/TilingAgentsDraft.pdf)
- Vingean reflection (Fallenstein & Soares 2015): a predecessor cannot predict a smarter successor's exact actions, so it must reason abstractly about the successor's reasoning; existing formal tools do this only in toy settings. — [Vingean Reflection: Reliable Reasoning for Self-Improving Agents](https://intelligence.org/files/VingeanReflection.pdf)
- Christiano, Yudkowsky, Herreshoff, Barasz (2013): a probabilistic reflection principle (a < P(φ) < b implies P(a < P(⌜φ⌝) < b) = 1) is consistent, evading Tarski by allowing infinitesimal imprecision about one's own probabilities. — [Definability of Truth in Probabilistic Logic](https://intelligence.org/files/DefinabilityTruthDraft.pdf)
- Logical induction (Garrabrant, Benson-Tilsen, Critch, Soares, Taylor 2016): there is a computable algorithm whose beliefs about logical sentences cannot be exploited by any polynomial-time trader; consequences include limit coherence, calibration, and a form of trust in its own future beliefs. The algorithm is computable but wildly impractical, and the guarantees are asymptotic. — [arXiv:1609.03543](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03543)
- Reflective oracles (Fallenstein, Taylor, Christiano 2015): probabilistic oracle machines may query an oracle about machines with access to the same oracle; a consistent such oracle exists by a fixed-point argument when it may randomise on the boundary, and yields Nash equilibria among mutually modelling agents. Leike, Taylor and Fallenstein (2016) show reflective oracles can be limit-computable and give a formal solution to the "grain of truth" problem. — [arXiv:1508.04145](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04145); [arXiv:1609.05058](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05058)
- Löbian cooperation: Bárász et al. (2014) show "FairBot" (cooperate iff I can prove you cooperate with me) provably cooperates with itself, by Löb; Critch (2016) gives a resource-bounded version; Critch, Dennis and Russell (2022) survey open-source game theory and its surprises for institution design. — [arXiv:1401.5577](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.5577); [arXiv:1602.04184](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04184); [arXiv:2208.07006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07006)
- Payor's lemma (James Payor, presented by Critch 2023): if ⊢ □(□x → x) → x then ⊢ x. It needs only necessitation and distribution, not Löb's theorem, and supports a "modal handshake" for group cooperation. Löb's theorem fails when proof is relaxed to probabilistic belief; a probabilistic Payor lemma has been argued to hold in Christiano's self-referential probabilistic logic (Demski; Kumar 2023), with cooperation among arbitrarily many threshold agents — but the authors flag that the assumptions are not fully confirmed, and these are forum posts, not peer-reviewed results. **[checked: search summary; post bodies not fully retrieved]** — [Modal Fixpoint Cooperation without Löb's Theorem](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/2WpPRrqrFQa6n2x3W/modal-fixpoint-cooperation-without-loeb-s-theorem); [Probabilistic Payor Lemma?](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/ZWhJcHPmRaXAPAK5k/probabilistic-payor-lemma); [Self-Referential Probabilistic Logic Admits the Payor's Lemma](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/wmJT2j3rdT8ngFkPN/self-referential-probabilistic-logic-admits-the-payor-s)
- Embedded Agency (Demski & Garrabrant 2019) frames the open problems for an agent that is part of the world it models: decision theory, embedded world-models, robust delegation (including successor trust) and subsystem alignment; it is a problem statement, not a solution. — [arXiv:1902.09469](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09469)
- Cartesian frames (Garrabrant, Herrmann, Lopez-Wild 2021) re-found agent/environment boundaries on Chu spaces; finite factored sets (Garrabrant 2021) give a factorisation-based notion of time and conditional orthogonality as an alternative to Pearl-style causal graphs. Both are conceptual frameworks with few downstream theorems so far. — [arXiv:2109.10996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10996); [arXiv:2109.11513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11513)
- Infra-Bayesianism (Kosoy & Appel, 2020–): beliefs are convex sets of (sub)distributions with maximin decision rule, intended to handle non-realisability (the true environment is outside the hypothesis class, as it must be for an embedded agent). A peer-reviewed strand now exists: Kosoy, "Imprecise Multi-Armed Bandits" (JMLR 2025) defines regret against credal sets. A 2026 arXiv paper (2605.23146) reports a first implemented infra-Bayesian RL agent; I did not verify its authors or claims. **[checked]** — [Infra-Bayesianism sequence](https://www.lesswrong.com/s/n7qFxakSnxGuvmYAX); [Kosoy JMLR 2025](https://mlanthology.org/jmlr/2025/kosoy2025jmlr-imprecise/)
- Recent state of tiling work: Demski ran a "Lecture Series on Tiling Agents" (Jan 2025) and Payor posted "Working through a small tiling result" (May 2025); a commenter on Demski's Nov 2024 call for collaborators reports losing interest in the Löbian approach because probabilistic tiling results may be strong enough. This indicates active but unfinished research. **[checked: listing only; contents not retrieved]** — [LessWrong: Tiling Agents tag](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/tiling-agents); [Demski, Seeking Collaborators](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7AzexLYpXKMqevttN/seeking-collaborators)
- Meulemans et al. (Nov 2025), "Embedded Universal Predictive Intelligence", define a reflective universal inductor for multi-agent learning in which agents model a universe containing themselves; secondary summaries report grain-of-truth and convergence-to-equilibrium results that I did not verify against the paper. A related 2025 paper formalises embeddedness failures of AIXI. **[checked: existence and abstract-level only]** — [arXiv:2511.22226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22226); [arXiv:2505.17882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17882)

**Self-modifying systems in practice, and "guaranteed safe" proposals**
- Schmidhuber's Gödel machine (2003) rewrites itself only after proving the rewrite improves expected utility; it has never been practically realised. The Darwin Gödel Machine (Zhang et al. 2025) explicitly drops the proof requirement and validates self-modifications empirically on benchmarks. — [arXiv:cs/0309048](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0309048); [arXiv:2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954)
- "Towards Guaranteed Safe AI" (Dalrymple et al. 2024) proposes world model + safety specification + verifier producing an auditable proof certificate. The most cited critiques (Dickson, "Limitations on Formal Verification for AI Safety"; Hatfield-Dodds) argue that proofs hold only relative to a formal world model and that near-term strong guarantees against major threats are implausible; the authors reply that the world model need only handle uncertainty conservatively. **[checked]** — [arXiv:2405.06624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06624); [Dickson critique](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/B2bg677TaS4cmDPzL/limitations-on-formal-verification-for-ai-safety); [Response to critiques](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/DZuBHHKao6jsDDreH/in-response-to-critiques-of-guaranteed-safe-ai)

### Inferences
- What is solved: (a) self-reference without paradox (Kleene, guarded recursion, AFA); (b) a system verifying a strictly weaker system or a bounded number of self-modification steps; (c) consistent probabilistic self-trust and limit-coherent self-prediction (logical induction, reflective oracles) in idealised, computationally infeasible form; (d) proof-based mutual cooperation among transparent agents (Löb, Payor) in toy settings.
- What is not solved: a system that soundly and with undiminished strength certifies its own governance, or an unbounded chain of successors, by proof. Every consistent arrangement has one of these shapes: descending strength; an external or higher level that is trusted but not verified from inside (reflection principle as an axiom, i.e. an act of faith that raises the proof-theoretic ordinal); time-indexing (trust yesterday's self, as in guarded recursion); or replacing proof by probability/empirical evidence with non-zero residual risk.
- Governance consequence: "self-governing" can only rigorously mean "each level is checked by something it cannot fully check itself, and the regress terminates in an unverified trust anchor or goes round in a cycle of mutual, partial checks". A foundation must name its anchor(s) explicitly. Constitutions with amendment rules are the institutional analogue of reflective towers; unrestricted self-amendment is the analogue of fexprs (no stable invariants).
- Proof-theoretic ordinals give a usable design idea: termination/well-foundedness of a governance recursion (appeals, escalations, self-amendments) is itself a statement of some ordinal strength; the system cannot certify the well-foundedness of its own strongest recursion.

- The contents of Demski's 2025 lecture series and Payor's 2025 tiling result were not retrieved; I cannot state what, if anything, they prove.
- Hurwicz's Nobel lecture PDF returned HTTP 403, so details of his treatment are in section 5 from the search summary only.
- I found no peer-reviewed proof of a probabilistic Löbian/Payor cooperation theorem as of this search; the status is "plausible, forum-level".

## 3. Computability and complexity limits, and how structure buys tractability

### Takeaway
General verification of arbitrary behaviour is undecidable (Rice); verification of families of unboundedly many even finite-state components is undecidable (Apt–Kozen, Suzuki); decentralised control and synthesis are NEXP-complete or undecidable as soon as controllers have incomparable information (Bernstein et al.; Pnueli–Rosner; Tripakis); equilibrium computation is PPAD-hard. Tractability returns only by giving something up: restrict the program class (totality, types, monotonicity), restrict the topology (bounded treewidth, hierarchy without information forks, symmetry/anonymity), restrict the property (safety invariants, local contracts), or accept sound-but-incomplete analysis. None of these restrictions is free in expressiveness.

### Cited Findings

**Undecidability of verification**
- Rice's theorem (1953): every non-trivial extensional property of the partial computable functions is undecidable from program text. Scope: arbitrary programs in a Turing-complete language, exact decision. It does not forbid sound over-approximation, decidable restricted languages, or checking a supplied proof. — [Wikipedia: Rice's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice%27s_theorem)
- Harrison, Ruzzo, Ullman (1976): in the general access-matrix model, the "safety problem" (can subject s ever obtain right r on object o?) is undecidable; it becomes decidable for restricted models (e.g. mono-operational systems; take-grant is linear-time by Lipton & Snyder 1977). This is Rice's theorem for governance of rights. — [HRU, CACM 19(8)](https://doi.org/10.1145/360303.360333)
- Apt and Kozen (1986): it is undecidable whether a parameterised family of finite-state concurrent systems satisfies a temporal property for all sizes N. Suzuki (1988) showed this remains so for rings of identical finite-state processes. Later work finds decidable fragments via cut-offs, well-structured systems and symmetry. **[Apt–Kozen checked; Suzuki not confirmed in this session's search]** — [Apt & Kozen, IPL 22(6)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(86)90071-2); [Suzuki, IPL 28(4)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(88)90211-6); [Esparza, "Keeping a Crowd Safe", arXiv:1405.1841](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.1841)
- AI-specific restatements: Alfonseca et al. (JAIR 2021) argue total containment of a superintelligence is incomputable by reduction from halting; a 2024 paper shows inner alignment of an arbitrary model is undecidable by Rice and concludes systems should instead be built with alignment guarantees by construction; a 2024 paper (arXiv:2402.09500) contests the applicability of Rice where the relevant traits are syntactic. Brcic and Yampolskiy survey impossibility results in AI. **[partly checked]** — [arXiv:1607.00913](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00913); [arXiv:2408.08995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08995); [arXiv:2402.09500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09500); [arXiv:2109.00484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00484)

**Complexity of model checking and synthesis**
- LTL model checking is PSPACE-complete in the formula (Sistla & Clarke 1985), linear in the state space — but the state space of n composed components is exponential in n (state explosion). — [Sistla & Clarke, JACM 32(3)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828.3837); [Wikipedia: Model checking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_checking)
- Reachability for Petri nets / vector addition systems (unboundedly many identical tokens, no identities) is decidable but Ackermann-complete (Czerwiński & Orlikowski; Leroux, 2021). — [arXiv:2104.13866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13866)
- Reactive synthesis from LTL is 2EXPTIME-complete (Pnueli & Rosner 1989). Distributed synthesis is undecidable in general (Pnueli & Rosner 1990): already for two processes neither of which sees the other's inputs; it is decidable (non-elementarily) for hierarchical/pipeline architectures; the dividing line is the presence of an "information fork", a pair of processes with incomparable knowledge (Finkbeiner & Schewe 2005). **[checked]** — [Finkbeiner & Schewe, "Uniform distributed synthesis" (via survey PDF)](https://finkbeiner.groups.cispa.de/publications/atva07.pdf); [Wikipedia: Reactive synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactive_synthesis)
- Supervisory control (Ramadge & Wonham 1987) is polynomial for one explicit plant with full observation, but PSPACE-complete when the plant is a product of modules (Rohloff & Lafortune 2005), and joint observability / decentralised control with two or more observers is undecidable for regular languages (Tripakis 2004). **[Tripakis checked]** — [Tripakis, Verimag TR-2004-14](https://www-verimag.imag.fr/TR/TR-2004-14.pdf); [Rohloff & Lafortune, DEDS 15, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10626-004-6210-5)

**Decentralised decision-making**
- Bernstein, Givan, Immerman, Zilberstein (2002): finite-horizon decentralised POMDPs and decentralised MDPs with two or more agents are NEXP-complete, hence provably not in P; contrast MDP (P-complete) and single-agent POMDP (PSPACE-complete, Papadimitriou & Tsitsiklis 1987). Infinite-horizon POMDP policy existence is undecidable (Madani, Hanks, Condon). — [Bernstein et al., arXiv:1301.3836](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3836); [Math. OR 27(4)](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.27.4.819.297)
- Witsenhausen's counterexample (1968): a two-stage linear-quadratic-Gaussian problem with a non-classical information pattern (second controller does not know what the first knew) has non-linear strategies beating all affine ones; the optimum is still unknown; Papadimitriou and Tsitsiklis (1986) proved a discrete version NP-complete. — [Wikipedia: Witsenhausen's counterexample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witsenhausen%27s_counterexample)

**Games and mechanisms**
- Computing a Nash equilibrium is PPAD-complete (Daskalakis, Goldberg, Papadimitriou 2009; Chen, Deng, Teng 2009 for two players); Rubinstein (2016) shows even constant-approximate two-player Nash needs quasi-polynomial time under an ETH for PPAD. Hart and Mas-Colell (2003): no "uncoupled" dynamics (each player's adjustment depends only on own payoffs) converge to Nash in all games. Correlated equilibria, by contrast, are computable in polynomial time and reached by no-regret dynamics. — [DGP, SIAM J. Comput. 39(1)](https://doi.org/10.1137/070699652); [Chen–Deng–Teng, arXiv:0704.1678](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.1678); [Rubinstein, arXiv:1606.04550](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04550); [Wikipedia: PPAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPAD_(complexity))
- Automated mechanism design is NP-complete for deterministic mechanisms even in simple settings (Conitzer & Sandholm 2002); VCG requires solving the (NP-hard) winner-determination problem exactly, and approximate solutions generally break truthfulness (Nisan & Ronen). — [Conitzer & Sandholm, arXiv:cs/0205075](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0205075); [Wikipedia: Algorithmic mechanism design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_mechanism_design)
- Smart contracts on Turing-complete platforms inherit Rice's theorem; the documented attack classes (reentrancy as in The DAO, etc.) are behavioural properties not decidable in general. — [Atzei, Bartoletti, Cimoli, "A survey of attacks on Ethereum smart contracts"](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/1007)

**Irreducibility and No Free Lunch**
- Computational irreducibility (Wolfram 1985) is a thesis, not a general theorem: for systems capable of universal computation (e.g. Rule 110), long-run questions are undecidable and there is no general shortcut to simulation. Israeli and Goldenfeld (2004) show some irreducible cellular automata become predictable after coarse-graining, i.e. irreducibility at the micro level does not preclude tractable macro descriptions. — [Wikipedia: Computational irreducibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_irreducibility); [Israeli & Goldenfeld, arXiv:nlin/0309047](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0309047)
- No Free Lunch (Wolpert & Macready 1997): averaged uniformly over all objective functions on a finite domain, all black-box optimisers perform identically. Assumption: uniform prior over all functions, which real problem classes violate; the lesson is that performance comes from matching assumptions to structure, not from universality. — [Wikipedia: No free lunch theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_free_lunch_theorem)

**How structure restores tractability, and its price**
- Courcelle's theorem (1990): any property expressible in monadic second-order logic is decidable in linear time on graphs of bounded treewidth — with a constant that is non-elementary in the formula. Many graph-structured problems (inference, constraint satisfaction, equilibrium on tree-like graphical games) are polynomial for bounded treewidth. — [Wikipedia: Courcelle's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courcelle%27s_theorem); [Kearns, Littman, Singh, "Graphical models for game theory", arXiv:1301.2281](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.2281)
- Assume-guarantee / contract reasoning (Misra–Chandy 1981; Jones 1983; Abadi & Lamport 1993): verify each component against an assumption about its environment; the composition theorem is sound for safety properties (with care about circularity). Empirically, automated two-way assume-guarantee decomposition often gives little or no saving over monolithic checking (Cobleigh, Avrunin, Clarke 2008). — [Abadi & Lamport, "Composing specifications", TOPLAS 15(1)](https://doi.org/10.1145/151646.151649); [Cobleigh et al., "Breaking up is hard to do", TOSEM 17(2)](https://doi.org/10.1145/1348250.1348253); [Benveniste et al., "Contracts for System Design"](https://doi.org/10.1561/1000000053)
- Types: checking is decidable and compositional, but a type theory with decidable checking and logical consistency must be total (no general recursion), so it cannot express all computable functions, in particular not its own evaluator. — [Wikipedia: Total functional programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_functional_programming); [SEP: Type theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/type-theory/)
- Symmetry/anonymity: mean-field game theory (Lasry–Lions; Huang–Malhamé–Caines, 2006–07) makes the N → ∞ limit of exchangeable agents tractable; the cost is assuming agents are statistically identical and individually negligible. — [Wikipedia: Mean-field game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean-field_game_theory)
- Cost of full formal assurance at small scale: seL4 (Klein et al. 2009) verified about 8,700 lines of C with about 200,000 lines of Isabelle proof and on the order of 20 person-years in total. — [Klein et al., SOSP 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1629575.1629596)

### Inferences
- The decisive structural variable for control and synthesis is the information structure, not the number of agents: nested/hierarchical information (each controller knows what its subordinates know) keeps problems decidable; information forks (peers with incomparable private information who must coordinate) give NEXP-hardness or undecidability. Real societies are full of information forks, so optimal decentralised governance is uncomputable in the worst case; only satisficing, locally verifiable rules are available.
- Rice's theorem implies "govern arbitrary agents by inspecting their code" is impossible; the workable alternatives are (a) correctness by construction in a restricted language, (b) proof-carrying/attested behaviour where the actor bears the burden of supplying a checkable certificate, (c) runtime enforcement of safety properties (monitors, capabilities), (d) sound over-approximation with false alarms.
- A foundation that is universal (Turing-complete actors, arbitrary topologies, arbitrary properties) cannot also be decidable. The interesting design question is the choice of narrow fragments: which properties are enforced by construction (cheap, compositional), which by local contract, and which are merely monitored statistically.
- Bounded treewidth and "no information forks" both say roughly "tree-like". Hierarchy is the tractable shape — which is in tension with the robustness, incentive and control-loss problems of hierarchies in sections 5 and 7.

### Gaps
- I did not locate a single survey giving complexity of supervisor synthesis across all architectures; the three cited results are the standard anchors.
- No rigorous general theorem quantifies "expressiveness cost per unit tractability"; the trade-off is known case by case (language fragments, logics, topologies).

## 4. Limits of compositionality: what composes, what does not

### Takeaway
Being compositional in syntax (systems plug together) does not make properties compositional. Trace safety properties, types, capability confinement, monotone (CALM) computation, linearizability, passivity and differential-privacy budgets compose under stated conditions; liveness, timing/performance, information-flow security (possibilistic non-interference), incentive compatibility, fairness, equilibrium efficiency and AI "safety" demonstrably do not in general. Physics adds that macroscopic properties of composed local rules can be undecidable (spectral gap), and sheaf theory gives the exact form of "locally consistent, globally inconsistent".

### Cited Findings

**Safety, liveness, hyperproperties**
- Alpern and Schneider (1985): every trace property is the intersection of a safety and a liveness property. Abadi and Lamport's composition theorem covers assume-guarantee specifications where the guarantees are safety properties; liveness needs extra side conditions. — [Wikipedia: Safety and liveness properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_and_liveness_properties); [Abadi & Lamport 1993](https://doi.org/10.1145/151646.151649)
- Clarkson and Schneider (2010): security policies such as non-interference are hyperproperties (sets of sets of traces), not trace properties; refinement never invalidates a trace property but can invalidate a hyperproperty, which is why refining a secure specification can yield an insecure implementation (the "refinement paradox" noted by Jacob and resolved for deterministic systems by Roscoe). **[checked]** — [Clarkson & Schneider, Hyperproperties (TR)](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstream/1813/11660/4/hyperproperties-tr.pdf)
- McCullough (1987–88): multilevel security properties including generalised non-interference and non-deducibility on inputs are not preserved by "hook-up" composition; McLean (1994): for possibilistic security properties defined by closure under selective interleaving functions, closure is generally preserved by product and cascade, but not generally by feedback, internal composition or refinement. **[checked]** — [McLean, "A general theory of composition for trace sets closed under selective interleaving functions"](https://web.mst.edu/~ff/classes/6600/mclean-sp94.pdf); [Mantel, "On the composition of secure systems"](https://www.mais.informatik.tu-darmstadt.de/WebBibPHP/papers/2002/2002ieee-ssp.pdf)
- Universal composability (Canetti 2001) shows a security notion can be designed to compose under arbitrary concurrent composition, but at a price: many tasks (e.g. commitment) are provably not UC-realisable without trusted set-up (Canetti & Fischlin 2001). — [Canetti, ePrint 2000/067](https://eprint.iacr.org/2000/067); [Wikipedia: Universal composability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_composability)
- Feature interaction: independently correct telecom features interfere when combined; decades of work produced detection methods and architectures, not a general solution. — [Calder et al., "Feature interaction: a critical review and considered forecast"](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1389-1286(02)00352-3)

**Emergence and undecidable macro-properties**
- Anderson (1972), "More is Different": the ability to reduce everything to simple laws does not imply the ability to reconstruct the whole from them; each level of scale has its own laws. — [Science 177:393](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.177.4047.393)
- Cubitt, Perez-Garcia, Wolf (2015): for translation-invariant nearest-neighbour Hamiltonians on a 2D lattice with fixed finite local dimension, whether the system is gapped or gapless in the thermodynamic limit is undecidable; Bausch, Cubitt, Lucia, Perez-Garcia (2020) extend to 1D. Scope: infinite-size limit of specially constructed families; any fixed finite size is decidable. — [arXiv:1502.04573](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04573); [arXiv:1810.01858](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01858)
- Gu, Weedbrook, Perales, Nielsen (2009), "More really is different": macroscopic observables of infinite periodic Ising-type lattices (encoding cellular automata) are undecidable from the microscopic description. — [arXiv:0809.0151](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.0151)

**Local-to-global obstructions**
- Abramsky and Brandenburger (2011): contextuality and non-locality are exactly the non-existence of a global section for a compatible family of local sections of a presheaf (of distributions or possibilities) over measurement contexts; Čech cohomology gives sufficient (not complete) obstructions (Abramsky, Mansfield, Barbosa 2011). The same structure appears in relational databases (Abramsky 2012). — [arXiv:1102.0264](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0264); [arXiv:1111.3620](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3620); [arXiv:1208.6416](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.6416)
- Positive counterpart: for acyclic database schemas (equivalently Vorob'ev-regular complexes), pairwise consistency implies global consistency; cycles in the overlap structure are what create the obstruction (Beeri, Fagin, Maier, Yannakakis 1983). — [BFMY, JACM 30(3)](https://doi.org/10.1145/2402.322389)

**Game-theoretic and cybernetic non-compositionality**
- Braess's paradox: adding a link to a congested network can raise everyone's equilibrium travel time. Roughgarden and Tardos (2002): the price of anarchy of selfish routing is exactly 4/3 for linear latencies and unbounded for general latencies. — [Wikipedia: Braess's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braess%27s_paradox); [Wikipedia: Price of anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_of_anarchy)
- Goodhart's law / Campbell's law: a measure used as a control target loses its informational value. Formal relatives: Holmström–Milgrom multitask moral hazard (strong incentives on measurable tasks crowd out unmeasured ones); Manheim and Garrabrant's taxonomy (regressional, extremal, causal, adversarial Goodhart). — [Wikipedia: Goodhart's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law); [Manheim & Garrabrant, arXiv:1803.04585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)
- Ashby's law of requisite variety (1956): the variety of outcomes cannot be reduced below (variety of disturbances) / (variety of the regulator); in entropy form H(outcome) ≥ H(disturbance) − H(regulator) (minus channel capacity limits). — [Wikipedia: Variety (cybernetics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(cybernetics))
- Conant and Ashby (1970), "Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system": the actual theorem is modest — among optimal regulators, the simplest is a deterministic function of the system state — and its popular reading is contested. Stronger modern results: Richens and Everitt (2024) prove that agents robust to distribution shifts must have learned an approximate causal model; Richens et al. (2025) argue general goal-directed agents must contain a world model. — [Wikipedia: Good regulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_regulator); [arXiv:2402.10877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10877); [arXiv:2506.01622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01622)

**Properties known to compose (with conditions)**
- Capability confinement: in object-capability systems authority propagates only along references ("only connectivity begets connectivity"), enabling local reasoning about what a component can affect (Miller 2006; formalised e.g. by Maffeis, Mitchell, Taly 2010). — [Miller, Robust Composition (thesis)](http://www.erights.org/talks/thesis/)
- Differential privacy: k mechanisms that are each ε-DP compose to kε-DP (basic), or roughly ε·√(2k ln(1/δ')) with extra δ' (advanced composition; Dwork, Rothblum, Vadhan 2010); post-processing is free. Composition is quantitative: the budget is consumed. Exact optimal composition for heterogeneous mechanisms is #P-complete (Murtagh & Vadhan 2016). — [Dwork & Roth, Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~aaroth/Papers/privacybook.pdf); [arXiv:1507.03113](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.03113)
- CALM theorem (Hellerstein; proved by Ameloot, Neven, Van den Bussche): a problem has a consistent, coordination-free distributed implementation if and only if it is monotonic. Non-monotone logic (negation, aggregation over "all", deletion, mutual exclusion) needs coordination. — [Hellerstein & Alvaro, "Keeping CALM", arXiv:1901.01930](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01930)
- Linearizability is local: a system is linearizable iff each object is; serializability is not local (Herlihy & Wing 1990). — [Herlihy & Wing, TOPLAS 12(3)](https://doi.org/10.1145/78969.78972)
- Control: stability does not compose (feedback of two stable systems can be unstable), but passivity does, and the small-gain theorem gives a sufficient compositional condition. — [Wikipedia: Small-gain theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small-gain_theorem); [Wikipedia: Passivity (engineering)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passivity_(engineering))

**Properties known not to compose**
- Incentive compatibility: truthfulness is generally lost when mechanisms are run simultaneously or in sequence for agents with complementarities; Syrgkanis and Tardos (2013) show the composable notion is not truthfulness but "smoothness", which preserves only approximate-efficiency (price-of-anarchy) bounds. — [arXiv:1211.1325](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.1325)
- Fairness: Dwork and Ilvento (2018) show classifiers that are individually fair in isolation need not be fair under composition; Kleinberg, Mullainathan and Raghavan (2016) show calibration and balance for the positive and negative classes cannot all hold except in degenerate cases. — [arXiv:1806.06122](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06122); [arXiv:1609.05807](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05807)
- AI safety/alignment: Jones, Dragan, Steinhardt (ICML 2025) show adversaries can decompose a malicious task across individually "safe" models; combining Claude 3 Opus and Llama 2 70B yields 43% success generating vulnerable code where neither alone exceeds 3%. **[checked]** Hammond et al. (2025) catalogue multi-agent failure modes (miscoordination, conflict, collusion) that do not appear in single-agent evaluation. — [arXiv:2406.14595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14595); [arXiv:2502.14143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14143)
- Collective rationality: aggregating individually consistent preferences or judgments by majority can be inconsistent (Condorcet; discursive dilemma) — see section 5.

### Inferences
- A useful classification for the report: a property composes "for free" when it is (i) a safety property of single traces, (ii) monotone/closed under intersection or under the composition operators (invariants, types, capabilities, CRDT-style merges), or (iii) a resource with a sub-additive budget (privacy loss, gain, error probability via union bound). It fails to compose when it is (a) a liveness or progress property, (b) a relation between multiple executions (information flow, fairness, counterfactual/causal properties), (c) defined by a fixed point over all participants' best responses (equilibrium, incentive compatibility, alignment under strategic interaction), or (d) defined against a context the component cannot see (performance, global optimality).
- Whenever a non-compositional property has been made compositional (UC security, smoothness, passivity, DP), it was by replacing it with a strictly stronger or differently shaped property with an explicit quantitative budget and often a trusted set-up. Expect the same for "governance" and "alignment": a composable surrogate will be more conservative than the target property.
- The sheaf/acyclicity results say that cyclic overlap structure among local views is where global inconsistency lives. Trees glue; cycles need either a global arbiter or tolerance of inconsistency.
- Spectral-gap undecidability is evidence against any claim that macro behaviour of a composed system is derivable in general from local rules, but it is a statement about infinite limits of adversarially constructed families; it should not be cited as showing real finite organisations are unpredictable.

### Gaps
- I found no general theorem stating "alignment does not compose"; the evidence is empirical (Jones et al.) and by analogy with hyperproperties and equilibrium properties.
- The exact original citations for the "refinement paradox" (Jacob 1989; Roscoe 1995) were not retrieved in this session; the Clarkson–Schneider paper is the checked source for the phenomenon.

## 5. Social-choice and game-theoretic impossibilities for governance at scale

### Takeaway
No aggregation or allocation mechanism satisfies all the desiderata one would naively demand: Arrow, Gibbard–Satterthwaite, Sen, List–Pettit, Myerson–Satterthwaite, Holmström and Hurwicz each show a small set of individually reasonable axioms is jointly unsatisfiable. Enforcement is itself a game (Hurwicz's guardians), hierarchies lose control multiplicatively per level, flat agreement among n parties tolerates fewer than n/3 Byzantine members and costs Ω(n²) messages, and identity cannot be made Sybil-proof without a trusted authority or a scarce resource. Every governance design therefore chooses which axiom to relax, and should say which.

### Cited Findings

**Aggregation**
- Arrow (1951): with at least three alternatives and finitely many voters, no social welfare function on unrestricted preference profiles satisfies weak Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives and non-dictatorship. Escapes: restricted domains (single-peaked), cardinal/interpersonally comparable information, or dropping IIA. — [SEP: Arrow's theorem](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arrows-theorem/)
- Gibbard (1973) / Satterthwaite (1975): with at least three possible outcomes, every onto, strategy-proof, deterministic voting rule on unrestricted preferences is dictatorial. — [Wikipedia: Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbard%E2%80%93Satterthwaite_theorem)
- Sen (1970), "The impossibility of a Paretian liberal": unrestricted domain, weak Pareto and minimal liberalism (two individuals each decisive over one pair) are inconsistent. Consequence: local autonomy (subsidiarity) and global Pareto efficiency conflict. — [Wikipedia: Liberal paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_paradox)
- List and Pettit (2002): on a sufficiently interconnected agenda, no judgment aggregation rule satisfies universal domain, collective rationality (complete, consistent outputs), anonymity and systematicity; proposition-wise majority can yield an inconsistent collective view from consistent individuals (discursive dilemma). — [SEP: Social choice theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-choice/)

**Mechanisms and teams**
- Myerson and Satterthwaite (1983): for bilateral trade with independent private values whose supports overlap, no mechanism is simultaneously Bayesian incentive compatible, interim individually rational, budget balanced and ex post efficient. — [Wikipedia: Myerson–Satterthwaite theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myerson%E2%80%93Satterthwaite_theorem)
- Holmström (1982), "Moral hazard in teams": with unobservable individual effort and joint output, no budget-balanced sharing rule makes efficient effort a Nash equilibrium; efficiency needs a budget breaker (an outside residual claimant). — [Wikipedia: Holmström's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmstr%C3%B6m%27s_theorem)
- Folk theorems (e.g. Fudenberg & Maskin 1986): in repeated games with sufficiently patient players, every feasible, individually rational payoff vector is an equilibrium outcome. Cooperation is sustainable, but so is almost anything; equilibrium selection is not determined by the game. — [Wikipedia: Folk theorem (game theory)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_theorem_(game_theory))

**Guardians and hierarchies**
- Hurwicz (Nobel lecture 2007; AER 2008), "But who will guard the guardians?": treats enforcement as part of the game — a mechanism is only "implemented" if its rules are themselves an equilibrium of the larger game including enforcers; "if implementation is impossible or prohibitively costly, even the most attractive mechanism remains a utopia". **[checked via search summary; PDF itself returned 403]** I recall, but did not verify in this session, that his way out of the regress relies on the existence of at least some "intervenors" who follow the rules for intrinsic reasons, and on arrangements where guardians guard each other. — [Nobel lecture PDF](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/hurwicz_lecture.pdf); [AER 98(3):577–585](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v98y2008i3p577-85.html)
- Williamson (1967), "Hierarchical control and optimum firm size" (JPE 75(2):123–138): models a cumulative, multiplicative loss of control across hierarchical levels, giving a finite optimal firm size even under static conditions. **[citation checked; content from secondary summaries]** — [Williamson, JPE](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/259258)
- Calvo and Wellisz (1978), "Supervision, loss of control, and the optimum size of the firm" (JPE 86(5):943–952): whether loss of control limits firm size depends crucially on the supervision technology (as I recall: random, unannounced monitoring removes the limit; predictable monitoring does not). **[citation checked; the characterisation of the result was not confirmed from the paper itself]** — [Calvo & Wellisz, JPE](https://doi.org/10.1086/260719)

**Fault tolerance, identity, collusion**
- Lamport, Shostak, Pease (1982): with unauthenticated messages, Byzantine agreement among n parties is possible iff n ≥ 3f + 1; with unforgeable signatures (synchronous model) any number of faults is tolerable. — [LSP, TOPLAS 4(3)](https://doi.org/10.1145/357172.357176)
- Fischer, Lynch, Paterson (1985): no deterministic protocol solves consensus in a fully asynchronous system with even one crash fault. Gilbert and Lynch (2002): consistency, availability and partition tolerance cannot all be guaranteed (CAP). — [FLP, JACM 32(2)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3149.214121); [Wikipedia: CAP theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAP_theorem)
- Dolev and Reischuk (1985): deterministic Byzantine agreement needs Ω(n·f) messages, i.e. Ω(n²) for f proportional to n. — [Dolev & Reischuk, JACM 32(1)](https://doi.org/10.1145/2455.214112)
- Douceur (2002), "The Sybil attack": without a logically centralised trusted authority, a system cannot prevent one entity from presenting many identities, except under unrealistic assumptions of resource parity and coordination among entities. Current responses rely on scarce resources (work, stake) or issued credentials (e.g. "personhood credentials", Adler et al. 2024). — [Douceur, The Sybil Attack](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-sybil-attack/); [arXiv:2408.07892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07892)
- Collusion: VCG is strategy-proof for individuals but vulnerable to collusion and shill bidding. Learning algorithms can reach supra-competitive prices without communication (Calvano et al. 2020), LLM pricing agents do likewise in experiments (Fish, Gonczarowski, Shorrer 2024), and AI agents can collude via steganographic channels that overseers cannot detect (Motwani et al. 2024). — [Wikipedia: Vickrey–Clarke–Groves auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickrey%E2%80%93Clarke%E2%80%93Groves_auction); [Calvano et al., AER 110(10)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20190623); [arXiv:2404.00806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00806); [arXiv:2402.07510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07510)

### Inferences
- These theorems are about worst-case domains and exact satisfaction. Working institutions escape by restricting the domain (structured choices, single-peakedness), using money/transfers and an outside budget breaker, accepting approximate efficiency, randomising, or tolerating some manipulability. A candidate foundation should expose these as explicit, typed choices rather than hide them.
- Recursion makes the problems recur at every level: if each level aggregates its members' judgments, the discursive dilemma and Arrow apply per level, and level-wise majority can differ from global majority (the same phenomenon as gerrymandering and Ostrogorski/Simpson-type paradoxes). "Fractal democracy" does not inherit the properties of the base rule.
- The guardians regress has the same logical shape as Löb/Gödel in section 2: the system cannot internally certify its own enforcers. Formal treatments end in (i) intrinsically rule-following agents, (ii) mutual monitoring cycles where deviation is unprofitable in equilibrium (folk-theorem logic, hence equilibrium-selection fragile), or (iii) an external anchor.
- Byzantine bounds apply per consensus group. With 10^10 actors, flat deterministic agreement is infeasible (Ω(n²) ≈ 10^20 messages per decision); scalable designs use small sampled committees (probabilistic guarantees, requires Sybil resistance and unbiased randomness) or hierarchy (then the 1/3 bound must hold at every committee, and a small captured committee high up overrides honest majorities below).
- Sybil impossibility matters far more with AI agents: identities are cheap to mint, so "one actor one vote" presupposes an identity authority — a centralised trust anchor that the foundation must include rather than derive.

### Gaps
- Hurwicz's formal apparatus was not read first-hand in this session.
- Williamson's and Calvo–Wellisz's exact functional forms were not retrieved (paywalled); the per-level control-loss arithmetic in section 7 uses an illustrative parameter, not their calibrated values.
- I know of no theorem giving a tight general bound on collusion-resistance at scale; results are mechanism-specific.

## 6. Universal-foundation attempts: why they stalled, and what "narrow waist" successes suggest

### Takeaway
Attempts at a universal content-bearing foundation (a universal language, ontology, model or plan) recur across three centuries and stall for a small number of repeating reasons: provable logical limits (Hilbert), failure of reduction to a fixed base (Carnap), unbounded hand-encoding and lack of convergence on one ontology (Cyc, upper ontologies, Semantic Web), reasoning complexity, low practitioner adoption (UML/MDA), computational intractability plus misreported inputs (central planning), and institutional resistance (OGAS) or the loss of local knowledge under imposed legibility (Scott). The durable successes are narrow waists: minimal, content-agnostic interfaces that standardise one thin thing and leave everything above and below free.

### Cited Findings

**Logical and philosophical programmes**
- Leibniz's characteristica universalis and calculus ratiocinator: a universal symbolic language in which reasoning becomes calculation; never realised, but ancestral to symbolic logic. — [Wikipedia: Characteristica universalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristica_universalis)
- Hilbert's programme: formalise all mathematics and prove consistency by finitary means; Gödel's second theorem shows this is impossible as stated, but relativised/partial versions succeed (ordinal analysis; reverse mathematics shows large parts of ordinary mathematics are finitistically reducible). — [SEP: Hilbert's program](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hilbert-program/)
- Carnap's Aufbau (1928): constitute all scientific concepts from a single basis (elementary experiences plus one relation) by explicit definition; the constructions break down beyond the lowest levels, as Quine and Carnap himself acknowledged. — [SEP: Rudolf Carnap](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/carnap/)
- Bourbaki: Corry (1992) shows the formal concept of "structure" laid down in the treatise's foundations plays almost no role in the mathematics that follows; the unifying power came from an informal structural style, and the group did not absorb category theory. — [Corry, Synthese 92:315–348](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00414286)
- General Systems Theory (von Bertalanffy) sought isomorphic laws across all systems; it produced vocabulary and influential heuristics rather than a predictive formal theory. — [Wikipedia: Systems theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_theory)

**Ontologies, the Semantic Web, modelling languages**
- Cyc (1984– ) hand-encodes common-sense knowledge into a single logical ontology; after four decades and millions of assertions it remains a niche system. Lenat and Marcus (2023) present it as a complement to LLMs rather than a stand-alone route to general AI. — [Wikipedia: Cyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyc); [Lenat & Marcus, arXiv:2308.04445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04445)
- Upper ontologies (SUMO, BFO, DOLCE and others) coexist without convergence; they embody incompatible philosophical commitments, and BFO's standardisation (ISO/IEC 21838-2) did not end the plurality. — [Wikipedia: Upper ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology)
- Semantic Web: the full vision of machine-reasoning agents over a global ontology web (Berners-Lee, Hendler, Lassila 2001) was not realised; pragmatic subsets (linked data, schema.org, knowledge graphs) were. Reasoning in full OWL 2 DL is N2EXPTIME-complete, which is why tractable profiles (EL, QL, RL) were standardised. — [Wikipedia: Semantic Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_Web); [W3C OWL 2 Profiles](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/)
- UML/MDA: Petre (ICSE 2013) interviewed 50 professional developers; 35 did not use UML at all and most others used it selectively and informally. — [Petre, "UML in practice"](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE.2013.6606618)
- Wolfram Physics Project / ruliad: proposes that physics emerges from all possible hypergraph rewriting rules; physicists' published criticism is that it has not produced novel quantitative, testable predictions. — [Wolfram, "The Concept of the Ruliad"](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2021/11/the-concept-of-the-ruliad/); [Scientific American, 2020](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/physicists-criticize-stephen-wolframs-theory-of-everything/)
- Constructor theory (Deutsch 2013; Deutsch & Marletto 2015) recasts physical laws as statements about which transformations are possible or impossible; it is an active research programme whose main outputs so far are reformulations (information, thermodynamics, life) and proposed witnesses of non-classicality. — [arXiv:1210.7439](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7439); [arXiv:1405.5563](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5563)

**Planning and state legibility**
- Economic calculation debate: Mises (1920) argued rational allocation is impossible without market prices; Hayek (1945) reframed it as a knowledge problem — the relevant knowledge is dispersed, tacit and local, and prices are the summarising signal; Lange proposed trial-and-error market socialism; Cockshott and Cottrell (1993–) argue modern computing makes labour-value-based planning tractable. — [Hayek, "The Use of Knowledge in Society"](https://www.econlib.org/library/Essays/hykKnw.html); [Wikipedia: Socialist calculation debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_calculation_debate)
- Shalizi (2012), "In Soviet Union, Optimization Problem Solves You": citing Nove, the USSR had about 12 million identifiably different products in 1983. Interior-point LP needs time proportional to (m+n)^{3/2} n² log(1/ε), roughly n^3.5; a good solver handles 12–13 million variables in minutes, but multiplying variables by 1,000 (to index by time/place) multiplies time by about 3×10^10; non-convexities (increasing returns) make the problem generally intractable; participants will "straight-up lie" about capacities, with no good mathematical remedy. He concludes whole-economy planning is intractable for the foreseeable future, that planning works within bounded domains, and that markets are not a satisfactory alternative everywhere either. **[checked by page fetch]** — [Shalizi, bactra.org mirror](https://bactra.org/weblog/918.html); [Crooked Timber original](https://crookedtimber.org/?p=24607)
- Project Cybersyn (Chile 1971–73; Stafford Beer): a telex network linking nationalised firms to a central operations room, built on the Viable System Model; it was only partly built, was used notably during the October 1972 strike, and ended with the 1973 coup. Medina stresses the gap between the designers' decentralising intent and what the technology and politics permitted. — [Medina, Cybernetic Revolutionaries (MIT Press)](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262525961/cybernetic-revolutionaries/); [Wikipedia: Project Cybersyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Cybersyn)
- OGAS (USSR; Glushkov, proposed from 1962): a three-tier national network for economic management; never built at scale. Peters argues it failed not for technical reasons but because ministries and bureaucrats competed and defended their information — "the capitalists behaved like socialists while the socialists behaved like capitalists". — [Peters, How Not to Network a Nation (MIT Press)](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262534666/how-not-to-network-a-nation/); [Wikipedia: OGAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OGAS)
- James C. Scott, Seeing Like a State (1998): states simplify society to make it legible; large schemes fail catastrophically when legibility-driven simplification, high-modernist ideology, authoritarian power and a weak civil society combine, because they destroy the practical local knowledge (mētis) on which the system actually ran. — [Wikipedia: Seeing Like a State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seeing_Like_a_State)

**Narrow-waist successes**
- Internet hourglass: Beck (2019) formalises the "hourglass theorem" — the weaker (more minimal) the spanning layer's specification, the more possible supports below and (subject to sufficiency) applications above; Akhshabi and Dovrolis (2011) derive the hourglass shape from an evolutionary model of layered protocols. — [Beck, "On the hourglass model", CACM 62(7)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3274770); [Akhshabi & Dovrolis, SIGCOMM 2011](https://doi.org/10.1145/2018436.2018460)
- Relational model (Codd 1970): a deliberately sub-Turing query language (relational algebra ≡ relational calculus) that gave data independence, optimisation and decidable reasoning about queries. — [Codd, CACM 13(6)](https://doi.org/10.1145/362384.362685)
- λ-calculus / Turing machines: a minimal universal model of computation — universal for *expressing* computation, and for that very reason subject to Rice's theorem. — [SEP: The Church–Turing thesis](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/church-turing/)
- Double-entry bookkeeping: one conservation invariant (debits = credits) that composes across accounts and entities and makes local errors globally detectable; legal personhood: one interface (can own, contract, sue and be sued) uniform across humans, firms and states; the shipping container: one physical interface (ISO dimensions and corner fittings) indifferent to contents. — [Wikipedia: Double-entry bookkeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-entry_bookkeeping); [Wikipedia: Legal person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_person); [Wikipedia: Intermodal container](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_container)

### Inferences
- Recurring failure pattern: the attempt standardises *content* (what things are, what the plan is, what everything means) and therefore must (a) encode unbounded, changing, tacit local knowledge, (b) win agreement on one ontology, (c) pay worst-case reasoning complexity, and (d) rely on truthful inputs from parties with incentives to shade them. Recurring success pattern: standardise a thin *interface plus one invariant* (packet delivery; tuples and a closed algebra; conservation of value; who can be a party; box corners), be agnostic about content, and keep the waist too weak to express everything.
- The successful waists are each compositional in exactly the cheap senses of section 4: closure (a network of networks is a network; a relation of relations; consolidated accounts; a corporation owning corporations) and a safety/conservation invariant. None of them delivers liveness, fairness, optimality or good incentives; those are left to layers above.
- "Legal person" is the strongest existing precedent for the investigation's recursion goal: humans, firms, states (and potentially AI agents) present the same type to contract and liability law, and persons can contain persons. Its guarantees are correspondingly thin (accountability and capacity, not behaviour).
- Lesson for a "smaller, more fundamental foundation": smaller is plausible and historically rewarded; "from which everything can be constructed *and governed*" conflates expressive universality (achievable, cheap) with property-universality (blocked by sections 2–5).
- Cybersyn/OGAS show the binding constraint on society-scale cybernetic governance has been institutional incentives and legitimacy before computation; Shalizi shows computation would also bind.

### Gaps
- Enterprise architecture frameworks (Zachman, TOGAF) and SysML: I did not retrieve rigorous evaluations in this session; I am aware of practitioner critiques but cite none.
- Cockshott and Cottrell's own complexity estimates were not retrieved; the search surfaced only the existence of the disagreement with Shalizi.
- Quantitative details of Cybersyn (machine counts) and OGAS (dates of specific Politburo decisions) were not verified and are omitted.
- The assessment of constructor theory and GST as "few predictive results" is my characterisation; no systematic evaluation was found.

## 7. Physical and informational bounds for about 10^10 actors

### Takeaway
Fundamental physics (Landauer, Bremermann, Bekenstein) is nowhere near binding at 10^10 actors; the binding limits are quadratic interaction counts, human attention (order 10 bits/s per person), latency of order 100 ms across the planet, the data-processing inequality up any hierarchy, and linear lower bounds on communication for global consistency checks. Addressing 10^10 actors takes 34 bits; coordinating them takes structure.

### Cited Findings
- Landauer's principle: erasing one bit dissipates at least kT ln 2 (about 2.9×10^-21 J at 300 K). Lloyd (2000) derives ultimate limits for a 1 kg, 1 litre computer of about 5×10^50 operations per second and about 10^31 bits. — [Wikipedia: Landauer's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landauer%27s_principle); [Lloyd, arXiv:quant-ph/9908043](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9908043)
- Human throughput: Zheng and Meister (Neuron 2025) estimate human behavioural information throughput at about 10 bits/s across tasks, against about 10^9 bits/s of sensory input; Coupé et al. (2019) find speech across 17 languages converges on about 39 bits/s. — [arXiv:2408.10234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10234); [Coupé et al., Science Advances](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaw2594)
- Human relational span: Dunbar's number (about 150 stable relationships) and short-term memory span of 7±2 (Miller; later estimates about 4 chunks) are the usual empirical anchors for span of control. — [Wikipedia: Dunbar's number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbar%27s_number); [Wikipedia: The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magical_Number_Seven,_Plus_or_Minus_Two)
- Data-processing inequality: for a Markov chain X → Y → Z, I(X;Z) ≤ I(X;Y); no processing at an intermediate level can recover information lost below it. — [Wikipedia: Data processing inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing_inequality)
- Communication complexity: deciding whether two parties' n-bit sets are disjoint requires Ω(n) bits of communication even with randomisation (Kalyanasundaram–Schnitger; Razborov). — [Wikipedia: Communication complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_complexity)
- Epidemic dissemination: randomised rumour spreading informs all n nodes in O(log n) rounds with O(n log log n) messages (Karp, Schindelhauer, Shenker, Vöcking 2000). — [Wikipedia: Gossip protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gossip_protocol)
- Existence proof of scale by aggregation: the global Internet routes among billions of hosts with a BGP table on the order of 10^6 prefixes, via hierarchical address aggregation and autonomous systems that hide internal structure. — [BGP Routing Table Analysis Reports](https://bgp.potaroo.net/)
- Speed of light: 299,792 km/s in vacuum; roughly two-thirds of that in optical fibre. — [Wikipedia: Speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light)

### Inferences (my arithmetic; check before quoting)
- Addressing: log2(10^10) ≈ 33.2, so 34 bits name every actor; 10^10 actors have about 5×10^19 unordered pairs (about 66 bits to name a pair). Any mechanism whose cost is per-pair is out of reach; cost must be per-actor times polylog.
- Hierarchy depth = ⌈log_s N⌉: span 7 → 12 levels; span 10 → 10 levels; span 30 → 7 levels; span 150 → 5 levels (150^5 ≈ 7.6×10^10). So even at Dunbar-scale spans a single hierarchy over humanity-plus-agents is 5 deep; at managerial spans it is 10–12 deep.
- Control loss: if a fraction α of intent/information survives each level (Williamson's idea; α illustrative), across 9 hand-offs (10 levels) α = 0.9 leaves 39%, α = 0.8 leaves 13%; across 4 hand-offs (5 levels) α = 0.9 leaves 66%. By the data-processing inequality this loss cannot be undone higher up, only avoided by side channels (direct sampling/audit of the base) or by not needing the information centrally (subsidiarity).
- Latency: half of Earth's circumference is about 20,000 km → at least 67 ms one way in vacuum, about 100 ms in fibre; antipodal round trip ≥ 130–200 ms. Any globally coordinated decision is bounded to a few per second per sequential round; a k-round global protocol takes at least k × ~0.2 s. Fast control loops (machine-speed agents acting in microseconds to milliseconds) must therefore be governed locally with pre-delegated authority; global governance can only set slowly varying constraints.
- Dissemination is cheap, agreement is not: gossip reaches 10^10 nodes in about log2(10^10) ≈ 33 rounds (plus a small constant factor), so broadcasting rules or revocations is feasible in seconds; but flat Byzantine agreement costs Ω(n²) ≈ 10^20 messages, and exact global consistency checks need communication linear in state size.
- Audit volume: if each actor emits one 100-byte auditable event per second, that is 10^12 bytes/s ≈ 86 PB/day — storable by today's hyperscale standards but not humanly reviewable. Total human deliberate attention is about 8×10^9 × 10 bits/s ≈ 10^11 bits/s, roughly 1% of that event stream's bit rate even if all humans did nothing else. Oversight must therefore be by sampling, by automated checkers (reintroducing the guardians problem), or by construction. Random audit detects a violation occurring at rate p with confidence 1−δ after about ln(1/δ)/p samples, independent of N — statistical assurance scales; exhaustive assurance does not.
- Ashby in numbers: a regulator can only select among as many responses as it has capacity for; a central body with bounded channel capacity C bits/s cannot remove more than C bits/s of disturbance variety from 10^10 actors. Most variety must be absorbed where it arises.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published end-to-end quantitative model of governance overhead at 10^10 actors; the above is assembled from standard bounds.
- Empirical per-level control-loss coefficients for human or AI-agent hierarchies were not found; α values are illustrative only.
- Current BGP table size was not re-read this session beyond the order of magnitude.

## 8. Synthesis: design constraints any candidate foundation must satisfy, and red-flag claims

### Takeaway
A credible foundation will look like a narrow waist — closure under composition plus a short list of invariants that provably compose — with explicit stratification for self-reference, explicit trust anchors, and explicit, budgeted approximations for everything that does not compose. Claims of completeness, self-certification, or property-preserving universality contradict specific theorems and should be treated as red flags.

### Cited Findings
(Each constraint or red flag below is a consequence of results cited in sections 1–7; the key source is repeated for traceability.)

**Design constraints**
1. Separate closure from property preservation. "A system of systems is a system" should be a theorem of the syntax (operad/monoidal closure); for each property the foundation must state the composition operator(s) under which it is preserved and the side conditions. — [Spivak, arXiv:1305.0297](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0297); [McLean 1994](https://web.mst.edu/~ff/classes/6600/mclean-sp94.pdf)
2. State the recursion scheme: is Sys the initial algebra (finite, well-founded nesting, induction available) or the final coalgebra (open-ended behaviour, only coinduction)? Do not claim both termination-style guarantees and unbounded reactive behaviour for the same objects. — [nLab: initial algebra](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/initial+algebra+of+an+endofunctor); [Rutten 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00056-6)
3. Stratify or guard self-reference. Self-description is free (Kleene); self-evaluation of truth/safety must be level-indexed, time-indexed (guarded), partial, or probabilistic. No "type of all systems" containing itself at the same level. — [Yanofsky, arXiv:math/0305282](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305282); [Wikipedia: System U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_U); [Birkedal et al., arXiv:1208.3596](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.3596)
4. Name the trust anchors. By Gödel II/Löb and Hurwicz, the governance of the governance cannot be fully certified from inside; the design must say what is assumed rather than proved (root axioms, root identities, root enforcers) and how those are contested and replaced. — [SEP: Provability logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-provability/); [Hurwicz, AER 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v98y2008i3p577-85.html)
5. Self-modification rules must preserve a fixed kernel of invariants or pass through a strictly stronger/earlier checker; unrestricted reflective self-amendment destroys all modular reasoning. Expect proof-based successor trust to be bounded in depth or to lose strength; budget for empirical/probabilistic assurance. — [Tiling Agents](https://intelligence.org/files/TilingAgentsDraft.pdf); [Wikipedia: Fexpr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fexpr)
6. Restrict what is verified, not just how. Arbitrary behaviour of Turing-complete actors is unverifiable (Rice; HRU). Use correctness by construction in restricted languages, certificates supplied by the actor, capability confinement, and runtime monitors for safety properties. — [Wikipedia: Rice's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice%27s_theorem); [HRU 1976](https://doi.org/10.1145/360303.360333); [Miller thesis](http://www.erights.org/talks/thesis/)
7. Guarantees must hold for all sizes by a parametric argument (cut-off, induction on structure, symmetry), since model-checking instances does not generalise (Apt–Kozen) and state spaces explode. — [Apt & Kozen 1986](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(86)90071-2)
8. Design the information structure deliberately. Avoid requiring optimal coordination across information forks (NEXP/undecidable); prefer nested information, local objectives and satisficing contracts. — [Bernstein et al., arXiv:1301.3836](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3836); [Tripakis TR](https://www-verimag.imag.fr/TR/TR-2004-14.pdf)
9. Prefer monotone, coordination-free cores (CALM, CRDT-like state, append-only logs, capabilities that only attenuate) and isolate the non-monotone decisions (revocation, exclusion, allocation of scarce goods) where coordination cost is paid knowingly. — [Hellerstein & Alvaro, arXiv:1901.01930](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01930)
10. Treat non-compositional properties with budgets and re-verification at each composition: liveness, performance, information flow, fairness, incentive compatibility, AI safety. Provide a conservative composable surrogate (as DP, UC, passivity, smoothness do) and state its slack. — [Clarkson & Schneider](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstream/1813/11660/4/hyperproperties-tr.pdf); [Dwork & Roth](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~aaroth/Papers/privacybook.pdf); [Jones et al., arXiv:2406.14595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14595)
11. Declare which social-choice axiom is relaxed at each aggregation point (domain restriction, IIA, strategy-proofness, budget balance, anonymity) and expect level-wise aggregation to differ from global aggregation. — [SEP: Arrow's theorem](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arrows-theorem/); [SEP: Social choice theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-choice/)
12. Include an identity/Sybil-resistance primitive as an assumption, and state fault thresholds per committee (n ≥ 3f+1) and what happens when a level is captured. — [Douceur 2002](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-sybil-attack/); [LSP 1982](https://doi.org/10.1145/357172.357176)
13. Respect variety and attention arithmetic: absorb variety locally (subsidiarity), move only compressed summaries upward (accepting data-processing loss), audit by sampling, and keep depth × per-level loss within tolerance. — [Wikipedia: Variety (cybernetics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(cybernetics)); [Williamson 1967](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/259258)
14. Keep the waist minimal and content-agnostic; do not standardise a universal ontology or global objective; leave room for local knowledge and for plural, competing layers above the waist. — [Beck, CACM 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3274770); [Wikipedia: Seeing Like a State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seeing_Like_a_State)
15. Anticipate Goodhart: any metric used for control will be gamed; rotate, randomise or keep some evaluation unobservable to the evaluated. — [Manheim & Garrabrant, arXiv:1803.04585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)

**Red-flag claims**
- "The system can verify/prove its own consistency, safety or alignment" (without a named stronger outer system or weakened base) — contradicts Gödel II/Löb; Willard-style exceptions are too weak for arithmetic with total multiplication. — [Wikipedia: Self-verifying theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-verifying_theories)
- "One universal type/universe contains everything including itself" — Girard/Russell/Lawvere unless stratified or restricted. — [Wikipedia: System U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_U)
- "Fully reflective and fully analysable" — Wand's fexpr result; reflection must be restricted to keep invariants. — [Wikipedia: Fexpr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fexpr)
- "Compositional, therefore correct/safe/secure by construction at every scale" without a per-property composition theorem — contradicted by McCullough/McLean, hyperproperties, feature interaction, and safe-model-combination attacks. — [arXiv:2406.14595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14595)
- "Verifies arbitrary agents / arbitrary smart contracts / arbitrary AI behaviour automatically" — Rice, HRU, Apt–Kozen. — [arXiv:1405.1841](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.1841)
- "Computes the optimal decentralised policy / global plan / equilibrium for all participants" — NEXP (Dec-POMDP), PPAD (Nash), LP scaling and non-convexity (planning), plus misreporting. — [arXiv:1301.3836](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3836); [Shalizi 2012](https://bactra.org/weblog/918.html)
- "Fair, efficient, strategy-proof, budget-balanced and non-dictatorial" in any combination ruled out by Arrow, Gibbard–Satterthwaite, Myerson–Satterthwaite, Holmström. — [Wikipedia: Myerson–Satterthwaite theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myerson%E2%80%93Satterthwaite_theorem)
- "Fully decentralised, permissionless and Sybil-proof / one-entity-one-vote with no identity authority and no scarce resource" — Douceur. — [Douceur 2002](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-sybil-attack/)
- "Consistent, available and partition-tolerant", or "deterministic asynchronous consensus with guaranteed termination", or Byzantine tolerance at or above one third without signatures and synchrony — CAP, FLP, LSP. — [Wikipedia: CAP theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAP_theorem)
- "Emergent global behaviour is derivable/predictable from local rules in general" — computational universality of local rules; spectral-gap undecidability (in the infinite limit). — [arXiv:1502.04573](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04573)
- "A universal ontology/language everyone will adopt" — Carnap, Cyc, upper ontologies, Semantic Web, UML adoption data. — [Petre 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE.2013.6606618)
- "Same guarantees at every level because the structure is self-similar" — self-similarity of description is not self-similarity of properties; control loss, aggregation paradoxes and fault thresholds compound per level. — [Williamson 1967](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/259258)
- "Works for any environment/objective" — No Free Lunch; requisite variety. — [Wikipedia: No free lunch theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_free_lunch_theorem)
- Invoking Gödel, spectral-gap undecidability or "computational irreducibility" to claim that *nothing* can be verified or predicted is equally a red flag: these are worst-case, infinite-limit or arbitrary-program results, and restricted classes are decidable by construction. — [Israeli & Goldenfeld, arXiv:nlin/0309047](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0309047); [arXiv:2402.09500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09500)

### Inferences
- The positive space left by the impossibility results is specific and not small: (1) a typed, stratified, compositional calculus of actors with closure under nesting; (2) a kernel of monotone, safety-type invariants (conservation/accounting, capability attenuation, identity/accountability, append-only provenance) that provably compose and can be enforced locally; (3) local contracts with assume-guarantee reasoning for safety; (4) explicitly budgeted, statistical or re-verified treatment of liveness, incentives, fairness and alignment; (5) named trust anchors and a bounded, guarded procedure for self-amendment. This is essentially "legal person + double-entry + capabilities + types", i.e. a narrow waist, rather than a theory of everything.
- The single most important honest sentence for the final report: a foundation can be universal in what it can *express* or strong in what it can *guarantee*, and the theorems above price the exchange rate between the two; nothing known gives both.
- Unresolved research frontier (as of late 2026): successor/self-trust beyond toy settings; composable surrogates for alignment and incentive compatibility; parametric verification techniques that cover heterogeneous learning agents; Sybil-resistant identity for AI agents without a single global authority.

### Gaps
- This synthesis is my inference from the cited results; I found no single prior source that assembles these constraints for "compositional governance of systems of systems" specifically. The closest surveys are Brcic & Yampolskiy (impossibility results in AI) and Hammond et al. (multi-agent risks), neither of which covers the recursion-theoretic or categorical material.
- Verification status: most classical citations were not re-opened in this session; DOIs for Suzuki (1988), Calvo–Wellisz (1978), Rohloff–Lafortune (2005), Smith (1984), Feferman (1962) and Akhshabi–Dovrolis (2011) are given from memory and should be spot-checked before publication.
