# Logic, type theory and formal methods as candidate foundations for specifying, composing and governing systems of every scale

Provenance note for the report writer. Findings tagged **[S]** were confirmed against search results in this session (November 2026). Findings tagged **[bg]** are stated from the researcher's background knowledge of the cited primary source; the DOI/URL is the canonical one but was not re-fetched in this session, so exact figures and wording should be spot-checked before being quoted verbatim. Status markers: **(established)** = published theorem or deployed practice; **(aspiration)** = research programme, partial result, or the researcher's synthesis.

## 1. Contract-based and assume-guarantee reasoning: is there a universal meta-theory of contracts and refinement, and what algebraic laws does it give?

### Takeaway
Yes, in a precise but modest sense: Benveniste et al.'s meta-theory (a contract is a pair of sets: admissible environments and admissible implementations) and Incer's algebra of assume-guarantee contracts and hypercontracts give one small set of operators (refinement order, composition, quotient as right adjoint of composition, conjunction/merging) that all concrete contract theories instantiate. It is the best existing "calculus of interfaces", but it is a theory of behavioural interfaces between components; it says nothing natively about authority, obligation-with-violation, or knowledge, and its concrete instances are tractable only for restricted specification classes.

### Cited Findings
- Benveniste, Caillaud, Nickovic, Passerone, Raclet, Reinkemeier, Sangiovanni-Vincentelli, Damm, Henzinger, Larsen, "Contracts for System Design", Foundations and Trends in EDA 12(2-3):124-400, 2018, DOI 10.1561/1000000053. It builds an abstract framework ("meta-theory") from which the methodology's properties are derived and shows how assume/guarantee contracts, interface automata, modal interfaces, synchronous Moore interfaces, rely/guarantee reasoning and scheduling contracts are instances; worked examples are a parking-garage requirements study and AUTOSAR timing contracts. **[S]** (established) — [Now Publishers](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/EDA-053); [AAU record](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/contracts-for-system-design)
- Meta-theory primitives: a class of components with a composition operator and an "environment-of" relation; a contract C is a pair (E_C, M_C) of sets of environments and implementations; refinement C' ≤ C iff M_C' ⊆ M_C and E_C' ⊇ E_C (accept more environments, allow fewer implementations); conjunction is the greatest lower bound in this order; parallel composition is the least contract such that composing implementations yields an implementation and each component's environment obligations are discharged by the other; key theorems are independent implementability (refine each contract separately, composition still refines) and substitutability. **[bg]** (established) — [Now Publishers](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/EDA-053)
- Concrete A/G contracts: a contract is a pair (A, G) of sets of behaviours, put in saturated form (A, G ∪ ¬A). Composition: G = G1 ∩ G2, A = (A1 ∩ A2) ∪ ¬(G1 ∩ G2). Conjunction: A = A1 ∪ A2, G = G1 ∩ G2. Refinement: A ⊆ A', G' ⊆ G. **[bg]** (established) — [Now Publishers](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/EDA-053)
- Incer's 2022 Berkeley dissertation "The Algebra of Contracts" develops the algebra of A/G contracts and introduces hypercontracts, an assume-guarantee theory over pairs of arbitrary hyperproperties (so it can state security properties such as non-interference that trace-set contracts cannot). **[S]** (established) — [EECS-2022-99](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2022/EECS-2022-99.html)
- Incer, Benveniste, Sangiovanni-Vincentelli, Seshia, "Hypercontracts" (2021): the hypercontract model has a richer algebra than the earlier meta-theory for refinement, parallel composition and quotient; the earlier meta-theory is characterised as low-level because it specifies contracts as unstructured sets of environments and implementations; "conic" hypercontracts are generic yet finitely describable. **[S]** (established) — [arXiv 2106.02449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02449); [Berkeley TR](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2021/EECS-2021-158.html)
- Quotient (the "missing component" operator: given a system spec C and a component spec C1, the most permissive C2 with C1 ∥ C2 ≤ C) is the right adjoint of composition; in a Heyting-algebra setting with composition as meet, quotient is the exponential. The A/G quotient first appeared at MEMOCODE 2018. **[S]** (established) — [arXiv 2106.02449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02449)
- A 2023 paper by Incer, Benveniste and Sangiovanni-Vincentelli catalogues the known and new contract operations and how they are related (duality between composition/quotient and merging/separation, etc.). **[S]** (established) — [arXiv 2309.08875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08875)
- Pacti is an open-source Python tool (Incer et al., 2023) implementing A/G contract composition, quotient and merging efficiently by restricting assumptions and guarantees to polyhedral (linear-inequality) constraints, with case studies in aerospace mission design and synthetic biology. **[bg]** (established tool, research-grade adoption) — [Pacti GitHub](https://github.com/pacti-org/pacti); [arXiv 2303.17751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17751)
- Interface automata (de Alfaro and Henzinger, FSE 2001): interfaces as automata with input/output/internal actions and an "optimistic" view: two interfaces are compatible if there exists some environment that keeps them out of error states; refinement is alternating simulation (contravariant on inputs, covariant on outputs); compatibility and refinement are checkable in polynomial time. "Interface Theories for Component-Based Design" (EMSOFT 2001) abstracts this into axioms: independent implementability and incremental design. **[bg]** (established) — [Interface Automata](https://doi.org/10.1145/503209.503226); [Interface Theories](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-45449-7_11)
- Modal transition systems (Larsen and Thomsen, LICS 1988) add "may" and "must" transitions, giving a specification theory where refinement narrows may and preserves must; modal interfaces (Raclet, Badouel, Benveniste, Caillaud, Legay, Passerone, 2011) combine MTS with interface automata and support conjunction and quotient. Modal refinement is polynomial; thorough (semantic) refinement is EXPTIME-complete (Beneš, Křetínský, Larsen, Srba). **[bg]** (established) — [Larsen-Thomsen](https://doi.org/10.1109/LICS.1988.5119); [Modal interfaces](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2011-416); [EXPTIME-completeness](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ic.2012.08.001)
- Rely-guarantee (C. B. Jones, TOPLAS 1983): a concurrent component is specified by (pre, rely, guarantee, post); parallel composition is sound when each thread's guarantee implies the other threads' rely. It is the shared-memory ancestor of A/G contracts. **[bg]** (established) — [Jones 1983](https://doi.org/10.1145/69575.69577)
- Refinement calculus (Back 1978; Morgan; Back and von Wright 1998): programs and specifications live in one complete lattice of monotone predicate transformers ordered by refinement; angelic and demonic choice are joins and meets; a contract is literally a game between an agent and its environment, and "agent can achieve postcondition q" is a winning-strategy statement. This is the oldest worked-out lattice-theoretic "universal" theory of contracts and refinement. **[bg]** (established) — [Back and von Wright, Refinement Calculus](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-1674-2)
- Abadi and Lamport's Composition Theorem ("Conjoining Specifications", TOPLAS 1995) makes circular assume-guarantee reasoning sound: components each guaranteeing M_i as long as the environment satisfies E_i can be composed even when each E_i is established by the others' M_j, provided the assumptions are safety properties and the guarantee holds "one step longer" than the assumption (the operator written E -+> M). **[bg]** (established) — [Conjoining Specifications](https://doi.org/10.1145/203095.201069); [Composing Specifications](https://doi.org/10.1145/151646.151649)

### Inferences
- The algebraic signature that recurs across all these theories is small: a preorder (refinement), a monoidal product (parallel composition) monotone in refinement, a meet (conjunction of viewpoints), and a residual/right adjoint (quotient). That is a residuated lattice / quantale-like structure, which is also the algebraic shape of linear logic and separation logic (tensor and its adjoint "lollipop"/"magic wand"). The convergence suggests that "monoidal preorder with residuation and meets" is the common algebraic kernel of compositional specification, independent of the logic used to write assumptions and guarantees.
- Circular A/G reasoning is unsound for liveness in general; the governance reading is that mutual promises ("I behave if you behave") compose safely only for prohibitions (safety), not for positive obligations to eventually act, unless a well-founded ordering or fairness argument is added.
- Recursion ("a system of systems is a system") is native here: a composed contract is again a contract. What does not scale is checking refinement of rich concrete contracts, not the algebra.

### Gaps
- No source found showing contract algebra applied at organisational or institutional scale (contracts between departments, agencies, or agents with normative content); industrial use is confined to cyber-physical/automotive/aerospace design flows (AUTOSAR, mission design).
- Whether hypercontracts have decidable or tractable refinement for useful classes beyond the "conic" structure was not confirmed in this session.
- Pacti's adoption beyond the authors' groups (Berkeley, Caltech, JPL-related case studies) could not be confirmed.

## 2. Specification and refinement: TLA+, Event-B, Abadi-Lamport, UTP, institution-independent specification

### Takeaway
TLA+ shows that a very small logic (set theory plus one temporal operator, with composition as conjunction, hiding as existential quantification and implementation as implication) is enough to specify industrial distributed systems; UTP and institutions are the two serious attempts at a unifying meta-level, the first by fixing one semantic domain (relations/designs), the second by refusing to fix a logic at all and instead axiomatising what any logic must provide.

### Cited Findings
- TLA+ (Lamport, "Specifying Systems", 2002): a specification is one temporal formula Init ∧ □[Next]_vars ∧ Fairness over untyped ZF set theory; all formulas are stuttering-invariant, which is what makes refinement between abstraction levels expressible as plain implication. Chapter 10 treats composition: parallel composition of components is conjunction of their specifications, hiding internal state is temporal existential quantification, "implements" is logical implication. **[bg]** (established) — [Specifying Systems](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book.html)
- Abadi and Lamport, "The Existence of Refinement Mappings" (TCS 1991): refinement mappings are complete for proving implementation once history and prophecy variables are added, under conditions of machine closure, finite invisible nondeterminism and internal continuity. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1016/0304-3975(91)90224-P](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(91)90224-P)
- Newcombe, Rath, Zhang, Munteanu, Brooker, Deardeuff, "How Amazon Web Services Uses Formal Methods" (CACM 2015): AWS has used TLA+ since 2011 on S3, DynamoDB, EBS and an internal lock manager; model checking found subtle bugs, including a DynamoDB bug whose shortest trace had 35 high-level steps; engineers learned TLA+ in two to three weeks. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/2699417](https://doi.org/10.1145/2699417)
- Brooker and Desai, "Systems Correctness Practices at Amazon Web Services" (CACM/ACM Queue, 2025; DOI 10.1145/3729175): the follow-up reports that many engineers struggled to become productive with TLA+ as adoption spread; AWS has developed the P state-machine language since 2019 as a strategic open-source project; PObserve (2023) checks structured production logs post hoc against the P specification; the portfolio now spans theorem proving and model checking through property-based testing, fuzzing and runtime monitoring. **[S]** (established) — [CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/practice/systems-correctness-practices-at-amazon-web-services); [ACM Queue](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=3712057)
- Event-B (Abrial, "Modeling in Event-B", 2010): machines of guarded events over set-theoretic state, developed by stepwise refinement with generated proof obligations (invariant preservation, guard strengthening, simulation), supported by the Rodin platform; its ancestor the B method was used for the Paris Métro line 14 (Météor) driverless control software, with on the order of 27,800 discharged proof obligations reported by Behm et al. (FM 1999). **[bg]** (established; figure to be spot-checked) — [Modeling in Event-B](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139195881); [Météor](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-48119-2_22)
- Unifying Theories of Programming (Hoare and He, 1998): every program, design and specification in every paradigm is an alphabetised relation (a predicate over observation variables); paradigms are subtheories picked out by "healthiness conditions" (idempotent functions whose fixed points are the valid predicates); refinement is universal reverse implication; theories are linked by Galois connections. **[bg]** (established as theory; limited industrial use) — [UTP site](http://www.unifyingtheories.org)
- Institutions (Goguen and Burstall, JACM 1992): a logical system is a category of signatures, a sentence functor, a model functor and a satisfaction relation obeying the satisfaction condition ("truth is invariant under change of notation"); structuring operations for specifications (union, renaming, hiding, parameterisation) are defined once, independently of the logic. **[S]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/147508.147524](https://doi.org/10.1145/147508.147524); [LFCS report](https://www.lfcs.inf.ed.ac.uk/reports/90/ECS-LFCS-90-106/index.html); [IEP Institution Theory](https://iep.utm.edu/insti-th/)
- Hets, the Heterogeneous Tool Set (Mossakowski, Maeder, Lüttich, TACAS 2007), implements the Grothendieck institution over a graph of logics, treating logic translations (comorphisms) as first-class and providing parsing, static analysis and proof management for heterogeneous specifications; CASL is its central specification language and DOL (OMG) standardises the heterogeneous structuring language. **[S]** for Hets, **[bg]** for DOL (established, academic/ontology use) — [Hets paper](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-71209-1_40); [OMG DOL](https://www.omg.org/spec/DOL/)

### Inferences
- TLA+ is evidence for minimality: a first-order set theory plus a single "always" modality, with composition = conjunction, covers resource, program and service scale. Its limits are equally instructive: no native probability, no native real-time beyond encoding, no ownership/resource discipline, no agents, obligations or authority; refinement proofs for liveness are hard; and the AWS 2025 report shows the human bottleneck (learnability) dominated, pushing practice towards executable state-machine languages and runtime log checking.
- UTP and institutions are opposite answers to "one foundation": UTP says one semantic universe (relations) with many healthiness-condition subtheories; institutions say many logics with a uniform notion of translation. Institutions are the more honest fit for a foundation meant to span programs and norms, because they do not require every domain's logic to embed in one.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found on the size of the largest TLA+ specifications or on TLAPS proof effort at AWS.
- Hets/DOL industrial uptake appears small; no deployment figures found.

## 3. Linear logic and resources: separation logic, Iris, bunched implications, session types, choreographies, typestate

### Takeaway
Substructural logic is the established foundation for resources, ownership and protocols: separation logic gives local reasoning with the frame rule, Iris generalises it to user-defined resources (any resource algebra) and invariants inside a proof assistant, and session types/choreographies give correct-by-construction interaction. These are the strongest candidates for the "resource and interaction" layer of any kernel, with propositional linear logic's undecidability and Iris's proof cost as the main limits.

### Cited Findings
- Linear logic (Girard, TCS 1987) drops weakening and contraction so that hypotheses are consumed exactly once; the exponential "!" reintroduces reusable facts. Full propositional linear logic is undecidable (Lincoln, Mitchell, Scedrov, Shankar, APAL 1992); the multiplicative-additive fragment MALL is PSPACE-complete; decidability of MELL is a long-standing open problem. **[bg]** (established) — [Girard 1987](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(87)90045-4); [Lincoln et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0168-0072(92)90075-B)
- Bunched implications (O'Hearn and Pym, BSL 1999) combine an additive (intuitionistic) and a multiplicative (resource-splitting) conjunction with their two implications; it is the assertion logic underlying separation logic. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.2307/421090](https://doi.org/10.2307/421090)
- Separation logic (Reynolds, LICS 2002): the separating conjunction P * Q asserts P and Q hold of disjoint resources; the frame rule {P} C {Q} ⊢ {P * R} C {Q * R} gives local reasoning. Concurrent separation logic (O'Hearn; Brookes, TCS 2007) adds the parallel rule: threads owning disjoint resources compose without interference. **[bg]** (established) — [Reynolds 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/LICS.2002.1029817); [O'Hearn 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2006.12.035)
- Industrial scale: Meta's Infer static analyser, built on separation logic and bi-abduction, runs compositionally (procedure-at-a-time) on code bases of tens of millions of lines, as reported in "Scaling Static Analyses at Facebook" (CACM 2019) and O'Hearn's "Separation Logic" (CACM 2019). **[bg]** (established) — [Scaling static analyses](https://doi.org/10.1145/3338112); [Separation Logic](https://doi.org/10.1145/3211968)
- Iris (Jung, Krebbers, Jourdan, Bizjak, Birkedal, Dreyer, "Iris from the ground up", JFP 2018): a framework for higher-order concurrent separation logic implemented in Coq and "deployed very effectively in a wide variety of verification projects"; the paper presents Iris 3.1 from first principles. **[S]** (established) — [JFP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-functional-programming/article/iris-from-the-ground-up-a-modular-foundation-for-higherorder-concurrent-separation-logic/26301B518CE2C52796BFA12B8BAB5B5F); [author PDF](https://cs.au.dk/~birke/papers/iris-journal.pdf); [Iris project](https://iris-project.org)
- Iris primitives: (i) resource algebras ("cameras", a generalisation of partial commutative monoids with a validity predicate and a core) as user-chosen ghost state; (ii) invariants, i.e. propositions shared by all threads and openable for one atomic step; (iii) the later modality ▷, a step-index guard needed because invariants and higher-order ghost state are impredicative/self-referential; (iv) the persistence modality □ for duplicable knowledge; (v) the update modality for frame-preserving ghost updates. The base logic is small and the program logic (weakest preconditions, Hoare triples) is derived inside it. **[bg]** (established) — [author PDF](https://cs.au.dk/~birke/papers/iris-journal.pdf)
- RustBelt (Jung, Jourdan, Krebbers, Dreyer, POPL 2018) used Iris to give the first machine-checked safety proof for a realistic subset of Rust including unsafe library code, with ownership and borrowing modelled as a "lifetime logic" of Iris resources. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/3158154](https://doi.org/10.1145/3158154)
- Session types and multiparty session types (Honda, Yoshida, Carbone, POPL 2008; JACM 2016): a global type describes a protocol among roles; projection yields a local type per role; well-typed processes enjoy communication safety, protocol fidelity and progress within a session. **[bg]** (established) — [POPL 2008](https://doi.org/10.1145/1328438.1328472); [JACM 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2827695)
- Soundness history: Scalas and Yoshida, "Less is More: Multiparty Session Types Revisited" (POPL 2019) reported that classic MPST had limited subject reduction and that this led to flawed type systems in earlier work, proposing a bottom-up approach checking properties of local types directly; Yoshida and Hou, "Less is More Revisited" (arXiv 2024; journal version in TCS 2026 with Kuhn) show via an association relation between global and local types that end-point projection with mergeability is sound and guarantees session fidelity, deadlock freedom and liveness from global types. **[S]** (established; note the correction history) — [Less is More](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290343); [Less is More Revisited](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16741v3)
- Logical foundation: session types correspond to intuitionistic linear logic propositions (Caires and Pfenning, CONCUR 2010) and to classical linear logic (Wadler, "Propositions as Sessions", ICFP 2012); cut elimination is communication, giving deadlock freedom as a theorem of the logic. **[bg]** (established) — [Caires-Pfenning](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-15375-4_16); [Wadler](https://doi.org/10.1145/2364527.2364568)
- Choreographies (Carbone and Montesi, "Deadlock-freedom-by-design", POPL 2013; Montesi, "Introduction to Choreographies", CUP 2023): a single global program is compiled by endpoint projection into distributed implementations that are deadlock-free by construction. **[bg]** (established as theory and research languages; limited industrial adoption) — [POPL 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2429069.2429101); [Montesi 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108981491)
- Limit: verification of unrestricted communicating finite-state machines over unbounded FIFO channels is undecidable (Brand and Zafiropulo, JACM 1983), which is why session-type systems restrict protocol shape. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/322374.322380](https://doi.org/10.1145/322374.322380)
- Typestate (Strom and Yemini, IEEE TSE 1986): the set of operations permitted on an object depends on its current state, tracked statically; it is the single-object counterpart of session types. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1109/TSE.1986.6312929](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.1986.6312929)

### Inferences
- Iris's "resource algebra + invariant + later + persistence + update" is the most general published account of ownership: money, permissions, tokens, capabilities, protocol states and delegated shares are all resource algebras. It is a plausible template for a governance kernel's resource layer, but everything in Iris is proved interactively, by experts, about one program at a time.
- Session types and choreographies give correctness by construction only for closed, statically known sets of roles and protocols without failure or defection. Open systems with dynamically joining, possibly non-compliant participants (the organisational case) are outside the guarantees; there, monitoring against the local type is the fallback.
- The separation-logic frame rule and the contract-algebra independent-implementability theorem are the same kind of result: what makes composition scale is that a local proof stays valid in any larger context that respects the declared footprint.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of multiparty session types or choreographies deployed at large industrial scale; adoption appears to be research toolchains.
- No published end-to-end cost figures for Iris-based verifications were retrieved.

## 4. Dependent type theory and proof assistants; large-scale verification achievements and their real cost

### Takeaway
Dependent type theory is the only candidate that is simultaneously a specification language, a programming language and a proof calculus with a small trusted kernel, and it has carried the largest verified artefacts (seL4, CompCert). The real cost has been roughly an order of magnitude more proof than code and person-decades of expert effort, with residual bugs concentrated in specifications and unverified interfaces; 2025-2026 AI-assisted proving is reducing proof labour, and the bottleneck is shifting to specification.

### Cited Findings
- Propositions as types: propositions correspond to types and proofs to programs, so one calculus serves for specifying and constructing (Wadler, CACM 2015). Lean 4 (de Moura and Ullrich, CADE 2021), Coq/Rocq and Agda implement variants of dependent type theory with small proof-checking kernels. Homotopy type theory adds the univalence axiom (equivalent types are equal) and higher inductive types. **[bg]** (established) — [Propositions as Types](https://doi.org/10.1145/2699407); [Lean 4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-79876-5_37); [HoTT book](https://homotopytypetheory.org/book/)
- seL4 (Klein et al., SOSP 2009; TOCS 2014): machine-checked proof in Isabelle/HOL that the C implementation refines the abstract specification. The proof is about 200,000 lines of Isabelle script for about 7,500 lines of C in the original verification (source counts vary between 7,500 and 8,700 lines of C). **[S]** (established) — [Trustworthy Systems numbers](https://trustworthy.systems/projects/OLD/l4.verified/numbers); [SOSP 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1629575.1629596); [TOCS 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2560537)
- seL4 cost: about 25 person-years for design, documentation, implementation and verification in total; about 11 person-years for the initial functional-correctness proof excluding reusable frameworks; about USD 700 per line of code taking all 25 person-years; the team estimated a repeat for a similar code base at 8-10 person-years; all seL4-related proof files were about 500,000 lines by 2015 and the proof base has since grown to well over a million lines. **[S]** (established) — [Trustworthy Systems numbers](https://trustworthy.systems/projects/OLD/l4.verified/numbers); [Heiser 2026](https://www.trustworthy.systems/publications/papers/Heiser_26.pdf); [Heiser blog](https://microkerneldude.org/2016/06/16/verified-software-can-and-will-be-cheaper-than-buggy-stuff/)
- CompCert (Leroy, CACM 2009): a C compiler verified in Coq to preserve semantics; the Csmith random-testing study (Yang, Chen, Eide, Regehr, PLDI 2011) found wrong-code bugs in every compiler tested except in CompCert's verified middle end, the only bugs being in unverified parts. **[bg]** (established) — [Leroy 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1538788.1538814); [Csmith](https://doi.org/10.1145/1993498.1993532)
- IronFleet (Hawblitzel et al., SOSP 2015) verified a Paxos-based replicated state machine and a sharded key-value store in Dafny, including liveness; Verdi (Wilcox et al., PLDI 2015) verified Raft in Coq using verified system transformers across fault models. **[bg]** (established) — [IronFleet](https://doi.org/10.1145/2815400.2815428); [Verdi](https://doi.org/10.1145/2737924.2737958)
- Fonseca, Zhang, Wang, Krishnamurthy, "An Empirical Study on the Correctness of Formally Verified Distributed Systems" (EuroSys 2017) found 16 bugs in three verified systems (IronFleet, Verdi, Chapar), none in verified code and all in the trusted base: incomplete or wrong specifications, unverified shim/OS-interface layers, and verification tools. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/3064176.3064183](https://doi.org/10.1145/3064176.3064183)
- Amazon s2n: the HMAC and DRBG code of the s2n TLS library is proved against specifications with SAW/Cryptol/Coq and the proofs are replayed in continuous integration on every change (Chudnov et al., CAV 2018). **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-96142-2_26](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-96142-2_26)
- AWS automated reasoning at scale: Zelkova translates IAM policies to SMT to answer questions such as "is this bucket publicly accessible" (Backes et al., FMCAD 2018); Rungta, "A Billion SMT Queries a Day" (CAV 2022) reports that order of daily volume. **[bg]** (established) — [Zelkova](https://doi.org/10.23919/FMCAD.2018.8602994); [Billion SMT queries](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-13185-1_1)
- Cedar: "How We Built Cedar: A Verification-Guided Approach" (Disselkoen, Eline, He, Headley, Hicks et al., FSE 2024 industry track): an executable Lean model of Cedar is proved to satisfy key correctness and security properties, and differential random testing checks the Rust production code against the model; proofs found and fixed 4 soundness bugs in the policy validator, and differential and property-based testing found 21 bugs in the parser, evaluator, authorizer and validator. An earlier version of the model was written in Dafny. **[S]** (established) — [arXiv 2407.01688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01688v1); [FSE 2024 listing](https://2024.esec-fse.org/details/fse-2024-industry/33/How-We-Built-Cedar-A-Verification-Guided-Approach); [cedar-policy](https://github.com/cedar-policy)
- AI-assisted proof (2026): AutoReal (Zhejiang University, Feb 2026) reports 51.67% proof success on 660 seL4 theorems with a 7B-parameter locally deployable prover, against 27.06% for prior attempts; this is a benchmark result, not an end-to-end verification. A May 2026 Runtime Verification experience report describes a Rust-to-Lean pipeline with AI provers and stresses that every proof is checked by the Lean kernel so AI output cannot compromise soundness. **[S]** (aspiration / early-stage) — [arXiv 2602.08384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08384); [Rust-to-Lean report](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.30106.md)
- A secondary report (not confirmed against a primary source in this session) says Google DeepMind published work on 10 November 2026 in which AI agents produced about 6.5 million lines of Lean proof about the compiled machine code of Signal's libsignal, and that both parties named specification, not proof, as the remaining bottleneck. **[S, secondary only: treat as unverified]** — [letsdatascience report](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/deepmind-agents-wrote-6-5-million-lines-of-proof-for-signal)

### Inferences
- The proof-to-code ratio (roughly 20:1 or more for seL4) and the Fonseca study together say: a type-theoretic kernel makes the proved part essentially bug-free, but relocates risk to the specification and the boundary with the unverified world. For governing organisations, where the "specification" is contested social intent, this boundary problem is the dominant one.
- The small-kernel architecture (de Bruijn criterion: untrusted, arbitrarily clever proof search; tiny trusted checker) is exactly the pattern needed if untrusted AI agents are to produce governance artefacts: the agent proposes, a small kernel checks. The 2026 results make this economically plausible for code; nothing comparable exists yet for norms.
- The most widely deployed formal reasoning (Zelkova, Cedar analysis, Infer) is automated, restricted and domain-specific, not general dependent type theory. Generality and scale have so far traded off against each other.

### Gaps
- Precise CompCert and IronFleet effort figures (person-years, proof-to-code ratios) were not re-verified in this session.
- The DeepMind/libsignal claim needs a primary source.
- No source found for a verified artefact above the scale of a kernel, compiler, cryptographic library or single distributed protocol; there is no verified "enterprise".

## 5. Normative and social logics: obligation, rights, authority, delegation, agency, group ability, knowledge, commitments, contract languages

### Takeaway
Every governance concept has a logic, but they are a family of mostly independent modal systems with different semantics, and the well-behaved ones in practice are the computationally weak ones: Datalog-style authorization (SecPAL, Cedar, Zanzibar), defeasible rule systems (Catala, defeasible deontic logic) and event-based contract languages. Expressive agency logics (STIT, strategy logic, epistemic logic with common knowledge or imperfect information) reach high complexity or undecidability quickly.

### Cited Findings
- Standard deontic logic (SDL) is the normal modal logic KD with O (obligatory), P (permitted), F (forbidden). Its known paradoxes include Ross's paradox, the Good Samaritan, and Chisholm's contrary-to-duty paradox (what one ought to do given a violation cannot be represented consistently and independently in SDL). **[bg]** (established) — [SEP Deontic Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-deontic/)
- Input/output logic (Makinson and van der Torre, J. Phil. Logic 2000) originates in the study of conditional norms and treats norms as pairs (condition, obligation) that are not truth-bearing; logic's task is the "modest" one of processing inputs through a normative code; constraining outputs to be consistent with the input is its mechanism for contrary-to-duty obligations; three notions of permission are distinguished. **[S]** (established) — [Makinson, van der Torre, "What is Input/Output Logic?"](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/DagSemProc.07122.32); [original paper](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1004748624537)
- Parent and van der Torre (DEON 2018) add a consistency check to I/O operations, preventing Prakken and Sergot's "pragmatic oddity" and Sergot's "drowning problem". **[S]** (established) — [DEON 2018 paper](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/37887/1/Pages%20from%20DEON2018.pdf)
- Defeasible deontic logic (Governatori and co-authors) represents norms as prioritised defeasible rules with obligation and permission modalities and a compensation operator for contrary-to-duty chains; propositional defeasible logic has linear-time consequence computation (Maher, TPLP 2001), which is why it is used for business-process compliance checking. **[bg]** (established) — [Maher 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1471068401001168); [Governatori et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10992-013-9295-1)
- Hohfeld's jural relations: four correlative pairs (claim-right/duty, privilege/no-right, power/liability, immunity/disability); first-order relations concern actions, second-order ones (power, immunity) concern changing others' normative positions. Formalisations: the Kanger-Lindahl theory of normative positions, developed computationally by Sergot; Jones and Sergot, "A Formal Characterisation of Institutionalised Power" (1996) formalise power with a "counts-as" conditional relative to an institution; Markovich (Studia Logica 2020) gives a recent formal account. **[bg]** (established as theory; no dominant standard) — [SEP Rights](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights/); [Jones and Sergot 1996](https://doi.org/10.1093/jigpal/4.3.427); [Markovich 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11225-019-09870-5)
- Authority and delegation: Lampson, Abadi, Burrows, Wobber (TOCS 1992) and Abadi, Burrows, Lampson, Plotkin (TOPLAS 1993) introduce "A says s" and "A speaks for B" (if A says s then B says s), giving a calculus in which delegation chains are proofs. **[S]** for the speaks-for definition, **[bg]** for citations (established) — [ABLP 1993](https://doi.org/10.1145/155183.155225); [Lampson et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1145/138873.138874)
- Abadi's tutorial "Logic in Access Control" surveys logical foundations and policy languages; Garg and Abadi, "A Modal Deconstruction of Access Control Logics" (FoSSaCS 2008) give a sound and complete translation of a "says" logic into classical modal logic S4 and show speaks-for is definable. "says" behaves as a lax modality (a monad), as in Abadi's Dependency Core Calculus treatment. **[S]** (established) — [Garg-Abadi](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-540-78499-9_16.pdf); [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=145152); [Logic in Access Control tutorial notes](https://www.springerprofessional.de/logic-in-access-control-tutorial-notes/3170610)
- SecPAL (Becker, Fournet, Gordon, JCS 2010) is a decentralised authorization language with "says", constrained delegation ("can say") and "can act as" (resembling speaks-for), with semantics by translation to constrained Datalog so that queries are decidable and tractable. **[S]** for the can-act-as/speaks-for relation, **[bg]** for the rest (established) — [SecPAL](https://doi.org/10.3233/JCS-2009-0364); [Fibred Security Language paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/31200065.pdf)
- Proof-carrying authorization (Appel and Felten, CCS 1999) and Nexus Authorization Logic (Schneider, Walsh, Sirer, TISSEC 2011) have the requester supply a checkable proof of authority in a higher-order/constructive logic; the guard only checks proofs. **[bg]** (established research systems) — [Appel-Felten](https://doi.org/10.1145/319709.319718); [NAL](https://doi.org/10.1145/1952982.1952988)
- Limit: in the general Harrison-Ruzzo-Ullman access-matrix model the safety question ("can subject s ever obtain right r?") is undecidable (CACM 1976); decidability returns only for restricted models. **[bg]** (established) — [HRU 1976](https://doi.org/10.1145/360303.360333)
- Zanzibar (Pang et al., USENIX ATC 2019): Google's global relationship-based authorization system stores relation tuples (object#relation@user) with userset rewrite rules, serves trillions of ACLs and millions of checks per second with low-millisecond latency, and uses consistency tokens ("zookies") over Spanner snapshots to respect causal ordering of ACL updates (the "new enemy" problem). **[bg]** (established; the session search did not re-confirm the figures) — [USENIX ATC 2019](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc19/presentation/pang)
- Cedar: a policy language with permit/forbid policies, default deny and forbid-overrides, designed to be analyzable by SMT and proved in Lean to satisfy its key authorization properties (see section 4). **[S]** (established) — [arXiv 2407.01688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01688v1)
- STIT ("sees to it that") logic (Belnap, Perloff, Xu) models agency in branching time: an agent's choice partitions the histories through a moment and [i stit φ] holds when the choice guarantees φ; Horty's deontic STIT places obligation on choices. Group STIT with unboundedly many agents is undecidable and not finitely axiomatisable (Herzig and Schwarzentruber). **[bg]** (established) — [SEP Logic of Action](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-action/)
- Coalition logic (Pauly, J. Logic Comput. 2002) has a modality [C]φ ("coalition C can enforce φ in one step") axiomatised via effectivity functions; ATL (Alur, Henzinger, Kupferman, JACM 2002) adds temporal operators. **[bg]** (established) — [Pauly 2002](https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/12.1.149); [ATL](https://doi.org/10.1145/585265.585270)
- Complexity of "what groups can enforce": ATL model checking with perfect information is PTIME-complete; ATL* with perfect information and memoryful strategies is 2EXPTIME-complete; with imperfect information ATL with memoryless strategies is Δ2^P-complete and with perfect recall it is undecidable. Strategy Logic model checking is polynomial in the model but non-elementary in the formula (number of exponentials grows with quantifier alternation), its satisfiability is undecidable, and the fragment SL[1G], which strictly subsumes ATL*, is 2EXPTIME-complete. **[S]** (established; the imperfect-information cells come from one survey and should be checked against primaries) — [survey arXiv 2310.02342](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02342); [Mogavero et al., arXiv 1112.6275](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.6275); [arXiv 1202.1309](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.1309)
- Epistemic logic: satisfiability for multi-agent S5_n is PSPACE-complete and becomes EXPTIME-complete with common knowledge (Halpern and Moses, AIJ 1992); common knowledge cannot be attained in systems without guaranteed synchronous communication (Halpern and Moses, JACM 1990); combining knowledge and time with perfect recall can be non-elementary or Π^1_1-complete (Halpern and Vardi, JCSS 1989). **[bg]** (established) — [Halpern-Moses 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(92)90049-4); [Halpern-Moses 1990](https://doi.org/10.1145/79147.79161); [Halpern-Vardi 1989](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(89)90039-1)
- Dynamic epistemic logic models announcements and information-changing events as model updates (event models and product update). **[bg]** (established) — [SEP Dynamic Epistemic Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dynamic-epistemic/)
- BDI: Cohen and Levesque, "Intention is Choice with Commitment" (AIJ 1990) and the Rao-Georgeff BDI logics model belief, desire and intention as modalities over branching time; in practice BDI architectures execute plans without using the logic for verification. **[bg]** (established as theory) — [Cohen-Levesque](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(90)90055-5)
- Social commitments (Singh, AI and Law 1999): C(debtor, creditor, antecedent, consequent) with operations create, discharge, cancel, release, delegate, assign; commitments are public and observable, unlike mental states, so compliance can be judged from the interaction record. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1023/A:1008319631231](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008319631231)
- Contract languages: Peyton Jones, Eber, Seward, "Composing Contracts" (ICFP 2000) define financial contracts from about ten combinators (zero, one, give, and, or, scale, when, anytime, until, ...) with compositional valuation semantics; CSL (Andersen, Elsborg, Henglein, Simonsen, Stefansen, STTT 2006) specifies commercial contracts compositionally over event traces with residuation (the contract that remains after an event); Symboleo (Sharifi et al., RE 2020) is a legal-contract specification language with obligations, powers and event-calculus-based semantics. **[bg]** (established) — [Composing Contracts](https://doi.org/10.1145/351240.351267); [CSL](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10009-006-0010-1); [Symboleo](https://doi.org/10.1109/RE48521.2020.00049)
- Catala (Merigoux, Chataing, Protzenko, ICFP 2021): a language for legislation whose core is prioritised default logic (general rule plus exceptions, mirroring statutory drafting), with a formalised semantics; it expressed section 121 of the US Internal Revenue Code and the entire French family-benefits computation, and uncovered a bug in the official French implementation. **[S]** (established) — [arXiv 2103.03198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03198v2)
- CUTECat (2024/2025) applies concolic execution to Catala, generating 186,390 tests for the French housing-benefits code, and notes that translations of legal text into code are legal interpretations that can be erroneous. **[S]** (established) — [arXiv 2410.18212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18212v2)
- LegalRuleML is an OASIS standard XML vocabulary for legal rules (defeasibility, deontic operators, provenance, temporal validity); the Accord Project provides open-source smart legal contract templates. Both are interchange/engineering formats without a single fixed proof theory. **[bg]** (established as standards/projects) — [LegalRuleML](https://docs.oasis-open.org/legalruleml/legalruleml-core-spec/v1.0/legalruleml-core-spec-v1.0.html); [Accord Project](https://accordproject.org)

### Inferences
- The expressiveness table for the report: obligation/permission (SDL, I/O logic, defeasible deontic logic), rights/powers (Hohfeld via normative positions and counts-as), authority/delegation (says/speaks-for, SecPAL, Cedar, Zanzibar), individual agency (STIT), group ability (coalition logic, ATL, strategy logic), knowledge (epistemic, DEL), mental states (BDI), inter-agent obligation as resource-like object (commitments), time (LTL/CTL/TLA), resources (linear/separation), probability (PCTL, section 7). No single deployed system covers more than two or three of these.
- The lesson of deontic logic for a kernel is negative and specific: obligation is not a normal truth-functional modality over propositions. The working treatments make norms first-class non-truth-bearing objects (I/O logic), prioritised defeasible rules (Catala, Governatori), or state-machine-like objects with lifecycle (commitments, CSL residuation). All three are compatible with "norm = typed data plus an operational/monitor semantics" rather than "norm = modal formula".
- Hohfeldian power and immunity are second-order (they change normative positions), matching delegation ("can say" in SecPAL) and the HRU undecidability result: once agents can create and transfer rights without restriction, global safety questions become undecidable. Practical systems therefore restrict delegation to Datalog-like monotone forms.
- Authorization is the one normative area with proven planetary scale (Zanzibar, IAM/Zelkova, Cedar), and it achieved that by being decidable, monotone or stratified, default-deny and locally evaluable.

- No Stanford Encyclopedia text was retrieved in this session for deontic logic, STIT or Hohfeld; statements rest on background knowledge of those entries.
- No mature mechanised, widely used formalisation of the full Hohfeldian scheme was found; no evidence of production use of Symboleo, CSL or LegalRuleML at scale was retrieved.
- Catala's production adoption status inside the French administration as of 2026 was not confirmed.

## 6. Runtime verification, monitoring, shielding and enforcement: which governance policies are enforceable at runtime?

### Takeaway
A monitor that only observes one execution and can only halt it enforces at most safety properties (Schneider); monitors that can also suppress, insert or buffer actions enforce more (edit automata: "infinite renewal" properties); information-flow and fairness-style policies are hyperproperties, not enforceable by observing a single trace, though k-safety hyperproperties reduce to safety on self-composed systems. Liveness obligations ("X must eventually happen") are never enforceable by prevention alone; they need deadlines (turning them into safety) or sanctions/compensation.

### Cited Findings
- Schneider, "Enforceable Security Policies" (TISSEC 2000) is the first major characterisation of what execution monitoring can enforce: the class EM is a subset of the safety properties in the Alpern-Schneider sense, with monitors modelled as security automata that halt the target. **[S]** (established) — [Schneider 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/353323.353382); [Princeton TR discussing EM](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/techreports/2002/649.pdf)
- Alpern and Schneider, "Defining Liveness" (IPL 1985): every trace property is the intersection of a safety property and a liveness property. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1016/0020-0190(85)90056-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(85)90056-0)
- Edit automata (Ligatti, Bauer, Walker) transform the action sequence when it deviates from policy, by inserting and suppressing actions as well as truncating; they are strictly more powerful than truncation automata, and the follow-up "Run-Time Enforcement of Nonsafety Policies" shows they enforce strictly more than safety, including infinite renewal properties. **[S]** (established) — [Edit automata TR](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~dpw/papers/editauto-tr-720-05.pdf); [Nonsafety policies](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~dpw/papers/nonsafety05.pdf)
- Hamlen, Morrisett, Schneider, "Computability Classes for Enforcement Mechanisms" (TOPLAS 2006) relate static analysis, execution monitoring and program rewriting to computability classes: EM-enforceable policies must additionally be co-recursively-enumerable (violations detectable in finite time), and program rewriting enforces a class incomparable to EM. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/1111596.1111601](https://doi.org/10.1145/1111596.1111601)
- Basin, Jugé, Klaedtke, Zălinescu, "Enforceable Security Policies Revisited" (TISSEC 2013) distinguish controllable actions from only-observable ones (e.g. the passage of time cannot be blocked) and characterise enforceability accordingly. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/2487222.2487224](https://doi.org/10.1145/2487222.2487224)
- Clarkson and Schneider, "Hyperproperties" (JCS 2010): a hyperproperty is a set of sets of traces; non-interference and many confidentiality and service-level policies are hyperproperties and not trace properties; every hyperproperty is the intersection of a hypersafety and a hyperliveness hyperproperty; k-safety hyperproperties can be reduced to safety properties of a k-fold self-composition. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.3233/JCS-2009-0393](https://doi.org/10.3233/JCS-2009-0393)
- HyperLTL (Clarkson, Finkbeiner, Koleini, Micinski, Rabe, Sánchez, POST 2014) adds trace quantifiers to LTL; model checking is decidable (PSPACE-complete in the formula for the alternation-free fragment, non-elementary in the number of quantifier alternations in general) and satisfiability is undecidable in general. **[bg]** (established) — [Temporal Logics for Hyperproperties](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-54792-8_15); [Algorithms for Model Checking HyperLTL](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-21690-4_3)
- LTL monitorability (Bauer, Leucker, Schallhart, TOSEM 2011): a three-valued semantics (true/false/inconclusive) on finite prefixes; some properties (e.g. "infinitely often p") are non-monitorable because no finite prefix ever yields a verdict. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/2000799.2000800](https://doi.org/10.1145/2000799.2000800)
- Shielding (Alshiekh, Bloem, Ehlers, Könighofer, Niekum, Topcu, AAAI 2018): a shield synthesised from a temporal-logic safety specification and an abstraction of the environment monitors a learning agent and corrects actions only when a violation would otherwise occur. **[bg]** (established research technique) — [arXiv 1708.08611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)
- Industrial runtime checking against formal specifications: AWS PObserve validates production logs post hoc against P specifications. **[S]** (established) — [CACM 2025](https://cacm.acm.org/practice/systems-correctness-practices-at-amazon-web-services)

### Inferences
- Classification of governance policies by enforceability, for the report:
  - Preventable in-line by a reference monitor: safety properties over controllable actions with finitely detectable violations (access control, rate limits, spending caps, separation of duty over a recorded history, "never do X without prior approval Y").
  - Enforceable with action rewriting/buffering: transactional and renewal properties (hold outputs until a commit condition is met; "every request is eventually logged" if the monitor can insert the log action).
  - Enforceable only on restructured systems: k-safety hyperproperties such as non-interference (self-composition, secure multi-execution, static typing).
  - Detectable but not preventable: bounded-liveness/deadline obligations (violation is observable when the deadline passes, since time is uncontrollable); these need sanctions or compensation, which is exactly the deontic contrary-to-duty structure.
  - Neither detectable nor preventable at runtime: pure liveness (eventual service, eventual fairness), hyperliveness (some possibilistic secrecy conditions), and properties of unobservable state (intent, knowledge, belief).
- Shields need a model of the environment; their guarantee is relative to that model, so for open multi-agent systems they inherit the assume-guarantee structure of section 1.
- For 10^10 actors the only enforcement that scales trivially is local: a per-actor monitor on a safety policy whose state depends only on that actor's observable history. Global policies (aggregate caps, cross-actor separation of duty) need shared state and inherit distributed-consistency costs.

### Gaps
- Clarkson-Schneider and HyperLTL details were not surfaced by this session's search; they are stated from background knowledge.
- No general theory was found of enforcement by sanction/incentive (as opposed to prevention) integrated with the Schneider hierarchy; this sits between formal methods and mechanism design.

## 7. Probabilistic and quantitative verification; parameterised verification and what it implies for 10^10 actors

### Takeaway
Verification of systems with an arbitrary number of identical processes is undecidable in general (Apt-Kozen, Suzuki); decidability returns only for restricted communication topologies and primitives through cut-offs and well-structured-systems arguments, often at very high complexity. So control of 10^10 actors cannot come from global model checking: it must come from compositional local proofs, symmetry and cut-offs, or statistical and mean-field abstractions with only probabilistic guarantees.

### Cited Findings
- Apt and Kozen (IPL 1986) proved parameterised model checking undecidable in general; Suzuki (IPL 1988) proved it undecidable even for rings of identical finite-state processes passing a token, by reduction from Turing-machine non-halting; proofs typically simulate two-counter machines. **[S]** (established) — [Bloem et al. survey](https://finkbeiner.groups.cispa.de/publications/BJKKRVW16.pdf); [Apt-Kozen](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(86)90071-2); [Suzuki](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(88)90211-6)
- Cut-off results: Emerson and Namjoshi give cut-offs for uni-directional token rings for indexed CTL* without the next operator (checking small rings suffices for all sizes); German and Sistla (JACM 1992) show a cut-off exists for control-process-plus-identical-users systems against indexed LTL. Where no cut-off exists the problem is in general undecidable. **[S]** (established) — [Aminof et al., arXiv 1311.4425](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1311.4425); [arXiv 1407.7305](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.7305); [Emerson-Namjoshi CAV 1996](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-61474-5_60.pdf); [German-Sistla](https://doi.org/10.1145/146637.146681)
- The standard reference is Bloem, Jacobs, Khalimov, Konnov, Rubin, Veith, Widder, "Decidability of Parameterized Verification" (Synthesis Lectures, 2015), organised by communication model: token passing, rendezvous, broadcast, guarded protocols, ad hoc networks. Esparza's "Keeping a Crowd Safe" (STACS 2014) surveys complexity for parameterised programs without identities. **[S]** (established) — [Book page](https://finkbeiner.groups.cispa.de/publications/BJKKRVW15.html); [Esparza STACS 2014](https://archive.model.in.tum.de/um/bibdb/esparza/stacs2014.pdf)
- Broadcast protocols (Esparza, Finkel, Mayr, LICS 1999): safety (coverability) is decidable via well-structured transition systems, liveness is undecidable; the complexity of coverability with broadcasts is non-primitive-recursive (Ackermannian). **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1109/LICS.1999.782630](https://doi.org/10.1109/LICS.1999.782630)
- Probabilistic model checking: PRISM (Kwiatkowska, Norman, Parker, CAV 2011) and Storm (Hensel, Junges, Katoen, Quatmann, Volk, STTT 2022) verify DTMCs, CTMCs, MDPs and extensions against PCTL/CSL and reward properties. PCTL model checking is polynomial in the model for Markov chains and MDPs (Hansson and Jonsson 1994); LTL model checking of MDPs is 2EXPTIME-complete in the formula (Courcoubetis and Yannakakis, JACM 1995). The practical limit is state-space explosion. **[bg]** (established) — [PRISM 4.0](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-22110-1_47); [Storm](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10009-021-00633-z); [Hansson-Jonsson](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01211866); [Courcoubetis-Yannakakis](https://doi.org/10.1145/210332.210339)
- Statistical model checking (Legay, Delahaye, Bensalem, RV 2010 overview) estimates the probability that a property holds by simulation and hypothesis testing, with cost independent of state-space size but giving only statistical confidence, with difficulty on rare events and nondeterminism. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1007/978-3-642-16612-9_11](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-16612-9_11)
- Mean-field/fluid approximation (Bortolussi, Hillston, Latella, Massink, Perf. Eval. 2013) replaces a large population of identical stochastic agents by ordinary differential equations over occupancy fractions, exact in the limit of infinite population, allowing properties of one agent in a very large population to be checked against the limit model. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1016/j.peva.2013.01.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.peva.2013.01.001)
- Compositional static analysis at scale (Infer) and SMT-based policy analysis at about a billion queries a day (AWS) are the existing proofs that per-component, automated, restricted reasoning scales to very large populations of artefacts. **[bg]** (established) — [Scaling static analyses](https://doi.org/10.1145/3338112); [Billion SMT queries](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-13185-1_1)

### Inferences
- Implication for 10^10 actors: (a) there is no algorithm for "check the whole population" for general interaction patterns; (b) where actors are anonymous and interact through restricted primitives (tokens, pairwise rendezvous, guarded commands), a property proved for a small cut-off instance holds for all sizes, so population size is irrelevant; (c) heterogeneous actors with identities and rich communication fall outside all known decidable classes; (d) therefore the architecture must make global properties follow from local contracts by a composition theorem (sections 1 and 3), restrict interaction topology by design (hierarchy, bounded fan-out, typed protocols), and accept statistical guarantees for aggregate behaviour.
- Symmetry is the scaling resource: governance designed so that actors of one role are interchangeable (same contract, same monitor) allows one proof per role instead of one per actor. This is how Zanzibar/Cedar/IAM scale: one evaluation semantics, proved once, applied per request.
- Quantitative guarantees compose worse than qualitative ones: probabilistic assume-guarantee reasoning exists but is much less developed than the Boolean theory; this is a real gap for agent systems whose components are stochastic (LLM agents).

### Gaps
- No source retrieved in this session on probabilistic/stochastic contract theories (e.g. probabilistic A/G contracts) or their maturity.
- No result found that directly addresses parameterised verification of heterogeneous, hierarchical, self-modifying populations, which is the actual target scenario.

## 8. The smallest logical kernel, and why experts say there is no single universal logic. Comparison of candidates.

### Takeaway
No single logic is both universal and tractable: combining modalities for time, knowledge, agency and obligation is well-defined (fusion, fibring, institutions) but interaction between them quickly produces undecidability, and deontic paradoxes show some notions resist the modal mould. The defensible position is a small meta-kernel (a dependent type theory or logical framework with a tiny trusted checker) in which substructural resources and modalities are defined as libraries or modes, plus decidable, domain-restricted fragments for anything checked at scale. The most minimal general algebraic kernel is the contract/residuated-lattice algebra; the most general expressive kernel is dependent type theory with a resource (separation/linear) layer.

### Cited Findings
- The SEP entry "Combining Logics" (Carnielli and Coniglio) presents the study of logic combination as a reflex of the pluralist character of contemporary logic and takes "why are there so many logics instead of just one?" as a central question of the philosophy of logic. **[S]** (established) — [SEP Combining Logics](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2014/entries/logic-combining)
- Fibring (Gabbay) is a general-purpose combination technique; in the simplest case fibring yields the least extension of both logics over the coproduct of their signatures. **[S]** (established) — [Asymmetric combination of logics survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.04170)
- The "collapsing problem": naive fibring of, for example, classical and intuitionistic logic collapses the weaker connectives into the stronger; Caleiro and Ramos, "From fibring to cryptofibring: a solution to the collapsing problem" (Logica Universalis 2007) is the standard reference. **[S]** (existence of the problem and reference confirmed via bibliography only) — [SEP Combining Logics bibliography](https://philpapers.org/sep/logic-combining/)
- Logical pluralism (Beall and Restall) holds that there is more than one correct consequence relation. **[bg]** (philosophical position, contested) — [SEP Logical Pluralism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logical-pluralism/)
- Combination results: fusions (independent joins) of decidable modal logics stay decidable, but products and logics with interaction axioms often do not; e.g. knowledge plus time with perfect recall and no learning can be Π^1_1-complete (Halpern and Vardi 1989); strategy logic and imperfect-information ATL with recall are non-elementary or undecidable (section 5). **[bg]/[S]** (established) — [Halpern-Vardi](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(89)90039-1); [survey arXiv 2310.02342](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02342)
- Institutions answer plurality structurally: they abstract from the syntax and semantics of any particular logic so that specification structuring is logic-independent, and can be combined; Hets implements a Grothendieck institution over a graph of logics with translations as first-class citizens. **[S]** (established) — [LFCS report](https://www.lfcs.inf.ed.ac.uk/reports/90/ECS-LFCS-90-106/index.html); [Hets](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-71209-1_40)
- Logical frameworks answer it by encoding: LF (Harper, Honsell, Plotkin, JACM 1993) represents object logics in a dependently typed λ-calculus via judgments-as-types. **[bg]** (established) — [doi:10.1145/138027.138060](https://doi.org/10.1145/138027.138060)
- Ingredients for a combined kernel that already exist as published type theories: linear plus dependent types (Cervesato and Pfenning's linear logical framework; Krishnaswami, Pradic, Benton, POPL 2015); quantitative type theory with usage-annotated variables (Atkey, LICS 2018) implemented in Idris 2; multimodal dependent type theory parameterised by a mode theory of modalities (Gratzer, Kavvos, Nuyts, Birkedal, LICS 2020); authorization proofs in a dependently typed language with "says" as a monad (Aura: Jia et al., ICFP 2008); separation logic with user-defined resources embedded in Coq (Iris). **[bg]** (each established individually; their union is an aspiration) — [LLF](https://doi.org/10.1006/inco.2001.2951); [Integrating linear and dependent types](https://doi.org/10.1145/2676726.2676969); [QTT](https://doi.org/10.1145/3209108.3209189); [MTT](https://doi.org/10.1145/3373718.3394736); [Aura](https://doi.org/10.1145/1411204.1411212); [Iris](https://iris-project.org)
- Evidence that one kernel can host verified governance artefacts: Cedar's semantics and validator soundness are proved in Lean, with production code tied to the model by differential testing. **[S]** (established) — [arXiv 2407.01688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01688v1)

- Most minimal kernel: the algebra of contracts. Its content is a preorder with a monoidal product, meets and a residual, i.e. the same residuated structure as linear and separation logic. It is the smallest thing that yields recursive composition ("a system of systems is a system") with theorems. It is general because it is parametric in the underlying notion of behaviour. Its weakness is that it is only a shell: all expressive power (time, probability, norms) must come from the chosen behaviour model and assertion language.
- Most general kernel: dependent type theory with a small trusted checker, used as a meta-language. Contract algebra, separation logic (Iris), session types, temporal logic, authorization semantics (Cedar) and default-logic semantics (Catala's is formalised) have all been defined inside such a system. This is the only candidate where "specification, construction and proof are one artefact" and where untrusted producers (including AI agents) can be checked by a small kernel.
- A plausible layered kernel, as an aspiration and not an existing system: (1) dependent type theory as the trusted meta-level; (2) a substructural/resource layer (separation-logic resource algebras or quantitative types) for ownership, budgets, capabilities and commitments treated as linear resources; (3) the contract algebra as the composition discipline between components at every scale; (4) modalities added as needed, with "says" as a monad indexed by principal, time as a temporal or guarded modality, knowledge as an indexed S5-like modality; (5) norms as first-class data (conditional, prioritised, with reparation chains) given semantics by monitors, not as a primitive O-modality; (6) for every policy checked at runtime or at population scale, a designated decidable fragment (Datalog-like authorization, safety automata, polyhedral contracts, defeasible rules) with a soundness proof against the kernel. Multimodal type theory, quantitative type theory, Iris and Aura each demonstrate one piece; nobody has demonstrated the whole.
- Why experts expect no single universal object logic: (a) expressiveness trades against decidability and complexity, so one logic adequate for everything is useless for automatic checking; (b) modal combinations with interaction are fragile (collapse under fibring, undecidability of products); (c) some notions have rival, incompatible formalisations with no convergence (obligation above all, also agency and knowledge under bounded rationality); (d) different domains need different structural rules (resources reject contraction and weakening; knowledge accepts them). The consensus response is a framework level (institutions, logical frameworks, type theory as meta-language) rather than a universal logic.
- What logic cannot supply for this programme: validity of the specification relative to human intent (Fonseca study; Catala's caveat that formalisation is interpretation), enforcement of liveness-type obligations (section 6), and whole-population guarantees for heterogeneous actors (section 7). These are the places where the other strands (cybernetics/control, organisation theory, incentives) have to carry the load.

### Gaps
- The SEP "Combining Logics" text on the collapse problem and on universal logic (Béziau) was not read directly; only the entry's framing and bibliography were confirmed.
- No published system was found that integrates dependent types, a separation-style resource layer, and modalities for time, knowledge, obligation and "says" in one implemented, used kernel; the layered kernel above is the researcher's synthesis.
- No expert survey or position paper was retrieved that explicitly evaluates a single logical kernel for organisation- or nation-scale governance; this appears to be open territory rather than settled prior art.
- Self-reference limits (Gödel, Tarski, Löb) bearing on a kernel that reasons about its own governance are covered by another strand and were deliberately not researched here.
