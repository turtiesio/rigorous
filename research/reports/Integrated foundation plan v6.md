# Plan for an integrated foundation

**Status.** This is a proposal, in its sixth version. It follows from the survey in [Compositional foundations for governed systems.md](/root/mic/reports/Compositional foundations for governed systems.md), which found that small rigorous substrates exist and that nobody has composed them into one foundation with a stated composition law for authority, obligation and liability. Five rounds of independent adversarial review shaped it (files beginning "plan_attack_" in /root/mic/reports). Round 1 found that the theorem targets could not fail. Round 2 found that the threat model made the accountability targets unsatisfiable, that rule authority had been wrongly tied to containment, and that several targets were already known. Round 3 found no critical defect and judged the plan ready to start step 0, with two serious problems in the evidence scheme and the conflict rule. Round 4 again judged it ready to start step 0 and found one serious error, in the consistency claim for conflicting norms. Round 5 found that two mechanisms added in response (a challenge period for verdicts, and authorities founded by consent) were unsound. This version removes mechanisms where it can: priorities are a total order, so there are no pending conflicts; verdicts are eventual; authorities founded by consent are not modelled; and one target has been reclassified as a lemma. Citations marked † are from memory and were not fetched; the reviewers checked the others at varying depth.

## What a positive result would and would not establish

A successful run would show that mediated, recorded interactions among units with specified controllers compose with stated guarantees about authority, resources, commitments and rule change, and that the guarantees survive nesting, several rule authorities over one unit, and creation and dissolution of units.

It would not show that a foundation for governing humans, learned agents and institutions exists in the full sense of the original goal. Humans, learned agents and third-party services are environments here. "Self-reflecting" is delivered as rule change checked by the rules in force, with a fixed set of entrenched invariants and a named anchor. "10^10" is delivered as stated growth bounds and a load calculation, not a deployment. Legitimacy, incentives and alignment are outside it. Several narrowings are specific to this design. Rule authority is acyclic, so mutual oversight between units (an auditor that audits the body setting its mandate) is not modelled. Where two authorities conflict, the lower-priority norm is overridden, lawfully and on the record, so the guarantee is conformance to a resolved set of norms and not to every authority. All rule authority descends from the anchor, and priority among authorities is a total order that the anchor can adjust, so the plan models a world with a root of authority; authorities that units found over themselves by consent, and conflicts of laws with no common superior, are not modelled. Witnesses, the anchor, the time source and the identity issuer are components and not units, so the recursion stops at parts the foundation does not govern. Verdicts are eventual: a wrong provisional verdict can stand for as long as evidence is delayed. And the results depend on named anchors: an identity issuer, root rules, a time source, and a bound on faulty witnesses. For each theorem that survives, the plan lists the anchors it depends on and reports any theorem that depends on all of them.

Three targets are open research (R1 to R3) and two are narrower open questions (R4 and R5). One piece of metatheory for the core is new work. Everything else is reuse of published results or application lemmas, and the plan reports reuse and novelty separately. A failed target is evidence against this candidate and its encoding, not an impossibility result about the goal.

## Non-goals

- No complete automatic verifier for arbitrary units.
- No unconditional self-certification. Trust anchors are named.
- No optimal decentralised control and no ideal preference aggregation.
- No guarantee about human or learned behaviour outside a mediated interface or a declared envelope.
- No strict Sybil-proofness without an identity anchor. Identity is an assumption of every result.
- No general liveness. Obligations are deadlines in a timed model.
- No confidentiality or privacy guarantee. An observation declaration bounds what is logged, not what a party can learn.
- No incentive compatibility. Sanctions are recorded and executed; whether they deter is not claimed.
- No accountability for effects that only colluding parties ever see.
- No claim about legitimacy.

## Step 0: definitions, assumptions, benchmark and frozen statements

**Unit.** An open transition system with input, output and internal actions, a declared interface, and a clock.

**Principals and roles.** A principal is an identity plus a set of role instances. Each role instance belongs to exactly one unit and each unit has at most one containing unit, so component containment is a tree. A unit can itself be a principal and hold roles in other units. What an identity carries between its own role instances in different units is not mediated.

**Four separate relations.** Membership (which role instances an identity holds). Component containment (a tree). Resource ownership (each resource has exactly one owning unit; other units may hold recorded funding claims on it). Rule authority (which units' rules constrain a unit). Rule authority is a declared directed acyclic relation that is independent of containment, and a unit may have several authorities. Every unit receives a rank when it is created, and an authority edge may run only from a higher rank to a lower one, so acyclicity is checked locally by each declarer. Authority edges are declared by the anchor or by an authority over the units concerned, and a constrained unit cannot alter them. Priority among authorities is a total order derived from rank and then from identifier, which only the anchor can override; two norms of one authority are ordered by time of issue, later first.

**Effective norms.** A conflict is a minimal set of norms that cannot be jointly satisfied from a given state; a conflicting pair is the special case, and three obligations of which any two can be met and all three cannot is a conflict of three. A unit's effective norm set at a state is the result of a prioritised selection over the norms of all its authority ancestors. Each norm carries its origin and is counted once, at its origin's priority, however many authority paths it arrives by. The selection is one published procedure chosen in step 0, and because the priority order on norms is total it leaves no conflict undecided. Each norm left out is recorded. An obligation left out is not owed, and an obligation that selection keeps and the unit misses is a violation under R2 like any other. An authority that issues a norm its own charter forbids has deviated. Invariants on a resource that are kept by rights allocated in advance, such as a spending cap, are not subject to selection: a commitment that would breach one is refused when it is made.

**Controllers.** Every unit has an atomic controller that owns the effect of every protected action: other components send requests, and only the controller emits the effect. Where load requires it, a unit has a set of atomic controllers (shards). Each shard owns a disjoint set of resources and rules, and a static function assigns every protected action to exactly one shard. An invariant that spans shards is kept only through rights allocated in advance. The assignment is a function of the resource name. One shard owns the charter and the assignment function, and every amendment is a coordinated action installed by lease. An action that needs two shards is a coordinated action and is counted as such in step 5.

**Receipts and witnesses.** Each unit, and each shard, has a witness set of 2f+1 members of which at most f are faulty. Witnesses are appointed by a rule authority or the anchor, never by the unit's own charter. A receipt is accepted only with f+1 witness signatures, so at least one signer is honest and f faulty witnesses cannot block the unit. A recipient has a duty to forward every receipt it relies on to the whole witness set. Witnesses hold the log contents, not only commitments to them, and replay the controller against a reference implementation at a stated period; the time to complete a replay is bounded only under a stated load. Controllers are therefore deterministic given their recorded inputs: requests from humans and learned agents, event order and clock readings are recorded as inputs, and their reasoning is not replayed. A witness is an atomic component and not a unit; its appointment is a receipted action, and its honesty up to the bound is an assumption that is not itself witnessed. Per-unit summaries carry f+1 signatures. A signature is bound to the controller's identity, the witness epoch, the policy version and the controller-signed log prefix it covers, and a summary says what held in that prefix and not what held in an interval. With f+1 signatures out of 2f+1, a faulty controller can show two honest witnesses different prefixes of a forked log, and two valid summaries can then exist; forwarded receipts reveal the fork eventually, which is the guarantee known as fork consistency†. Verdicts are therefore eventual. A verdict is provisional while evidence may still be in transit and is revised on the record if contradicted; it is final only when every link on the forwarding paths has met the bound of the performance envelope. Evidence needed for a provisional verdict is retained until the verdict is final. Each accountability result is stated per unit: when a unit's witness bound fails its own guarantees are void, and the plan must show that no compliant neighbour is named as a result. A compliant neighbour may still bear an allocated loss in that case, and that is recorded as outside the guarantee. That an appointing authority is independent of the disputes its appointees witness is an assumption. An external party that cannot check receipts, such as the vendor, is reached through a gateway that is a unit with its own controller and witnesses; what the vendor does behind the gateway is environment.

**Fractal.** Closure, substitutivity under stated hypotheses, and a uniform governance interface: every unit exposes authority, resources, commitments and norms, a log, and a rule-change procedure.

**Controlled.** A list of named properties, each with a formal statement and an assurance record with six fields: mechanisms, assumptions, what is observable, detection time, intervention available, and what is left unprotected. The mechanism is recorded on two separate axes: prevented, certified or detected; and enforced or assumed. A composite's record is derived by rules that are candidates to be checked, not an algebra already known to be sound. An interface assumption is removed only by instantiating a sound assume-guarantee rule with all its premises, which for the published rule include that the discharged assumptions are safety properties ([Abadi & Lamport](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/abadi-conjoining.pdf)). The rule used is entered in the reuse ledger. Two promises that are each conditional on the other, even with deadlines, do not discharge each other. Remaining assumptions and unprotected remainders are united. The composite's observables are what it exports at its boundary after hiding. Detection time is a local bound plus a named delivery term for every link on the path, and that term may be unbounded. Intervention is recorded per observer. Where the observable evidence cannot support a conclusion, or no rule applies, the record is "unestablished". Step 1 exercises the rules on serial paths, alternative paths, a circular pair of deadline promises and an unbounded reporting link, and checks that the derivation gives the same record however the units are grouped. If it does not, the claim that assurance records are fractal is withdrawn.

**Assumptions about faults and the network.**

- Any non-controller component may deviate arbitrarily, and any set may collude.
- Prevention results assume correct controllers.
- Accountability results allow a controller and up to f of its witnesses to deviate, and cover only effects for which an honest party holds a receipt, which it then forwards. An effect seen only by colluders is outside every guarantee.
- Links between a controller and its own witnesses deliver within a stated bound. Vendor links and all links between units, including links to an adjudicator in another unit, have no delivery bound, but every message is eventually delivered.
- Clocks are synchronised to within a stated skew by an external time source. That source is a trust anchor, since no protocol can synchronise clocks across links with no delivery bound.
- The identity anchor and the root rules are trusted.

**Obligations and verdicts.** An obligation is discharged by a receipt logged at the obligee's controller before the deadline, so a missed deadline is observable locally. There are three kinds of verdict, kept distinct: proved deviation, suspicion (evidence withheld or a party unresponsive, declared by f+1 witnesses), and allocated loss where nobody deviated. Only a proved deviation names a party for the purposes of R2; a suspicion never counts as blame. Lateness caused by an unbounded link is allocated loss borne by the obligor. Because the obligee's controller issues the discharging receipt, an obligor may submit its performance through the obligee's witnesses; receipts from f+1 of them discharge the obligation, timed by their receipt, and a suspicion verdict against the obligee moves the allocated loss to the obligee. Witnesses reject a log entry whose recorded time differs from their own receipt time by more than the link bound plus the clock skew.

**Useful behaviour.** Each benchmark variant lists outcomes that must occur whenever the delivery bounds of a stated performance envelope hold. Refusals outside the envelope are counted as unavailability. A controller that rejects everything inside the envelope fails.

**Smaller.** In steps 1 and 2: definitions and hypotheses counted on paper in a common notation. In step 3: lines of definitions plus axioms and trust anchors in the proof assistant, for the chosen core and the runner-up. Domain definitions, trusted assumptions, reused libraries and proof effort are counted separately. The runtime trust base is inventoried separately. No global minimality is claimed.

**Benchmark family.** A public procurement chain. Containment: a state contains a ministry; a parent group (enterprise) contains a subsidiary (organisation), which contains a team, which contains an approving officer's role (human) and a purchasing agent (learned agent) with its purchasing tool (program). Resources are a budget and purchased compute. A compute vendor is an external service. Rule authority: a supranational body over the state; the state over the ministry; the ministry over the group and the subsidiary through procurement law; the group over the subsidiary through group policy; a regulator in the group's home jurisdiction over the subsidiary, with no edge to or from the ministry; the subsidiary over the team, and through it the officer's team role, the agent and the tool; the state over an external audit body; and the audit body over the officer's audit role. The anchor sets the priority among the ministry, the group and the regulator. Procurement law and the state's audit law oblige the team and the audit body to report summaries of ministry-funded purchases to the ministry. The vendor is reached through a gateway unit. The ministry funds the group by a commitment. The variants are:

1. The compliant path.
2. A diamond: the officer also holds a role in the audit body, and the budget carries funding claims from two units.
3. Formation: a charter creates the subsidiary under a power granted by the group, and there is one declared amplification (officer approval combined with the agent's budget right). The subsidiary then creates project units, each of which may create further units, in a number that depends on an input from the environment.
4. Revocation and claw-back of a partly spent grant, concurrent with a purchase.
5. Creation, a deadline, then dissolution of the subsidiary with a commitment outstanding.
6. Vendor failure with every chain principal compliant, and a disputed delivery decided by a named adjudicating unit.
7. Purchases split to stay under the approval threshold, with the team and the audit body naming the purchase differently.
8. A controller omits a log entry, in these cases: an honest recipient holds a receipt; only a colluding recipient saw the effect, which must come out as outside coverage; a colluding witness countersigns while an honest one audits; a witness lies in a summary; the controller shows two honest witnesses different prefixes of a forked log, and the receipt that exposes the fork is delivered late; and an obligee's controller drops the obligor's performance or records a late time for it.
9. The ministry changes the spending cap while purchases are in flight and one shard hears late, in three cases: the new cap is below what has already been spent; the cap goes from 100 to 95 with 10 spent and 90 of old rights still held by the late shard; and the cap goes from 100 to 120 with 100 of old rights still held by the late shard.
10. Conflicting authorities over the subsidiary: the regulator obliges what procurement law forbids; three obligations of which any two can be met and all three cannot; two conflicting norms from one authority; a ministry norm that reaches the subsidiary directly and through the group with different translations; a second commitment that would exceed the cap, which must be refused when made; an authority that issues a norm its charter forbids; an amendment that satisfies the group and violates procurement law; and an authority edge from a lower rank to a higher one, which must be rejected.
11. The subsidiary validly amends its own amendment rule while a commitment is outstanding. It then attempts three amendments: one that removes the logging duty, one that keeps the duty and narrows the definition of "protected action", and one that keeps the witness duty and changes the replay period or the reference implementation the witnesses replay against, which must be rejected because those change only with the consent of the authority that appoints the witnesses.
12. An early breach that is irrelevant: one unit breaks a guarantee the chain property does not depend on, and a later unit causes the violation.

After the core is chosen, the external reviewers write one further variant that the authors have not seen.

**Frozen before any proof attempt, and stated over traces so that they do not presuppose one core.** The grammar of the policy fragment, its restriction rule, whether quantities are bounded, whether principals can be created during the analysis, the semantics of revocation, how quantities and rule syntax are represented and sized, and the complexity class that counts as success for each of the two questions separately (R1). The causal definition of deviation, the verdict type, the content of a receipt and of a per-unit summary, a bound on summary growth in terms of activity at the unit's boundary, and the performance envelope under which a verdict is final (R2). The class of mappings between vocabularies, the selection procedure, the bound for conflict detection, and the local compliance predicate for one authority edge together with the size of the description of the selection it may use (R3). The rule for which commitments survive a rule change, a class of useful changes that it accepts, and a bound on the grandfathered set as a function of the lease period and of the commitments and rights outstanding at the change (R4). The entrenched invariants, the semantic meaning of "preserves", and a class of useful self-amendments that must be accepted (R5). A ledger giving, for each reused result, its source, the theorem, and how it is instantiated. Thresholds: the ceiling on "unknown" classifications, the ceiling on coordinated decisions, the number of runs per variant and the pass rate required with language-model agents.

**Exit test.** Two reviewers independently judge ten named systems against each definition. The ten include a law-governed interaction deployment, a Cedar policy store, a capability-token deployment, a decentralised autonomous organisation, a national procurement regime and an open message bus with no governance. Two small constructed examples that are meant to satisfy the definitions are added to the ten, because the survey found no existing system that composes all the structures. Judgements are made clause by clause (each element of the governance interface, each field of the record) for each system. The step passes if chance-corrected agreement over those judgements reaches a level fixed in advance, each clause is judged satisfied by at least one of the ten existing systems and unsatisfied by at least two, and the message bus is rejected.

**Sources to read in full first.** The multi-level compliance paper, the timed I/O automata monograph, the dynamic I/O automata creation theorem, the accountable-log protocol, the compositional-blame and component-causality papers, the composition theorem for assume-guarantee specifications, prioritised norm selection ([Hansen](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10458-007-9016-7); [Liao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.08034)), conflict detection between interacting legal systems ([Li et al.](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/legal-conflict-detection-in-interacting-legal-systems/)), which may already contain part of R3, and the paper cited for conformance hierarchies, whose abstract does not mention them. Several were checked only at abstract level.

## Step 1: paper stage

This stage is done by hand in the default core of step 2. It comes first because it is cheap and decides whether the rest is worth doing.

**Immediate checks.** A set of small cases is run against the definitions before anything else: the cases of variant 8, including the forked log; the late update and the cap below past spending in variant 9; the three-norm conflict, the two-path norm and the refused commitment of variant 10; the early irrelevant breach of variant 12; and creation, a deadline and dissolution in the default core (variant 5), with a time limit. Dissolution by an outside decision has to be encoded as a request, because the published creation theorem allows a unit to end only by its own action. If the definitions do not handle a case, step 0 is revised.

| R4 | Commitments across a rule change. The frozen rule says which commitments made under version n must be honoured under version n+1. The target is that every change is either rejected or installed with an explicit set of grandfathered commitments no larger than the frozen bound. For an aggregate invariant such as a cap, the owning shard keeps one ledger of completed spending, outstanding commitments and every right that can still be exercised under any version. For the frozen grammar, the check that an amendment preserves the entrenched invariants (single ownership of resources, log continuity, the witness duty with witnesses appointed from outside, the obligation to run this check, and conformance to the effective norm set) terminates, is sound (it accepts no amendment that breaks an invariant, including one that does so by changing the meaning of a term), and is complete for the frozen class of useful self-amendments (it accepts every member that preserves the invariants). **New metatheory for the core.** Closure and substitutivity under trace inclusion for input/output automata with both time and creation is not a published result as far as the plan knows. Timed process calculi with creation and congruence results exist ([Berger](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-540-28644-8_8)), as do timed transition systems with dynamic creation and termination ([David et al. 2013](https://homes.cs.aau.dk/~kgl/SSFT2015/DYN.pdf)); both are screened first. A cheaper route is checked before any of this work. Every property the plan claims is decided on finite prefixes of a run, and a finite prefix contains finitely many creations. If closure and substitutivity can be proved for plain timed input/output automata with a pool of dormant units, for every pool size and with hypotheses that do not depend on the size, the plan takes that as its core, the published composition theorems apply, and the combined model is listed as not tested. Only if that argument fails is the combined metatheory attempted, first on variant 5.

**Results to reproduce and application lemmas, counted as reuse.**

- Complete mediation: with correct controllers, no protected effect occurs without a recorded derivation.
- A constraint across one identity's roles. Separation of duty for the officer (not approving and auditing the same purchase) is detected by the ministry from the reported summaries, given a declared mapping between the two units' purchase vocabularies and a stated rule for aggregating split purchases (variant 7). Whether this works is settled by what a summary contains, so it is an application lemma and not a research target.
- Conservation of a resource with a single owner, with funding claims retained in both the nested and the flattened reading.
- The lease argument: after a rule change, a controller that has not heard stops authorising within a time fixed by the lease period and clock skew. Online schema change with leases is the precedent ([Rae et al.](https://research.google/pubs/online-asynchronous-schema-change-in-f1/)).
- Coordination classification with existing tools: invariant-confluence checking and its segmented form ([Whittaker & Hellerstein](https://mwhittaker.github.io/publications/iconfluence_vldbj2020.pdf)), pre-allocated rights ([Balegas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.09052)), and conflict analysis with synthesised coordination ([Houshmand & Lesani](https://mohsenlesani.github.io/companion/popl19/POPL19.pdf)). The share of benchmark invariants in each class is reported, with a time limit per invariant and timeouts counted as unknown.
- An assume-guarantee contract for the vendor. Inside the declared envelope the purchase invariants hold; outside it the departure is detected at a named observation point within a stated local bound, and the dispute goes to the adjudicating unit, since exchange between two parties cannot be made fair without a third.

**Other work in this stage.** Derive the assurance records for the three-unit chain. Write the scale argument of step 5 on paper. Send everything to external reviewers.

**Stop rules.**

- If the frozen statement of R2 is false on a three-unit chain and the weakest repair needs inner logs, publish that as a negative result for this candidate and stop.
- The work continues only if R2 survives and at least one of R1 and R3 improves on its stated baseline, as judged by the external reviewers. Otherwise report which criterion failed, keep any result that is new on its own, and stop.
- A statement repaired after a counterexample is reported as a new conjecture, not as the surviving target.
- If R5 is unsound, amendments are limited to a syntactic class that cannot touch the entrenched terms and the claim about self-reflection is withdrawn. If R5 is sound and incomplete, the claim is narrowed to the amendments the check accepts. Only the conformance part of R5 depends on R3 and falls with it.
- If R4 fails, the bound on cap changes in step 5 is withdrawn.
- The external reviewers judge whether a surviving statement is definitional. A statement counts as a result only if it is refuted by some benchmark variant when one named hypothesis is dropped, which is a necessary check and not a sufficient one, and it is not an instance of a step-0 definition or of a result in the reuse ledger. A reviewer who says it is must exhibit the instance.

## Step 2: choose the core

**Default hypothesis.** Timed input/output automata with dynamic creation. The untimed model ([Lynch & Tuttle](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/tds/papers/Lynch/CWI89.pdf)) has no deadlines and no creation. Dynamic I/O automata add creation and destruction; their substitutivity theorem for creation has six premises ([Attie & Lynch](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06030)). Timed I/O automata add time; the conditions that stop a component blocking time matter for whether a deadline can be met vacuously†. The plan knows of no published theorem for the two together. The combined model therefore carries two groups of proof obligations, and failing to discharge them for variant 5 (creation, deadline, dissolution, with hiding) is a reason to drop this core. Creation by a constitutional step is made an observable action.

**Candidates.** Three concrete encodings compete: the default; plain timed input/output automata with a pool of dormant units, whose composition theorems are published; and a timed specification theory with refinement, conjunction, composition and quotient ([David et al.](https://homes.cs.aau.dk/~adavid/publications/41-hscc10.pdf)), which also has no creation and needs the same pool. A pool counts as expressing creation only if its theorems hold for every pool size with hypotheses that do not depend on the size. If the pool argument of step 1 succeeds, the two pool candidates are compared and the default is not needed; if it fails, the bake-off reduces to whether the default's new metatheory can be established. Law-governed interaction with an institutional monitor is kept as a baseline for expressiveness only, because it assumes trusted controllers and so cannot state R2. One further model, dynamic networks of timed machines†, is screened in the survey of existing work. Polynomial functors, bigraphs and reflective rewriting logic are left out of the bake-off to keep its cost bounded, which means the plan does not test the categorical candidates the survey describes.

**Decision rule, applied in this order.** Can the candidate express variants 2, 3, 5 and 9 under the same observable requirements and environment assumptions? Does it support the frozen statement of R2? Which semantic obligations and trust assumptions does it need, taken from one common inventory so that the count does not depend on how conditions are packaged, with incomparable conditions reported as such? How large is it on paper? Each encoding gets the same time budget, fixed in advance, and an advocate of each candidate reviews its encoding. "Not established within the budget" is an allowed outcome and is not evidence that a candidate is inferior. If no candidate is eligible the plan stops and reports it; if one is, it is chosen without a runner-up and its size is reported alone; a tie goes to the candidate with existing mechanisation. Existing mechanisations are surveyed first.

**If the core changes,** the module contracts are reassessed.

## Step 3: mechanise

Mechanise the core, the five structures (authority, resources, commitments and norms, log and time, rule change) and the targets that survived step 1.

**Rule change.** A unit's rules are controller state. An amendment is valid when it passes the unit's own charter procedure, conforms to the unit's effective norm set and passes the entrenchment check of R5. A charter may contain an amendment rule that applies to itself; version n+1 is checked under version n. This gives a history of authorised change and nothing more; what persists is the entrenched set of R5.

**Separability.** Each structure has a local lemma from the reuse list. The hypothesis fails if the proof of a local lemma imports definitions from more than one other structure. R1 to R5 are integration targets, and for them the dependency graph is reported without a pass or fail.

## Step 4: reference controller and benchmark run

- Write an executable controller, witness and policy evaluator for the decidable fragment.
- Test them against the mechanised model on sequences of transitions, concurrent revocation, crash recovery and version change. The model's theorems are proved. The executable's conformance is tested, not proved.
- Run every variant with language-model agents, scripted humans and an unmodified vendor service, for the fixed number of runs, and check the required outcomes against the fixed pass rate. Controller-side outcomes (wrongly blocked, wrongly allowed) are reported separately from whether the agents completed the task.

## Step 5: scale

Containment depth is fixed by the benchmark and fan-out is not bounded, so the load bounds carry the content. Rules flow along the authority relation and not the containment tree, so the bounds are stated in terms of containment depth, the longest authority path, the number of authorities over a unit, policy size, and the vertices and edges of the affected dependency closure. Each bound is first argued on paper in step 1, on cases that include a long authority chain and a wide join of authorities over shallow containment.

- **Per-decision cost.** Stated in policy size, the number of authority ancestors, the cost of conflict detection and selection, and the round trip for f+1 witness signatures.
- **Availability.** A unit can authorise only while every authority among its ancestors has renewed its lease in time. In the worst case allowed by eventual delivery, availability is zero, and that is reported. Inside the performance envelope, expected availability is stated as a function of the ancestor graph (the number of ancestor authorities and the dependencies they share), the lease period and its margin, and a per-link probability of meeting the envelope bound, which is a stated workload assumption. A floor is fixed in advance and measured on the wide-join case as well as the long chain.
- **Load per shard per unit time,** under a stated rate of active principals. It includes authorisation, lease renewal, receipt forwarding, and witness replay and its backlog. A unit whose load grows with population must be shown to shard under the definition in step 0.
- **Summary and log growth.** The size of the per-unit summaries used by R2, and of logs, as functions of activity.
- **Rule change,** as four separate quantities. The time after which no controller authorises under the old rule is finite and is proved from the lease period and clock skew along the longest authority path, with no assumption on delivery; a lease that depends on an authority cannot be renewed once that authority's own lease has expired. Installation time and recovery time are finite only under a stated bounded-delay workload assumption; otherwise they are unbounded and appear as unavailability. Message cost is proportional to the affected dependency closure, under a stated dissemination algorithm. When a cap is lowered, expiry of the rights already allocated stops further spending under them and does not undo spending already done. New rights are issued from the single ledger that R4 describes, so that total spending at every intermediate state stays within the larger of the two caps if R4 holds, and the new cap itself holds only if completed and grandfathered spending fit under it. Rate limits that renew are treated separately from cumulative caps.

The workload mix, taken from a real procurement workload if one can be obtained, is fixed before measuring. Simulation measures the constants at increasing sizes, including shared-root and several-authority workloads.

**The scale claim fails if** any unit cannot be sharded at the target workload, any bound exceeds its declared growth, availability falls below its floor, or the coordinated share exceeds its ceiling. Correlated faults, anchor failure and adversarial load are not covered.

## How the plan could fail as a whole

- **The formal model does not match the world.** The defence is the benchmark and review by people who run real procurement, and it is weak.
- **Most consequential actions are unmediated,** or are seen only by parties who collude. Then the foundation governs little.
- **The open targets collapse.** The stop rules fire and the result is negative for this candidate.
- **Trust anchors dominate.** If every guarantee reduces to the honesty of the identity anchor, the root rules and one witness per unit, the foundation is an accounting system for trust. The plan would say which it is.
