# Adversarial review of the integrated foundation plan, version 7

Reviewed 17 November 2026. I read the current plan and formed an independent assessment before consulting the earlier attacks. I then checked the review history, especially the round-6 findings and the passages removed from version 6, and fetched primary sources. I worked alone and modified only this report.

**The plan is sound enough to start step 0.** I found no CRITICAL or HIGH defect. Three MEDIUM findings below concern a lost non-vacuity check and two overgeneralised statements in the decisions table. None demonstrates that all permitted combinations of decisions fail. The restructuring substantially improves the plan: a listed research choice is no longer being presented as an established mechanism.

“Verified wrong” below means a demonstrably invalid assertion or inference, with the counterexample distinguished from the cited literature. “My judgement” covers experimental adequacy, severity and proposed repairs. “Could not verify” identifies limits of the source checks.

## 1. MEDIUM — The rewrite drops the explicit time-progress obligation, allowing an empty execution class to masquerade as deadline success

**Text attacked:** “Unit. An open transition system with input, output and internal actions, a declared interface, and a clock”; “Each benchmark variant lists outcomes that must occur whenever the delivery bounds of a stated performance envelope hold”; D12's cases are “Creation in a number set by an environment input; creation continuing while an earlier event awaits its verdict”.

**My judgement:** D12 and the trace freeze need an explicit admissibility/non-vacuity requirement. Having a clock does not ensure that a composed system can let time advance. Version 6 explicitly called out the conditions preventing a component from blocking time and required a creation/deadline/dissolution check with hiding. Version 7 retains the benchmark scenario but drops that metatheoretic requirement. It does not say whether admissible complete executions must exist for the tested environments, or what prevents a newly created, dormant or dissolving component from blocking time.

**Concrete failure mode, my construction:** Encode a pending obligation using a component whose clock invariant forbids time beyond its deadline, with no discharge transition. If deadlocked prefixes count, the model never reaches the state in which lateness is detected. If only time-divergent complete executions count, this implementation can instead have no admissible complete executions. Universal claims about all such executions, including required outcomes conditional on an envelope, then hold over an empty set. An urgent internal self-loop provides a related Zeno case; hiding the loop does not make time progress. The existing ban on rejecting every request does not explicitly test these mechanisms.

This is not a claim that published timed I/O models necessarily permit the bad implementation. Their implementation/admissibility conditions are precisely what the encoding must instantiate. Nor is it a demand to solve general liveness.

**Evidence:** [Goorden et al., *Timed I/O Automata: It Is Never Too Late to Complete Your Timed Specification Theory*, §3, especially the discussion following Definition 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04529) separates independent progress from Zeno behaviour and warns that an output self-loop can stop time. Its consistency machinery distinguishes specifications with implementations from unimplementable ones. These are primary-source reasons not to treat the mere presence of a clock as sufficient.

**Exact change:** Add to D12/the trace freeze: “Specify admissible time-divergent executions and the input/receptiveness or strategy conditions that prevent controller-induced time blocking. Show that the required environment class is nonempty and that the encoding admits the required progress against that class, before evaluating universal deadline and useful-behaviour claims.” Add cases for a dormant component, an urgent hidden loop, and creation followed by deadline and dissolution, checking preservation of the applicable progress conditions under composition and hiding. This is a MEDIUM addition to the already scheduled core/trace freeze, not a reason to reorder the whole project.

## 2. MEDIUM — D11's proposed bound forgets completed grandfathered spending when the last old allocation settles

**Text attacked:** “With two changes inside one lease period the bound is the largest cap among versions that still have unexpired or unsettled rights.” R4 requires that “an aggregate invariant holds at every intermediate state in the form D11 fixes”.

**Verified wrong as an unconditional bound; counterexample is mine:** Interpret the statement charitably as also including the current cap. Start with cap 100 and an allocation of 100. Lower the cap to 95, allow the old allocation to be used under its still-valid lease, and issue no new rights. The holder spends 100, its lease expires, and its authenticated final balance is received. There are now no unexpired or unsettled rights under cap 100. The proposed bound falls to 95, but completed spending remains 100. Settlement removes uncertainty; it does not reverse expenditure. An additional change to 90 before settlement gives the same counterexample with two changes, exactly the case D11 describes.

This is distinct from round 6's expired-but-unsettled defect, which version 7 correctly fixes. It occurs **after** settlement. The same issue arises immediately when a proposed cap is below spending already completed. Version 6 expressly required rejection or grandfathering for that case; version 7's table no longer gives it a case or states the exception.

**Evidence:** [Balegas et al., *Extending Eventually Consistent Cloud Databases for Enforcing Numeric Invariants*, §III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.09052) accounts for consumed and transferred rights explicitly. It provides no operation by which lease expiry or settlement erases prior consumption. The numerical contradiction above is my deduction, not a result claimed by that paper. The earlier version is available at [Integrated foundation plan v6.md](/root/mic/reports/Integrated%20foundation%20plan%20v6.md).

**Exact change:** Qualify the maximum-cap sentence by the treatment of irreversible spending. Restore “cap below completed spending” and add “all old allocations settled after spending above the new cap” to D11's cases. Freeze either rejection of such changes, or an explicit historical grandfathered-spending term with a precise accounting horizon; do not retire that term merely because its allocation settled. State separately the bound on cumulative expenditure and the bound on new issuable rights. Do not prescribe a new formula until the disjoint ledger categories have been checked.

**My judgement on severity:** MEDIUM. R4 deliberately leaves this ledger and the accepted class of changes to D11. Rejection is a viable option, and a properly defined grandfathered-spending bound is another. The faulty “known problems” sentence should not become the next unreviewed invariant.

## 3. MEDIUM — D10's “only the latest” rule silently assumes the old f+1 receipt threshold

**Text attacked:** “If the earliest or median time counts, faulty witnesses can backdate a late performance; only the latest presented time is safe against that, and it makes timeliness depend on the slowest link.” D8 also offers “3f+1 witnesses with 2f+1 signatures”.

**Verified wrong without a receipt-count qualification; proof is mine:** Suppose a certificate contains q distinct signed receipt times, at most f of which are faulty. Sort them increasingly. The (f+1)-st time cannot precede every honest receipt time: that would require f+1 faulty signers. Thus this order statistic prevents arbitrary Byzantine backdating below the earliest honest receipt, just as the latest timestamp does. With q=f+1 it is the latest time. With q=2f+1 it is the median. For f=1 and three times [0, 11, 12], where 0 is a fabricated backdate and the deadline is 10, the median correctly fails to certify timely receipt; using the slowest timestamp is unnecessary for this particular safety property.

This is not a full discharge protocol: authenticated event identity, distinct signers, the selected certificate, clock-skew margins and the distinction between performance time and receipt time still matter. In particular, a conservative deadline test must account for honest-clock error. The point is that D10's universal exclusion of the median is false under a threshold explicitly available elsewhere in the table.

**Fidelity:** [Fable r6, M8](/root/mic/reports/plan_attack_fable_r6.md) made its claim specifically about f+1 presented receipts, potentially containing f Byzantine times. Version 7 copied the conclusion but removed that premise while reopening quorum size in D8. The earlier attack does not establish the new unrestricted claim.

**Evidence:** [Constantinescu et al., *A Fair and Resilient Decentralized Clock Network for Transaction Ordering*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol286-opodis2023/LIPIcs.OPODIS.2023.8/LIPIcs.OPODIS.2023.8.pdf) supplies primary prior art on Byzantine receipt timestamps and median validity. It is not a turnkey discharge theorem for this plan; the elementary order-statistic proof above is the evidence for this correction.

**Exact change:** Replace the known-problems cell with: “For certificates of exactly f+1 receipt times with up to f faulty signers, the latest is the conservative order statistic against backdating. Other receipt thresholds require their own analysis; the (f+1)-st smallest time is another candidate when more receipts are required.” Freeze the receipt threshold jointly with D8, rather than assuming the log-summary threshold and discharge threshold are identical. Test backdating, withholding, postdating, and a slow honest signer for each admitted certificate size, with the chosen skew convention.

**My judgement on severity:** MEDIUM. A sound listed choice exists. This is an incorrectly narrowed decision and availability analysis, not an impossible architecture.

## Audit of the remaining choices and targets

**Fixed architecture — my judgement.** I did not find a demonstrated contradiction among single resource ownership, sharded effect ownership, separate containment and authority relations, and the explicit split between correct-controller prevention and faulty-controller accountability. The statement that this architecture “survived review” must not be read as a proof, but its core is substantially what the later reviews retained. Consent-founded authorities have genuinely been removed, rather than silently preserved through the new table. Outside witness appointment is not itself an independence theorem; the actual fault bound is now plainly an assumption and the ministry-as-counterparty problem appears in D8.

**D1–D7 — my judgement.** The self-induced conflict, conflict of one, missing ancestor check, contradictory translations, cap/priority collision and uncontrolled approval are now explicit tests. Each relevant row admits an approach not shown unsound. In particular, evaluating conflicts at activation can be combined with retaining later missed obligations for classification, rather than automatically deleting them. A direct-authority interpretation narrows the guarantee, but that is an express choice. D5's warning should be read as referring to the former low-priority/deletion design; admitting commitments to selection does not logically force their erasure without loss. The joint freeze should say so. I do not count this contextual shorthand as a separate finding.

**Verified source check:** D2 correctly reports the limitation of the cited selection work. [Liao et al., Definition 1 and Example 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.08034) uses literal-based norms and exhibits multiple Reduction extensions despite a total order. Version 7 no longer imports its consistency theorem for timed feasibility.

**D8–D9 — my judgement.** Quorum intersection, evidence completeness and verdict finality are different questions, but the row now explicitly recognizes the distinction and the late-evidence cases. Selecting a time bar must also narrow the event/evidence coverage in R2; it cannot coexist with unconditional completeness for evidence first presented after the bar. Selecting eventual accountability requires eventual forwarding and processing assumptions; “no delivery bound” alone supplies neither. These are compatibility obligations for the expressly joint step-0 review, not additional HIGH findings. [PeerReview, §§3–4](https://haeberlen.cis.upenn.edu/papers/peerreview-tr.pdf) separates evidence-based exposure, suspicion and eventual completeness; it does not make signatures alone a complete causal verdict.

**D12–D13 — my judgement.** Reporting pool results as bounded-population results repairs the round-6 inference from finite prefixes to unbounded eventuality. A joint renewal model or empirical availability on a fixed graph repairs the marginal-probability error. Neither establishes unrestricted scale, and neither now claims to.

**R1–R5 — my judgement.** R1 can fail its frozen complexity requirement. R2 can fail the same-summary/different-verdict test or falsely name a compliant party. R3 can fail either its bounded local-information implication or conflict-detection requirement. R4 can fail a useful-change acceptance requirement, its ledger bound or its grandfathering bound. R5 can fail soundness or completeness on the frozen class. Those are genuine tests, not proofs that the targets are feasible. R4's table should be read together with the frozen required class of useful changes; otherwise rejecting every change would satisfy its first sentence. The plan already provides that class, so I do not rate this as another defect.

**Could not verify:** I did not establish either novelty or impossibility of the full R1–R3 combination. [Caltais et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07880) explicitly restrict their compositional causality result to non-communicating systems; it does not settle R2. [Attie and Lynch](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06030) establishes conditional dynamic creation substitutivity, not by itself the proposed combined timed result. I found no primary source establishing exactly that combined theorem in the search performed. This is not evidence that no such theorem exists.

## Prior art, order and verdict

Add [Goorden et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04529) to the timed-core reading, alongside the 2010 paper. Its revised treatment of error states, pruning and composition matters when instantiating the candidate; it is more useful than treating the old citation as a sufficient theorem inventory. Add the decentralized-clock paper from finding 3 to the D8/D10 comparison. Neither source establishes the integrated foundation or requires abandoning the planned experiment.

**My judgement:** The narrowing is honest and still worth testing. A common-anchor controller/evidence calculus is much less than a self-governing foundation for human institutions, and the plan says so. Deferring choices has not eliminated the interesting R2 compression question or the R1/R3 analysis questions. The positive claim must continue to be reported with every failed target and fallback attached.

Start step 0. Read the primary sources before selecting mechanisms and freezing statements, notwithstanding the final order list's placement of source reading after the freeze. Review the decisions in interacting groups: D1–D7 with R4/R5, D8–D10 with R2, and D11–D13 with the core and scale argument. Then apply the three repairs above, freeze required outcomes and thresholds, and retain the paper-stage stop before mechanisation. I see no basis for a CRITICAL or HIGH rating merely because that joint design exercise may fail.
