# Adversarial review of version 3

Reviewed 17 November 2026. I formed the initial objections from the current report, consulted the seven notes selectively (including every strand's takeaways and relevant supporting passages), checked primary sources, and only then consulted the earlier attacks. I did not edit the report or notes. This is a targeted audit, not independent verification of every citation. The six numbered findings below are the counted findings; qualifications and surviving claims are not additional findings.

## 1. HIGH — The new human-level boundary mistakes incomplete prediction for inability to enforce guarantees

**Claim attacked:** “At the human level the unit stops being something whose behaviour can be specified in advance, so prevention gives way to incentives and accountability after the fact.” The human row adds “No behavioural model that carries guarantees; conformance is established after the fact.” The conclusion recommends “accountability after the fact for everything else, which includes most of what humans do.”

**Verified wrong:** The universal claim about human models has counterexamples. Pereira and Althoff's *Online Formal Verification of Robot Trajectories for Guaranteed Safety of Humans* constructs conservative human-motion models and uses online reachability checks to prevent unsafe robot motion. Its indexed primary-paper abstract reports both the guarantee and an important model-validation limitation: the ISO-based model sometimes misses fast movements. The exact human trajectory need not be predicted; a sound envelope is enough for a conditional safety guarantee. [Primary paper](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1379672/981669.pdf). Verification depth: primary-paper abstract and introduction indexed by search; direct PDF retrieval returned 429, so I am not asserting an audit of its proofs.

The report's own strongest scale example also defeats the categorical prevention claim. AWS evaluates requests against policies before granting access, including requests associated with users. Whether the principal is human does not disable preventive authorization. This enforces mediated actions, not the human's entire behaviour. [Chakarov et al., §§I–II](https://mhicks.me/papers/verified-cloud-authz.pdf).

**My judgement:** The relevant boundary is observability, model adequacy and enforceability, not “human upward.” Untrusted software also need not have a known behavioural implementation. Conversely, humans can be constrained at digital interfaces and by physical interlocks. The inference to “most” human activity has no denominator or evidence. It substantially distorts the answer to whether one compositional formalism can govern heterogeneous principals: nondeterministic environment models already accommodate unknown choices without pretending to model every motive.

**Exact fix:** Replace the paragraph's human claim with: “Human behaviour generally lacks a complete predictive model. Conditional guarantees remain possible for mediated actions and for interactions modelled by conservative behavioural bounds. Activities outside those models and enforcement boundaries require additional incentives, monitoring and accountability. This distinction also applies to untrusted software and learned agents.” Replace the human-row gap with: “Model validity and enforcement coverage; no comprehensive behavioural guarantee.” Delete “which includes most of what humans do,” unless independently substantiated.

## 2. HIGH — “No composition result” for learned-component safety is directly false

**Claim attacked:** AI-agent row, main gap: “No composition result for safety of learned components; Sybil-resistant identity.”

**Verified wrong:** Ivanov, Jothimurugan, Hsu, Vaidya, Alur and Bastani, *Compositional Learning and Verification of Neural Network Controllers* (EMSOFT/TECS 2021), explicitly verifies compositions of learned controllers. It checks safety and progress within each mode and compatibility of postconditions with the next mode's preconditions; these conditions imply safety across allowed compositions. It also verifies a learned mode predictor. This is not just a suggestive title: the construction and sufficient conditions appear in the introduction and formal development. [Authors' full paper, especially pp. 2–3](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~alur/Emsoft21.pdf); [publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3477023).

**My judgement:** This is serious missed prior art because it changes a table entry from absence of results to conditional results, exactly the distinction the report now correctly makes for control and liveness. It does not establish compositional alignment of arbitrary LLM organisations. The new row combines that much harder open problem with the much broader phrase “learned components,” thereby denying established work. Learned origin does not remove the applicability of a behavioural contract once the contract is verified.

**Exact fix:** “Conditional composition results exist for learned controllers with verified mode contracts and switching conditions. No result reviewed here establishes general safety or alignment for open-ended, heterogeneous, self-modifying LLM-agent organisations. Sybil resistance is a separate identity problem.” Add Ivanov et al. to the formal-methods/control comparison and explain its assumptions. Do not promote this counterexample into evidence for a billion-agent deployment.

## 3. HIGH — The new “demonstrated around 10^9” list bundles different measurements and different authority models

**Claim attacked:** “Demonstrated for populations around 10^9, each for a restricted function: closure with hierarchical naming; capability-style authority evaluated by runtime monitors over decidable policy languages, in one case with a verified engine; safety invariants under a single operator; conserved balances; and federated routing.”

**Verified source mismatch:** AWS's reported billion is authorization-engine invocations per second, not a population of distinct principals. Its verified property concerns implementation of authorization semantics; it does not demonstrate object-capability confinement, billion-principal governance, or all system safety invariants. Its policy interface explicitly uses principal/action/resource/condition statements. [Chakarov et al., abstract and §II](https://mhicks.me/papers/verified-cloud-authz.pdf).

Zanzibar really does support services used by billions of people, but it is an access-control-list system: its primary publication separately reports trillions of ACLs and millions of requests per second. It does not claim the AWS engine's implementation-verification result. [Pang et al., primary publication](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc19/presentation/pang).

**Could not verify:** A primary source establishing the conclusion's entire “capability-style authority … in one case with a verified engine” assertion for a billion distinct principals, or a population-denominated proof of “conserved balances,” was not supplied by the report or located in this check. This is lack of support, not proof that such deployments cannot exist.

**My judgement:** The list transfers strengths between examples: Zanzibar's user population, AWS's verified engine and throughput, the capability literature's authority guarantees, and accounting's algebraic identity. “Each for a restricted function” prevents interpreting this as one integrated system, but does not fix the units or proof-scope mismatch within individual entries. The body already warns against this exact inference; the rewritten conclusion reintroduces it. This is HIGH rather than CRITICAL because the defensible headline—restricted functions demonstrably operate at enormous scale—survives precise rewording.

**Exact fix:** “Large deployments establish several separate facts: Zanzibar supports services used by billions of people; AWS reports a formally verified authorization engine invoked about a billion times per second; Internet routing serves billions of users through far fewer routing domains. These measurements establish neither a common actor-count benchmark nor a combined governance guarantee. Authorization correctness, capability confinement and bookkeeping balance must be identified and evidenced separately.” Keep accounting and federation examples, but state their own denominators and the property actually proved or measured.

## 4. MEDIUM — The government/nation breakpoint is an imposed ontology, not a finding of the formal literature

**Claim attacked:** “At the level of government, authority stops being something delegated downward and attenuated and becomes something created by collective recognition.” Nation row: “Sovereignty: a root with no superior enforcer.” Related paragraph: “What is being governed changes kind twice.”

**Verified counterevidence:** The newly added King et al. paper explicitly models national legislation as governed by higher-level directives, human-rights instruments and supranational agreements, including sanctions and annulment. The report cannot cite that work as cross-level governance evidence and then classify the nation level categorically as lacking a superior enforcement relationship. [King et al., abstract and introduction](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/130977/1/AS4805164377456641491575454833_content_1.pdf).

Nor is it generally true that government authority “stops” being delegated. Federalist 51 describes power conferred by the people and divided between governments and departments. That is a primary articulation of government as delegated and constitutionally constrained authority, not authority originating only at the government level. [Federalist 51](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed51.asp).

**My judgement:** Collective recognition, constitutive rules, delegation and enforcement roots are different relations. They can coexist at organisational, governmental and transnational scales. “No universal sovereign above states” is different from “no superior enforcer” in any domain. The ten named categories are not a necessary linear containment hierarchy, and no evidence establishes exactly two changes of kind. The report's earlier graph/overlay discussion is more defensible.

**Exact fix:** “Constitutive recognition, delegated powers and enforcement relationships occur at several levels. Governments and states can be subject to constitutional and supranational constraints while remaining roots of authority in other domains. A uniform foundation must represent these distinct relations explicitly rather than assume a single resource-to-nation tree.” Change the nation-row gap to: “Domain-dependent enforcement and recognition anchors; no universally accepted final authority.” Remove the claim of exactly two categorical transitions.

## 5. MEDIUM — “Or unboundedly many actors” still loses the quantifier that makes the impossibility correct

**Claim attacked:** Opening: “Rice and Apt-Kozen rule out a complete automatic verifier for arbitrary or unboundedly many actors.” Conclusion: “complete automatic verification of arbitrary or unboundedly many actors” appears in the “shown impossible” list.

**Verified wrong under the literal disjunctive reading:** Unbounded population alone does not preclude complete automatic verification. Guarded-protocol cutoff results reduce model checking for arbitrarily many processes to a fixed finite size for specified model and specification classes. Jacobs and Sakr explicitly develop such results; the related fairness work proves decidability for its class. [Jacobs and Sakr](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01369); [Außerlechner, Jacobs and Khalimov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.03273).

**My judgement:** The intended unrestricted reading is correct, and the body already discusses cutoffs. That is why this is MEDIUM, not a newly discovered fatal theorem error. The headline's “or” nevertheless makes unbounded cardinality sound like an independent impossibility hypothesis. It also distracts from the user's bounded target of approximately 10^10 actors, which need not require a theorem for all finite populations.

**Exact fix, in both places:** “There is no sound, complete, terminating decision procedure for nontrivial semantic properties of arbitrary programs, or for the general parameterised verification problem. Restricted unbounded families can admit complete automatic verification, for example through cutoff theorems.” Preserve the existing qualification that bounded demonstrations must declare their workload and fault assumptions.

## 6. MEDIUM — The unchecked Radner citation does not establish a general logarithmic hierarchy law

**Claim attacked:** “Hierarchical trees are the efficient networks for aggregating information and delay grows logarithmically ([Radner])†.”

**Verified overstatement:** Radner studies a specified information-processing task, a processor/delay efficiency criterion, and one-shot versus repeated regimes. His one-shot theorem says a tree can attain the optimal delay for given numbers of items and processors; the surrounding discussion explicitly includes inefficient hierarchical networks. The claim is sufficiency of a suitable tree in the model, not that being a hierarchy characterises efficiency. The model's delay also depends on processor count: with one processor, adding N items takes N cycles, so the unqualified logarithmic claim is false. [Radner's primary paper, §3 and Theorem 1](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~rradner/publishedpapers/83OrganizationDecentralizedInfo.pdf); [alternate primary-paper copy](https://www.phy.pmf.unizg.hr/~bp/Radner.pdf).

Verification depth: the theorem and worked processor/delay table were available in indexed primary-paper extracts; page retrieval was intermittent. I am not claiming a fresh proof check of the full 39-page paper.

**Exact fix:** “In Radner's specified aggregation model, an appropriately chosen tree can attain the efficient processor–delay frontier. Logarithmic delay is attainable with sufficient parallel processing capacity; fixed processor budgets also impose workload-dependent delay. This is not a general optimality theorem for organisational hierarchies.”

## Revision fidelity, surviving checks and unresolved verification

The earlier reviews explain two risks that materialised here. Fable round 2 explicitly proposed the two-list conclusion, including the compact billion-scale bundle; the editor adopted the suggestion without resolving its denominators and guarantees. The level-by-level table was a reasonable response to the reviewers' request, but introduced the new categorical human/AI/government claims rather than testing a candidate construction. These are substantive additions, not merely corrections of wording.

Many other fixes are faithful. The report now distinguishes closure from congruence in its opening, restores conditional compositional control results, stops equating universality with absence of guarantees, includes transition-system baselines and institutional compliance, and treats the nine items as a checklist. I do not re-count those resolved objections. The added King et al., Minsky and proof-carrying-data sources are real and support their basic inclusion: [King et al.](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/130977/1/AS4805164377456641491575454833_content_1.pdf), [Minsky](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.4321), [Chiesa and Tromer](https://projects.csail.mit.edu/pcd/). The last source supports message-history compliance in its cryptographic model, not unmediated human conformance.

A dagger spot-check that survived: the Fonseca et al. study really reports 16 bugs associated with violated assumptions and verification boundaries; its findings support the report's warning about specifications and unverified integration. [Authors' paper](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/pfonseca/papers/eurosys2017-dsbugs.pdf). The verified AWS engine and its billion-per-second figure are also genuine; finding 3 concerns what follows from them, not their existence.

**Could not verify, and not counted as errors:** an exhaustive absence of an integrated governance calculus; all 2026 deployment ceilings; all dagger bibliographic details; and the entire claimed absence of liability propagation semantics. No finite targeted search proves those negatives. The report already acknowledges its limited search, so those limitations do not merit HIGH or CRITICAL findings. Douceur's primary paper supports a model-dependent remote-identity impossibility, but its exceptional resource-parity and coordination assumptions should remain explicit; it should not be turned into a universal theorem about every conceivable social identity system. [Douceur](https://nymity.ch/sybilhunting/pdf/Douceur2002a.pdf).

## Verdict on the opening and conclusion

- **“Small rigorous compositional substrates exist”: established.** The strongest objection is that “smaller” still has no common metric once encodings, semantic commitments and trusted machinery are charged. That prevents ranking a winner, not establishing existence of compact mathematical cores.
- **“Closure is available”: established, substantially definitional for syntactic composition.** It does not prove that a real nation and a resource have the same adequate interface semantics. The new sentence “The interface and containment structure is the same at every level, which is what closure provides” should therefore be restricted to models constructed inside the chosen formalism.
- **“Guarantees are conditional and property-specific”: established but nearly trivial without named properties and hypotheses.** The body supplies useful examples; findings 1–2 show why its absence claims must be equally conditional.
- **“No reviewed work delivers the complete combination”: defensible as a report of the search, not an established global nonexistence theorem.** The strongest counterargument is that one can encode all these actors in an open transition-system logic and impose enforceable contracts. The report has not specified an adequacy test that determines whether such an encoding qualifies as the requested foundation. This leaves the existence question underdetermined rather than resolved negatively.
- **“Shown impossible”: substantially correct only for the exact theorem hypotheses.** None of the cited impossibilities rules out the user's original request, which did not demand complete automatic verification, an infallible self-proof, costless identity or simultaneous satisfaction of every social-choice axiom. Finding 5 fixes the remaining misleading disjunction.
- **“Demonstrated around 10^9”: partly supported, overstated as written.** Large operational scale is real; the conclusion must keep populations, request rates and proof scopes separate.
- **“Formal coverage becomes thin at the human level”: plausible survey judgement, not a theorem.** Its current explanation is wrong: restricted human-interaction guarantees and compositional learned-controller guarantees already exist. Constitutional recognition also does not begin at one universal scale boundary.
- **“Four shapes at scale” and “nine-element checklist”: reasonable non-exhaustive synthesis.** Neither establishes a minimal foundation, and the report now says so explicitly.

**Does it answer the question?** Partly and honestly at its narrowest: it gives a broad map of rigorous ingredients and does not find a demonstrated integration at the requested scale. It still does not compare the strongest complete candidates using a shared construction, definition of “controlled,” or accounting of foundational size; it openly admits that omission, so I do not inflate it into a separate severe finding. The strongest next test remains a common worked construction, including a human-mediated action and a learned controller, so the comparison cannot win by simply declaring those domains outside formal guarantees.

**Final verdict:** No CRITICAL finding is justified: the carefully qualified central existence conclusion survives. Three significant supporting claims need correction, and three narrower claims need their assumptions restored. The most damaging regression is the invention of hard human/learned-agent boundaries in a report whose strongest valid lesson is that guarantees depend on models, interfaces and enforcement conditions.
